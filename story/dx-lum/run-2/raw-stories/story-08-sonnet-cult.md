# The Threshold Communion

Run 2 | Writer: sonnet | Emphasis: faith / ideology / a reactor cult

## Logline

When Liam Ryker traces a faint multiversal anomaly to a dead reactor in Quinmore's slums, he finds
a grieving city has already made sense of the disaster the only way it knows how: it prayed. The
Threshold Communion, a slum faith that venerates the reactor as a living god and its unexplained
"tears" as grace, turns out to be built on real multiversal knowledge, mythologized by three decades
of retelling — and both Nuradyne and the FMA have reasons to make sure nobody ever finds that out.

## How this version reads

Most of Quinmore's factions treat the reactor blast as a security problem, a PR problem, or a body
count. The Communion treats it as the night their god screamed. That's the register this story runs
in: everyone Ryker meets in the slums already has a working theology for the disaster before he
arrives with his flashlight and his forged badge, and the investigation keeps forcing him to decide
whether the truth he's uncovering is something he's allowed to hand back to the people who built a
religion around a garbled version of it. The tone stays close to Deus Ex's: dry, procedural, low
affect, with the horror delivered through documents, overheard conversations, and quiet confessions
rather than cutscenes.

## What I take as fixed, what I invented

Fixed: Ryker, the FMA, U-7134/Quinmore, the Corporate Union, Nuradyne/Atlas, the siphon-reactor
secret, the operative's incursion and the slum-reactor explosion, the cordon and checkpoint
screening, the relocation warehouse, the stranded FMA liaison who sold Nuradyne the tech, Director
Harmon, Kira Voight, the slum mother and her missing son, the erased-logs/datacube/Harmon lead
structure, and the Aire Gardens → elevator → subway level idea from `Ideas.md`.

Invented: the Threshold Communion (a slum religion built around the reactor and a folk-messiah
figure), its living founder Aldous Pryce (the liaison himself, still alive, worshipped without his
consent), Prelate Iris Vane (the Communion's current leader), Marcus Diehl (Nuradyne's "Director of
Community Stability," who has spent years quietly funding the faith), the "bleed-through" visions
(a real perceptual side-effect of proximity to reactor byproduct that the Communion mistakes for
religious ecstasy), the FMA's Cultural Sterilization Protocol, the operative Ashna Rell's own home
faith (a mirror religion in her dying universe), and the radical Communion splinter, the Unsealed.
The slum mother is named Denise Okafor; her son is Miles.

## Factions & power structure

**The Corporate Union (CU) board.** Nominal government of Quinmore; a rotating council of corporate
seats. It approved Nuradyne's reactors as "cutting-edge energy tech" and has no idea what they
actually do. It wants the slum-district incident closed fast, blame assigned, and the news cycle
moved on.

**Nuradyne.** Controls power generation and, by extension, every other corp's leverage on the board.
Runs the reactor program (Director Corwin Harmon), reactor security (Kira Voight), and — the thing
nobody in Community Relations puts in a memo out loud — a decades-long, deliberate policy of letting
a slum religion grow around its own infrastructure because a believing population doesn't riot when
the lights flicker. Its interest in the Threshold Communion is not spiritual. It's actuarial.

**Atlas Subsystems.** Builds and maintains dome shielding. Publicly blames Nuradyne for the blast;
privately suspects a Nuradyne power play. Atlas has no idea about the siphon and no idea about the
Communion's Nuradyne funding — but either fact, in Atlas's hands, is a weapon against Nuradyne's
board seat.

**The Threshold Communion.** A syncretic slum faith, roughly thirty years old, built around a man who
appeared out of nowhere at the war's end raving about "worlds beyond counting" and "an Authority that
judges the crossings between them," and around the reactor itself, which the Communion has come to
treat as a living, suffering god that "breathes in the Unmade to keep our air sweet." Structurally
loose — congregations ("hearths"), lay wardens, a central leadership (the Prelate) headquartered at
the Cathedral of the First Light, a converted pre-war municipal building on the Communion's edge of
the slums. Doctrinally split between a mainstream that treats the reactor's suffering as a burden to
be honored with charity and vigilance, and a radical fringe — the Unsealed — who believe the true
mercy is to let the domes fail and cross over.

**The FMA.** Ryker's employer. Publicly framed to Ryker (and to the player) as here to investigate an
anomaly and assess risk. What the mission briefing does not say, because the people who wrote it
don't expect to need it, is that the FMA has a standing doctrine for exactly this situation: a
primitive civilization that has independently reconstructed accurate multiversal cosmology, however
garbled, is a Class-1 doctrinal leak, and doctrinal leaks get sterilized, not studied.

**The drained universes.** Plural, this time — the Communion's own "sacred numbers" (recited in
services, meaningless to the flock) turn out to be a partial list of siphon targets, more than one.
Ashna Rell's home universe is the one that struck back. Its own culture, in its last years, grew a
mirror faith — the Vigil of the Last Light — around its own dying sun-analog, giving Ashna a faith of
her own to lose when she crossed over.

## Key characters

**Liam Ryker.** As established: blank-slate, low-affect, ex-military, morally flexible, no cover, no
contacts, learning Quinmore as the player does.

**Reya Solon.** Ryker's FMA field handler, staying at the outpost. Competent, dry, genuinely fond of
Ryker in the understated way career operators are fond of the only person they trust in a warzone.
Her arc is the FMA's arc in miniature: she believes in the mission until the mission asks her to burn
a church with people still inside it, and then she has to decide whether doctrine is worth more than
her own hands staying clean. She isn't written as a villain in reserve — every infolink she sends
before M14 is exactly the kind of steady, useful support the player would expect from a good handler,
which is what makes the M14 order land as betrayal rather than a twist she was obviously building
toward.

**Director Corwin Harmon.** Head of Nuradyne's reactor project. Suppressed the earlier abnormal-
readings reports; believed his fixes had solved the problem. Not a mustache-twirler — a man who
convinced himself that "acceptable losses" was a real category and has been living inside that
conviction long enough that it no longer feels like a lie.

**Kira Voight.** Nuradyne Chief of Security. Has been hunting the operative since hour one and is
close to done being polite about it. Doesn't know about the Communion's Nuradyne funding — that's
Diehl's budget line, not hers — and reacts to learning it about the way you'd expect a security chief
to react to discovering PR has been running an unauthorized asset in her own city for a decade.

**Marcus Diehl.** Nuradyne's Director of Community Stability. Architect of the quiet funding program
that keeps the Communion fed, housed, and un-radicalized. Genuinely believes he's doing humane work —
chapel leases, relic "authentication" grants, holiday food drives — and genuinely doesn't examine too
closely why his division has an off-ledger line item for it.

**Denise Okafor.** The slum mother from the district entrance. A lay warden in her local hearth,
devout without being credulous — she knows some of what she believes is metaphor and doesn't much
care, because it's the metaphor that got her through the war's aftermath and gets her through this
one. Her son Miles is missing; finding him is Ryker's first real errand into the slums and hers into
the plot.

**Aldous Pryce.** The stranded FMA liaison — canon's character, given a name. Thirty years past the
man he was. Cut loose by Nuradyne once his knowledge was drained; spent the intervening years
half-mad, half-hidden, and — without ever meaning to — half-worshipped. He is alive, he is dying, and
he has no idea how large the thing he accidentally started has grown.

**Prelate Iris Vane.** Current head of the Communion. Inherited the Cathedral from the founding
generation. Smart enough to have noticed the funding trail from Nuradyne years ago and made her own
cold peace with it — a faith needs a roof. Not smart enough, until Ryker arrives, to have connected
what her scripture actually describes.

**Ashna Rell.** The operative. Not a saboteur out for revenge — a soldier-priest sent to disable the
siphon's control matrix and, if possible, make contact and warn Quinmore what its power grid is
doing. The strike went wrong when Nuradyne security intercepted her at the aperture; people died who
didn't need to, on both sides. She has been hiding in plain sight inside a Threshold hearth, where her
force-field armor's residual shimmer reads to the faithful as a mark of grace.

## Full plot, mission by mission

**M0 — FMA Headquarters.** As established: recall from leave, briefing on the U-7134 anomaly,
medical, armory, optional orientation. Solon is assigned as handler. The briefing frames this as
routine anomaly assessment — faint, erratic, probably nothing, standard covert-embed protocol given
the uncertainty. Nobody in the room mentions doctrine on cultural leaks, because nobody expects to
need it. That's the setup for the twist landing later: the audience should be able to recall, at the
end, that the omission was never a lie — just a briefing that assumed a smaller problem.

**M1 — Outpost, Northgate, the train downtown.** Portal to the cloaked outpost; Solon triangulates
the residual energy to the slum-district reactor. Cloaked flight to Northgate, train downtown. Ryker
is jumped by opportunist thugs in Northgate (optional fight). On the ride downtown, the first texture
of the Communion appears without explanation: chalked doorframes with a stylized open-door sigil,
a storefront hearth with its windows papered over, a rail-car ad defaced to read UNSEAL YOUR EYES
over a corporate wellness slogan. None of it means anything yet. It's scenery. Later it's evidence.

**M2 — The district entrance.** As established: Denise Okafor arguing with a Nuradyne officer about
Miles; the officer redirects to Ryker, Ryker's forged clearance gets him through, Denise stops him to
ask him to find her boy. New beat: she's wearing a small brass door-shaped pendant and, mid-panic,
recites something under her breath that sounds like a prayer — "the Undying keeps the doorways, it
keeps the doorways" — before catching herself and apologizing, embarrassed. First plant.

**M3 — Reactor site.** As established: erased logs, dead or incapacitated security, the vent datacube
naming Harmon and "abnormal reactor readings." New: a side room shows signs of a second, informal
search that predates Ryker's — a torn Nuradyne badge, a canvas sack, and a hand-lettered tag reading
COLLECTED FOR THE HEARTH, dropped by scavengers who came for the reactor's melted slag before the
cordon tightened. It's the first thread tying the Communion to physical reactor material, and Ryker
has no reason yet to think it matters.

**M4 — Nuradyne HQ, finding Harmon.** As established: Harmon stonewalls, dismisses the datacube,
answers everything except the actual question. New: in the outer office, Ryker overhears (or reads,
on an idle terminal, if he lingers) a scheduling note — Harmon's assistant confirming a "Community
Stability quarterly" meeting with a Director Diehl. Meaningless in isolation. A second plant.

**M5 — The missing kid.** As established: Ryker finds Miles in an unlogged holding cell during the
main investigation. Miles overheard security talking, doesn't understand it, Ryker does: something
like "keep the flock fed" and "the Stranger's price is paid." Solon flags it over infolink as
probably slang, worth noting, not yet worth chasing. Ryker returns Miles toward Denise — the reunion
plays out over the following missions rather than immediately, since the district is still sealed and
evacuee tracking is a mess, which is its own quiet indictment of how Nuradyne runs a "crime scene."

**M6 — Aire Gardens and the warehouse-chapel.** Ryker's Harmon lead and Solon's own digging both point
toward the relocation effort — Nuradyne's evacuee processing runs out of Aire Gardens' lower levels.
Chopper into the landing area, the multiport concourse, the elevator down to the subway level (per
`Ideas.md`). At the bottom, adjoining the relocation warehouse where evacuees are still packed in
cots, Ryker finds the warehouse doubles after hours as a hearth — folding chairs stacked against cots,
a door-sigil banner over a service table. Denise is here, running both the food line and, tonight, the
liturgy. She has Miles back at her side by now (the reunion lands quietly here, in the middle of
displacement, not as a triumphant set-piece — nobody in this district gets a clean win). She'll help
Ryker with anything reactor-related in exchange for a favor: a rival scavenger crew lifted the
hearth's central relic, a data-slate they call the Ember Scripture, and she wants it back.

**M7 — The Ember Scripture.** Ryker tracks the scavenger crew (stealth, combat, or a straight buy-back
if he's built up currency/reputation — player's choice of approach). Recovering it and reading it
(read-computers verb) gives the first real payoff: it's not a slate at all, but an old audio
transcription, decades of hand-copying turning half-understood phrases into liturgy. "Worlds
numberless as sand." "The Authority who judges the crossings." "The Undying Machine that drinks the
Unmade's blood to keep our air sweet." Ryker's read on it, and the player's, should land before
Solon's does: this isn't metaphor, this is somebody's badly-remembered briefing. Infolink from Solon
follows once she's had the transcript for an hour — she's rattled. It reads like corrupted FMA
terminology. New objective: find out where the Communion's founding revelation actually came from.

**M8 — The Cathedral of the First Light.** Ryker meets Prelate Iris Vane, careful and political,
willing to talk in exchange for Ryker's discretion. She confirms the founding story: a man appeared
at the war's tail end, half-mad, near the old federal complex, talking about worlds and an Authority
and a door he'd walked through that wouldn't open again. The proto-Communion took him in. He preached,
on and off, for years, then vanished from any formal record about fifteen years ago — which Ryker can
independently date, reading an old CU relief-intake ledger in the Cathedral archive, to almost exactly
when Nuradyne's engineers stopped needing their liaison and cut him loose.

**M9 — The funding trail.** A second pass through Nuradyne HQ (following the Harmon-to-Diehl
scheduling thread from M4) puts Ryker in reach of Community Relations' systems. Reading a budget
memo — a normal act of reading a computer, per the player-verb rules — turns up a confidential,
off-ledger line item: "Threshold Communion — Stability Program," years of chapel leases, relic
"authentication" grants, holiday relief, routed to keep the faith fed, housed, and calm rather than
hungry, homeless, and asking questions. Finding it triggers the canon infolink mechanic: Solon comes
in hot over the channel, alarmed for a different reason than Ryker expects — not that Nuradyne is
running a slum charity, but that Nuradyne has apparently known for years that this "charity" is
sitting on real leaked cosmology and has done nothing but keep it fed.

**M10 — Marcus Diehl.** Ryker corners Diehl — office confrontation, or off-hours at a chapel dedication
he's quietly attending, player's choice of approach and tone. Diehl doesn't think of himself as
sinister. He'll explain, without much prompting, that a believing population doesn't riot when a
reactor "acts up," that early containment attempts against the original prophet backfired badly — a
suppressed incident thirty years back that the Communion now venerates as "the Silencing," a martyrdom
that only made the faith stronger — and that letting the thing grow, tended and funded, turned out to
be cheaper and safer than fighting it. He genuinely doesn't know Pryce is still alive; Nuradyne's
official position, even internally, has been that the liaison died or disappeared. Diehl built an
entire humane-sounding management program around a myth he assumed had no living author.

**M11 — Aldous Pryce.** Following Vane's private correspondence, or a chance remark from Denise about
"old Pryce, the beggar by the cordon, mutters just like the old hymns," Ryker finds the man himself:
alive, in a maintenance nook near the ruined reactor, filthy, terrified of being found by "them" — he
means Nuradyne, not the FMA, since as far as he knows the FMA gave up on him decades ago. Converse
with him and the whole thing collapses into one plain, exhausted confession: yes, he's real, yes,
he's the Stranger, no, he never meant to start a religion, he was just talking to stay sane and people
listened and then people started listening on purpose. He's sick, likely dying of something the
domes' filtered air was supposed to prevent, and he tells Ryker, almost as an afterthought, that he
can feel "something coming through again" — the same wrongness in the air he felt right before the
blast. He has no idea what that feeling is. Ryker, by now, does.

**M12 — Bleed-through.** Parallel to M11, reports surface — from hearth members, from Denise, from
services Ryker can sit in on — of shared visions during Communion rites involving the reactor's
"tears," fragments of Nuradyne slag the hearths collect as holy relics: flashes of a dying sky, a red
sun, screaming that isn't wind. Solon forwards a classified FMA phenomenology bulletin once Ryker
reports the pattern: this matches "bleed-through," a documented, rare perceptual effect of sustained
proximity to a stressed transversal aperture or its exotic residue — untrained minds perceiving raw
fragments of a linked universe and having no framework for it except the one they've built. The
Communion's holiest moments are, without exception, moments of a dying universe's suffering, felt
directly and mistaken for grace. This is the horror underneath the faith, and it's the game's clearest
statement of what the siphon actually costs, made visible to Ryker (and the player) rather than
explained in a briefing room. It also recontextualizes everything Ryker has already seen in the
hearths: the pendant Denise touches under pressure, the door-sigils chalked on Northgate frames, the
Feast liturgy itself — none of it is invented comfort. Every ritual gesture in the Communion turns
out to be downstream of somebody, somewhere, actually dying, and the congregation has no way to know
that, because the one person who could tell them is hiding from the corporation that made sure of it.

**M13 — Ashna Rell.** The operative surfaces inside Denise's own hearth, where she's been sheltering
as a recent convert — her armor's residual shimmer reads to the faithful as a living mark of the
Threshold Light, and nobody's asked questions of a quiet woman who helps with the food line. Combat,
stealth, or converse are all live approaches; converse becomes available once Ryker has enough of the
Communion's real history and the bleed-through evidence in hand to convince her he's not just another
hunter. If he gets her talking, she tells him what the operative subplot has withheld until now: her
strike on the reactor was meant to be surgical — disable the control matrix, make contact, warn this
world what its power grid is doing to hers — and it went wrong when Nuradyne security intercepted her
at the aperture. People died on both sides who didn't need to. Her own dying universe grew its own
mirror faith around its failing sun-analog, the Vigil of the Last Light, and she recognizes in the
Threshold Communion's grief-management, its relic-worship, its martyrs, something uncomfortably close
to home. She isn't the only one whose world learned to pray at what was killing it — which is why she
never once considered attacking the hearth that sheltered her, and why, if Ryker plays this badly, she
is fully willing to die inside it rather than be dragged out in front of people who took her in
without asking anything of her first.

**M14 — Sterilization.** With the Communion's origin, its funding, and the bleed-through phenomenon
all confirmed and reported, a priority infolink comes through from FMA command, relayed and visibly
unhappy through Solon: Class-1 doctrinal leak, standing protocol, Cultural Sterilization. Not a
metaphor — the order is to erase the Communion's texts and relics and to remove its living
carriers: Vane, the Cathedral's elders who know the founding story firsthand, and Aldous Pryce, the
one person who can personally confirm any of it. A cleanup team is being prepped at the outpost.
Solon is ordered to have Ryker handle it quietly, under deniable cover, before the team arrives —
or, she tells him privately, off the record, he could stall her long enough to file a dissent that
will probably end her career and might not even work. Simultaneously, Kira Voight — who has finally
traced Ashna Rell to the Cathedral's hearth network — is moving her own security detail in, prepared
to burn the building if that's what it takes to close the file on the bomber. Marcus Diehl, informed
that his "Stability Program" has attracted an actual government's attention, is scrambling to shut it
down and erase his own paper trail before anyone above him asks why. Three separate institutions, for
three separate reasons, are converging on the same building full of people who just wanted somewhere
to grieve.

**M15 — The Vigil.** The climax plays out at the Cathedral of the First Light during a major service —
the Threshold Feast — packed with hearth families, Denise and Miles among them, Vane presiding, Ashna
Rell in the crowd, and, if Ryker has chosen to bring him, Aldous Pryce, given one look at the
congregation he never meant to build before whatever comes next. Voight's team is closing from the
east entrance. An FMA extraction/cleanup element is inbound if Ryker hasn't stopped or redirected it.
And a hard core of the Unsealed — the radical wing that believes the true mercy is to let the domes
fail — has decided tonight is the night to force the issue: sabotage on the district's rebuilt dome
shielding, timed to the Feast, meant to turn the whole congregation's death into the mass "crossing"
their doctrine promises. Every thread the story has built — the funding, the founder, the visions, the
operative, the FMA's real doctrine — has to be resolved here, live, in front of the people it's
actually about, using nothing but conversation, infolink, force, and the doors Ryker chooses to open
or bar.

Mechanically, the Vigil is built as a set of near-simultaneous decisions rather than one branch point.
Ryker can warn Denise's hearth and start a quiet evacuation before either security team arrives, or
let the service run and use the crowd as cover for his own approach. He can converse with Voight in
person — she'll listen to a direct appeal, if Ryker has the funding-trail evidence in hand, since
learning Diehl ran an off-book asset in her city insults her professionally more than it moves her
morally, and that's a lever. He can answer Solon's infolink honestly, stall her with a partial report,
or go dark and act alone, each of which changes whether the FMA element breaches the Cathedral at all.
He can get Ashna Rell out through the same subway access from M6, hand her to Voight, or leave her to
whatever the crowd decides once — if — Ryker lets slip what she is. He can pull Pryce out before the
Unsealed make their move, or leave him to say, for the first and only time, a few honest words to the
congregation that built a religion out of his worst year. None of these are separately flagged as
"the twist decision" in the moment; they accumulate into whichever ending fits the pattern of choices
Ryker's made since M9.

## The central twists, stated plainly

**Twist one (new to this project): the myth is real, and someone built it on purpose.** The
Threshold Communion isn't a coping mechanism that happens to echo the truth — it's the actual, still-
living, still-funded record of what the FMA liaison told his rescuers thirty years ago, laundered
through a generation of oral retelling into scripture, and then deliberately kept alive by Nuradyne's
own PR division because a population that worships its power grid doesn't ask who's dying to keep it
lit. The founder isn't dead or legendary — he's an old man in a maintenance closet a ten-minute walk
from his own cathedral. And the faith's holiest experience, the shared visions the hearths treat as
grace, is a real, documented, horrifying perceptual bleed from a universe being drained to death. Every
layer peels back to something worse, not something fictional.

**Twist two (exploits the "FMA has a hidden agenda" hook): the Authority doesn't care that people are
dying to power the reactors. It cares that they found out.** Ryker's mission was framed as
anomaly assessment. It becomes, without anyone lying to him outright, a containment order against a
religion — its books, its relics, and the people who remember its origin firsthand. The FMA's doctrine
was never designed around cruelty; it was designed around secrecy, and secrecy doesn't distinguish
between a hostile leak and a grieving city that accidentally got the truth right. Nobody at FMA HQ
budgeted for that distinction mattering. Ryker has to decide whether it does.

## Player-choice structure & endings

**The Silence Kept (FMA doctrine ending).** Ryker executes or permits the Sterilization Protocol:
the Cathedral's archive is purged, Vane and the elders are removed under deniable cover, Pryce is
handed off (or worse) before the congregation ever learns who he was. Multiversal secrecy holds.
Ryker is filed as reliable. Within a generation the Communion is a folk memory with no founder and no
scripture, and the reactors keep siphoning, unremarked. Cold, procedural, consistent with a Ryker who
treats this as "just business." Solon's arc closes hardest here: she carries out her half of the
order competently and never quite forgives Ryker, or herself, for how easy it turned out to be. Her
last infolink of the game is a debrief line, not a goodbye — the closest the ending gets to telling
the player what it cost her.

**The Undying Machine (Nuradyne ending).** Ryker sides, actively or by omission, with Harmon and
Voight: neutralizes Ashna Rell quietly, feeds Solon a downgraded report that reframes the "leak" as a
false positive, and leaves the Communion standing as exactly what Diehl built it to be — a managed,
funded, permanently grateful population. The reactors keep running, the domes stay lit, and the
Communion gets a new holy day out of tonight, with Ryker, unknowingly, mythologized into it. The
horror of this ending is specifically that mercy toward the slum — letting them keep the comfort — is
what keeps the exploitation running underneath it. Diehl keeps his budget line and gets a bigger one
next year; he never has to reckon with what his "humane management" actually manages. Voight closes
her file clean. Harmon sleeps fine. The only two people who ever really understand what happened here
are Ryker and Solon, and the ending gives them nothing to do with that knowledge except carry it.

**The Open Doorway (exposure ending).** Ryker leaks the Ember Scripture's real translation and
Nuradyne's funding trail — to Atlas, to a CU board rival, to whoever will publish it — while helping
Ashna Rell get out alive and telling Vane and the elders the literal truth instead of letting either
Nuradyne or the FMA erase it. The Communion doesn't survive contact with the facts unchanged: some
of the flock walks away, some of it hardens into something new, a "Threshold Compact" that starts
demanding real accountability from the corporations for the first time in the district's history.
Costly for Ryker — he's likely burned with the FMA, Nuradyne, or both — but it's the ending that
treats the people who prayed at the truth by accident as owed the truth on purpose. It's also the only
ending where Ashna Rell's own request gets honored in full: not just her survival, but Ryker actually
carrying her warning about the siphon somewhere it can do damage to Nuradyne, rather than filing it
and moving on.

**The Unsealing (radical/nihilist ending).** If Ryker's choices leave the Unsealed unchecked, or if —
in the darkest branch — he's the one who hands them proof of what the bleed-through visions really
are and lets that proof do the radicalizing for him, the Feast ends in deliberate dome sabotage: a
mass, willing "crossing." Ryker can make a last stand against people who are, in every sense that
matters, victims of the same machine he's investigating — Denise and Miles among the crowd he'd be
fighting through, not against — or he can step back and let it happen, on the reasoning that a
population being quietly, incrementally poisoned by a system with no intention of ever telling them
the truth has already been sentenced, and the Unsealed are only asking to serve their own sentence on
their own terms. Neither choice resolves clean. It's the one place the story refuses to let "leaning
good, morally flexible" cash out as a comfortable answer, and it's the ending most likely to leave the
player unsure whether Ryker did the right thing at all.

## Themes

Faith as the only infrastructure the powerless get to build for themselves — and the first
infrastructure the powerful learn to lease. A city that turned its own slow poisoning into liturgy,
because liturgy was the one thing nobody could repossess. The obscenity of a corporation discovering
that grief is cheaper to manage than to fix, and doing the arithmetic without ever calling it that out
loud. A congregation whose most sacred moments are, unbeknownst to them, somebody else's universe
screaming as it dies — belief and atrocity sharing the same physical event and differing only in who's
allowed to know which one they're witnessing. And a multiverse-scale policing body whose actual job,
it turns out, isn't protecting the innocent from the machine — it's making sure nobody innocent ever
finds out the machine is there, doctrine that never had to name its own cruelty because it was written
by people who never expected to meet the people it would fall on.
