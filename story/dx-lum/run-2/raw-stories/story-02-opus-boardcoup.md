# DX_LUM — "The Continuity Mandate"

Run 2 | Writer: opus | Emphasis: CU board politics / coup

**Logline:** A merc sent to investigate a rogue reactor walks into a boardroom coup — and discovers that the "terrorist attack" everyone is fighting over was never the point. The office built to answer terror wants the whole board to burn, and Ryker is the match nobody can trace.

---

## How this version reads

A cold procedural that widens like a lens. It opens as a single-thread infiltration — one dead reactor, one missing kid — and keeps pulling back until the frame is the entire Corporate Union eating itself. The genre engine isn't "who bombed the reactor" (Ryker learns that early and it doesn't matter). It's *who profits from the story that it was a bomb.* Every faction hands Ryker a version of the truth calibrated to move him one square on a board he can't yet see. The horror underneath — that the domes run on serial universe-murder — is the one thing none of the schemers even know about, which is the point: they are fighting to the death over the wheelhouse of a machine, blind to what the machine actually does.

Voice: clipped, watchful, noir. Ryker doesn't editorialize. The reader assembles the conspiracy from documents, overheard rooms, and one-way infolink chatter, the same way the player does.

---

## Fixed vs. invented

**Taken as fixed (canon):** Ryker (blank-slate low-affect ex-military merc, morally flexible); the FMA and its transversal-anomaly detection; U-7134 / Quinmore (renamed **Umbermont** per `CityName.md`); the poisoned atmosphere and dome sectors; the Corporate Union as a board of corporate reps with no civilian government, split by domain (Nuradyne=power, Atlas=shielding, others=transit/housing/comms); the multiversal siphon secret known only to Nuradyne's inner circle; the drained universe's retaliating operative; the blast that killed Atlas shielding and flooded the sector; the terrorist framing, cordon, checkpoints, relocation warehouse; the stranded FMA liaison who sold Nuradyne the tech; Director Harmon (suppressed abnormal readings); Kira Voight (Chief of Security); the FMA partner at the outpost; the missing slum kid who overheard something; the whole prologue → outpost → Northgate → downtown beat sheet; the Aire Gardens station/elevator/subway idea; player-verb limits.

**Invented:** the **Union Continuity Office (UCO)** and **Chief Marshal Ada Corvin**; the dormant **Continuity Mandate** statute; the board seats **Meridian Transit**, **Halcyon Residential**, **Cyrene Media**; Nuradyne CEO **Lenore Ashfield**; Atlas board strategist **Sela Anand**; the liaison's name **Merrick**; the operative **Kadu**; the kid **Tobin** and mother **Della**; slum broker **Wren** and syndicate boss **Halloran**; FMA partner named **Kesler**; the **serial-retarget** nature of the reactors; the FMA's **eraser** directive; the four endings. None of this contradicts canon; it fills the gaps.

---

## The world before Ryker lands

Umbermont is thirty years into corporate rule and the arrangement has calcified into something that looks like government but has no citizen in it. Six seats on the Union board. Five are corporations that sell the city something it can't breathe without: **Nuradyne** (power), **Atlas Subsystems** (dome shielding), **Meridian Transit** (rail and lift), **Halcyon Residential** (housing, relocation, the warehouses), **Cyrene Media** (comms and the only broadcast anyone sees). The sixth seat isn't a company. It's the **Union Continuity Office** — the shared enforcement arm the corps chartered to arbitrate their disputes and to keep any one of them from turning its private security on another. On paper the UCO is a referee with no product and no ambition. In practice it has spent thirty years being the one body in Umbermont that answers to no shareholder.

Nuradyne runs the grid, which means every other seat's infrastructure lives or dies at Nuradyne's breaker. That is the standing insult of the board. Atlas has wanted Nuradyne's monopoly broken for a decade and has never had a lever. Then a reactor in the slum sector goes up, the air goes lethal, and suddenly there is a lever lying in the street for anyone quick enough to grab it.

Ryker arrives the day the grab begins.

## ACT I — THE CORDON

### Mission 1 — Prologue (canon)

FMA headquarters: recall from leave, briefing, medical, armory, the optional orientation beats. The stated mission is narrow and reassuring — a faint, unprecedented transversal anomaly out of U-7134, probably a small rogue operator, go look and report. Ryker portals to the cloaked outpost in the dead zone. His partner, **Nadia Kesler**, an FMA analyst who will run overwatch from the bunker, triangulates residual multiversal energy to a reactor in Umbermont's slum district. Cloaked aircraft to **Northgate**; train downtown. In Northgate a knot of thugs tests him — the first read on the city: it's poor, it's armed, and nobody's coming to help. Kesler's voice arrives clean over infolink, warm and procedural: *"Signal's steady from downtown. Get eyes on that reactor. And Ryker — low profile. We have no cover here. We don't exist."*

*(Planted, quietly: the anomaly is called "unprecedented." The FMA has never logged a signature like it. Kesler is a little too interested in the signature and a little too incurious about the city.)*

### Mission 2 — The Cordon (canon)

The slum sector is sealed. Reactor blown, Atlas shielding dead, backups fried, the sector's air gone to poison behind checkpoints. At the entrance a woman — **Della** — is arguing with a Nuradyne officer about her son. The officer waves Ryker off; Ryker claims clearance (forged, hacked, or talked); the officer, harried, lets him through. Della catches his sleeve: *find my boy. Tobin. He was playing where he shouldn't have been.* She isn't asking a soldier. She's asking the first person who didn't say no.

Getting in past the screening — the checkpoints are running facial capture on every evacuee, which the public is told is the manhunt for the bomber. Ryker can slip it by forged Nuradyne credentials, a multitooled service door, or by paying **Wren**, a slum fixer who runs bodies through a gap in the fence for a fee and a favor owed.

### Mission 3 — The Reactor Site (canon)

Dead Nuradyne security, no visible cause of death on some of them. Logs erased or corrupted. In a vent near a dead scientist, the datacube:

> *"Security doubled on our floor since yesterday. Can't reach anyone outside the facility — they cut my comm access. Reactor readings are way outside normal operating parameters. Reported it to Director Harmon, told me to forget about it. Something's not right. If anyone finds this—"*

Two leads: **Harmon**, and **the abnormal readings**. Reading the scientist's terminal pulls one more thread the datacube didn't — a half-sent internal ticket, subject line *RE: retarget event?*, body blank, recipient Harmon, killed before it sent. Ryker doesn't know what "retarget" means yet. Kesler, watching over his shoulder through the infolink relay, goes quiet for three seconds too long before saying: *"Copy the ticket. Move to Harmon. Don't linger on the reactor — you've been on-site long enough."*

*(Planted: Kesler wants the tech data more than she wants Harmon, and she wants Ryker away from the reactor as if the reactor itself could tell him something.)*

### Mission 4 — Nuradyne HQ, first pass

Ryker infiltrates Nuradyne HQ downtown to reach Harmon. Harmon is exactly where a guilty man who can't afford to look guilty must be: at his desk, running the reactor project, business as usual. He stonewalls — rehearsed lines, dismisses the datacube as a nervous junior, offers "terrorism" like a coin he's paid with a hundred times today. What Harmon says: it was an attack, tragic, under investigation. What Harmon doesn't say: anything about the readings, and — a tell — anything at all about the Union Continuity Office, which by now is all over his building and which he clearly wishes were not.

Because the second thing Ryker learns at Nuradyne HQ is that Nuradyne doesn't own its own crisis. Reading office terminals and drifting through rooms (the conversation-by-entering verb):

- **Kira Voight**, Chief of Security, is running a manhunt that has nothing to do with a bomber. Her people are cross-referencing the checkpoint face-capture against *nothing* — no suspect photo, no name. They're looking for someone with no record because he isn't from here. Her internal memos to Ashfield are furious and frightened in equal measure: *keep it contained, keep it quiet, we lose everything if this gets loud.*
- And it is getting loud. **Cyrene Media** has been running wall-to-wall coverage — *TERROR IN THE DOMES, WHO IS NEXT* — that Nuradyne never authorized and can't switch off. Voight's memo: *"Who is feeding Cyrene? It isn't us. We want this small."*
- A board notice sits in the CEO's queue: **Atlas Subsystems has filed for a Continuity review of Nuradyne's grid stewardship.** Negligence. If it carries, Nuradyne's monopoly goes into trusteeship.

So the surface conspiracy inverts on Ryker in the first act. Nuradyne, the villain who invented "terrorism" to hide the siphon, is not driving the terror story. Nuradyne wants it *quiet.* Someone else wants it *loud.* Ryker files that and moves.

Kesler, on the infolink, gives him the next objective and — a small new note — a second, softer one: *"That half-sent ticket. 'Retarget.' If you get near their reactor engineering data, I want it. Don't ask why. Just pull it."*

## ACT II — THE BOARD

### Mission 5 — The Missing Kid

During the Nuradyne investigation Ryker finds **Tobin** in an unlogged holding cell in a Nuradyne annex — grabbed by security under standing orders to detain anyone found in the sealed sector, then forgotten. He's scared and unhurt and he's been listening through a vent for a day and a half, because that's what bored terrified kids do. He doesn't understand what he heard. Ryker does. In kid-words, three things:

1. *"There's a guy they're looking for. A guard said he walked out of the fire and didn't burn. He said it like it was scary."* — the operative, force-field-suited, the thing that came out of the reactor.
2. *"They kept saying the Marshal wants it big and the security lady wants it small, and they were mad about it."* — the split. UCO wants the crisis maximal; Nuradyne wants it minimal. First direct evidence that the terror narrative is being pushed *against* Nuradyne's wishes by the Continuity Office.
3. *"One of them said, 'the whole board's gonna eat each other and that's the whole idea.'"* — a guard repeating something he heard from higher up, not understanding it either. The idea. Somebody's idea.

Getting Tobin out — carry him past his own captors — is the first place the player can choose cost: loud (kill the annex guards, fast, clean, Della gets her son in an hour) or quiet (multitool the service corridor, no bodies, slow, Tobin's terrified the whole way). Reunion with Della happens at the relocation warehouse, which is where Ryker needs to go anyway.

### Mission 6 — Atlas Comes Calling

Ryker has been noticed. Whoever moves against Nuradyne wants the off-grid investigator poking Nuradyne's wounds. **Sela Anand**, Atlas Subsystems' board strategist — sharp, gracious, entirely instrumental — makes contact. Atlas builds the shielding; Atlas's equipment died in the blast; Atlas can prove the shielding failure was catastrophic in a way that "a bomb near a reactor" doesn't explain. She wants Ryker to bring her *proof that the failure was internal to the reactor — Nuradyne's negligence, not terrorism* — to carry the Continuity review and break Nuradyne's grip on the grid.

She offers real things: Atlas credentials that open half the doors Ryker's been picking, a safehouse, money. She is the first faction to treat Ryker as an asset out loud. The player can take the deal, string her along, or refuse.

What Ryker can find in Anand's own comms, if he reads her terminal at the Atlas safehouse: Atlas didn't originate the receivership motion. A back-channel memo, source scrubbed but routed through a Continuity relay, *advised* Atlas that "the board mood favors a stewardship challenge" and "the Continuity Office would not obstruct such a filing." Anand believes the motion is Atlas's own bright idea. It was planted. Somebody wants Atlas to reach for Nuradyne's throat.

*(Twist 1 tightening: the same office pushing the terror narrative louder is also nudging Atlas to attack. Both ends of the board fight are being fed by the referee.)*

### Mission 7 — The Warehouse (Halcyon)

The relocation warehouse — Halcyon Residential's overcrowded, underfunded human dumping ground — is where the evacuated sector went to be forgotten. Ryker returns Tobin to Della here. It's the game's moral floor: no politics, just people breathing rationed air behind a corp's cost-benefit line, waiting on a "timeline" Nuradyne controls and will never actually publish, because the sector can't be reopened until Voight finds Kadu.

Della, grateful and unbought, gives Ryker the slum's own intelligence: there's a man everyone in the old sector knew to stay away from — foreign, old, terrified of the dark and of Nuradyne both, muttering about "other skies," fed by the syndicate out of pity or contempt. He didn't evacuate through the checkpoints. He went *down.* Into the subway.

To reach him Ryker goes through **Halloran**, who runs the **Ash Market** out of the tunnels under the warehouse district and controls the only unwatched way into the sealed sector's underside: the disused **Aire Gardens** line. Halloran will deal — for a price, for a job, or over his dead crew, player's call.

### Mission 8 — Aire Gardens, and Down

**Aire Gardens** is the set piece: a Meridian Transit station built when the corps still wanted the city to *look* like a place worth living — brass and glass and thirty-year-old luxury advertising for lives nobody in the warehouse will ever have. Ryker arrives at the concourse and takes the long inclinator down, and on the way the outer glass gives a straight view of the sector under quarantine: the dead dome, the poisoned air pressed against the panels, the reactor's black wound at the center of it. Whole way down, no cut, just the city dying behind glass.

The elevator bottoms out at the subway. Here Ryker can, if he pushes the right NPCs (a stranded Meridian tech, Halloran's people), learn a piece of grim comedy from `Ideas.md`: how the checkpoints were beaten. Not by the operative — by *Corvin's* people, weeks ago, dry-running a relay attack on aircraft and door authentication "for a continuity audit." The UCO had already probed the sector's security perimeter before the blast. Ryker files it. He'll understand it later.

### Mission 9 — Merrick

In the subway dark, behind a barricade of scavenged FMA-pattern junk, Ryker finds the liaison: **Merrick**. Old, wrecked, brilliant, terrified. He recognizes Ryker's gear before Ryker says a word — recognizes the *FMA* — and it breaks something open in him, thirty years of being the only man alive who knew and being unable to tell anyone who'd believe it.

Merrick delivers the cosmic layer, the thing none of the schemers upstairs know:

- The reactors aren't power plants. They're **siphons**. They reach into other universes and drain them. He gave Nuradyne the multiversal physics, decades ago, in exchange for shelter, hoping they'd build a door home. They built a straw instead.
- The universes being drained *die.* Not damaged — killed. And here is the part Merrick has never said aloud because it made him complicit in something with no name: **the reactors are serial.** When a drained universe hits its energy floor and can't sustain the tap, the reactor doesn't stop. It **retargets.** Automatically. It finds a fresh universe and starts again. "Abnormal readings" — Harmon's suppressed reports, the dead scientist's blank ticket — that's a retarget event. A world finishing, and the machine reaching for the next one, mid-cycle, hitching as it swallows.
- Every dome in the Corporate Union, every reactor in every city, is one of these. The whole civilization runs on a rotating slaughter of universes, and it has been running for thirty years, and nobody who profits from it has any idea, because the only three people who understand it are a broken exile, a director who chose not to, and now Ryker.

Merrick's last piece: the world that came back — the operative — is the world that was being finished when this reactor "acted up." They didn't send a bomber. They sent someone to *close the door from the outside* before the tap could retarget onto the next universe down the line. The explosion was the aperture tearing, not a weapon. There was never a bomb. The terrorist is a fiction on top of a fiction.

Kesler's voice, over infolink, the instant Merrick names the FMA: *"Ryker. Is that— confirm the asset's identity."* A pause. *"Do not tell him you've reported him. Continue."* First cold note. She knew Merrick could be alive. She's not surprised. She's *deciding.*

### Mission 10 — Kadu

Ryker crosses the operative. **Kadu** — the "Envoy," in a failing force-field suit, hunted by Voight through the tunnels, half-dead and entirely alone in a universe that murdered theirs. Kadu doesn't read as a monster. Kadu reads as the last functionary of a dead government doing the last thing on the list. They took Kadu for another Nuradyne gun until Ryker doesn't shoot.

Kadu confirms Merrick and adds the last turn of the horror: their universe is **gone.** Drained past the floor while Kadu was in transit. The door Kadu came to close closed behind them onto a world already dead. Kadu isn't retaliating for a wound. Kadu is a survivor of a completed genocide who crossed over to stop the *next* one — because the reactor, retargeting, has already found its next universe, and every hour it runs, that world dies faster. Kadu's mission is no longer revenge or even sealing. It's: **shut the reactors down, all of them, or the machine eats the multiverse one world at a time forever.**

This is the pivot of Ryker's whole understanding. The manhunt everyone's fighting over is a hunt for a witness to a serial crime the hunters don't even know is happening. And Ryker now holds the one truth that would blow apart every scheme on the board — if anyone above ground could be made to believe it.

## ACT III — THE MANDATE

### Mission 11 — Harmon, second pass

Armed with Merrick and Kadu, Ryker goes back through Nuradyne to nail Harmon down — this time not for a confession of terrorism but for the retarget data Kesler keeps asking for and for proof of what the reactors are. Harmon's private engineering archive (multitool the reactor-project vault, read his terminal) has it all: retarget event logs going back years, each one a world finishing; his suppressed reports up the chain; and Ashfield's replies telling him to contain it and keep building. Harmon isn't a fanatic. He's a man who looked at genocide, decided it was above his pay grade, and kept the lights on. That's worse.

The confrontation can end four ways in miniature (kill him, break him for a signed statement, leave him, or turn him — a frightened Harmon who's just watched an outsider assemble the whole thing might, for the first time in years, want it to stop). What Ryker takes from the vault is the load-bearing evidence for every ending: *the reactors are what they are, Nuradyne knew, and it's happening everywhere.*

And here Kesler's mask slips fully. The instant Ryker copies the reactor design core, her infolink changes register — from partner to handler: *"Good. That's the origin package. Ryker, new priority, and it comes from over my head. The siphon is FMA-derived. That is an FMA liability now. Your objectives are: recover or destroy the design data — done. The liaison is a disclosure risk — sanction him. The source universes are classified non-recoverable — do not engage the Envoy, do not attempt to close or preserve anything. Seal the incident. Come home for debrief."*

Sanction Merrick. Abandon the murdered universes as "non-recoverable." Don't stop the machine — *bury the paperwork.* Ryker was never sent to save anyone or stop anything. He was sent to make sure that the day the FMA's own lost tech turned up running a serial genocide in a primitive universe, there'd be no trace that it was ever FMA's, and no witness left to say so. He's not an investigator. He's an eraser. And "come home for debrief" is what you say to the last loose end before you tie it off.

*(Twist 2 lands, entirely through the infolink verb and the vault documents: the FMA's hidden agenda isn't a cartel or a market — it's institutional cowardice. A thirty-year-old scandal it would rather let a multiverse die than admit to.)*

### Mission 12 — Corvin

Now the political engine finishes assembling itself, and the game's central twist arrives — through a computer and a conversation, not a cutscene.

Reading anything routed through the Continuity relays (Corvin's own systems, reachable via the audit backdoor Ryker learned about at Aire Gardens, or handed to him by Anand once Atlas realizes it's been played), Ryker finds the shape of it laid out cold:

- A pre-drafted proclamation. The **Continuity Mandate** — a dormant CU statute from the founding charter, never invoked: *when board governance fails during an active threat to dome survival, the Union Continuity Office assumes direct executive authority until stability is restored.* It has never been triggered because "board governance failure during an existential threat" has never happened. Corvin is manufacturing both halves.
- The **existential threat**: the terror narrative. Corvin's office has been feeding Cyrene Media the "TERROR IN THE DOMES" line since hour one, over Nuradyne's objections, to make a single reactor accident feel like the opening shot of a campaign that could kill every sector's air. A city that believes terrorists can murder a whole dome's atmosphere will accept anything from whoever promises to stop them.
- The **governance failure**: the receivership fight. Corvin planted the stewardship motion in Atlas's lap and fed Nuradyne's paranoia in equal measure, engineering a deadlock — the two most powerful seats locked at each other's throats, the board unable to act, at the exact moment the city is most afraid. Modeled, in her files, to the day.
- When the deadlock and the panic peak, the Mandate triggers "automatically," and the UCO — the referee with no shareholder and no ambition — becomes the sole executive authority of the Corporate Union. Not a coup that shoots anyone. A coup that *waits*, having arranged for everyone else to shoot each other.

The kid's overheard line — *the whole board's gonna eat each other and that's the whole idea* — was Corvin's plan, repeated down the chain by people who thought it was a figure of speech.

And here is the twist's cruel core, the thing that reframes the entire investigation: **the "terrorist attack" was never Nuradyne's win.** Nuradyne invented the terror story to hide the siphon and immediately lost control of it, because a terror story is the single most useful object in Umbermont and the office built to answer terror simply took it. Nuradyne lit a fire to cover a body; Corvin took the fire and burned down the house. Every faction Ryker has met — Nuradyne, Atlas, the FMA, the slums — has been fighting over the wrong thing. The bomber doesn't exist. The negligence is real but beside the point. The multiverse genocide is real and *nobody on the board even knows about it.* The one person who fully understood the political board — Corvin — is the one person who never needed to know what the reactors actually are, because her weapon was never the reactor. It was the *word* "terrorist," and everyone's certainty that someone else must be behind it.

Then Corvin meets Ryker in person, and she's the most reasonable voice in the game. She knows what he is — off-grid, deniable, unlogged, the perfect instrument — and she doesn't threaten him. She offers him the one thing no one else has: *order.* The board is a squabbling cartel that lets a slum sector suffocate over a jurisdiction timeline; she can end that in a day. She'll open the sector, house the warehouse, and yes, break Nuradyne — put the grid under a single accountable hand. Hers. She doesn't know it's a genocide machine. When Ryker, if he chooses, tells her — she doesn't flinch. *"Then it's even more important that one person controls it. Imagine that in the hands of the board."* She may be right. That's the trap of her.

### Mission 13 — The Split

Everyone wants Ryker now, and their asks are mutually exclusive:

- **Kesler / the FMA:** kill Merrick, destroy the data, abandon the universes, come home to a debrief you may not survive. *Bury it.*
- **Anand / Atlas:** give me the negligence proof, break Nuradyne, save the board. (Anand doesn't know about the siphon; if Ryker tells her, she has to decide whether Atlas can survive that truth being public.) *Distribute it.*
- **Corvin / UCO:** step aside or step in; let the Mandate trigger; hand the machine to one clean hand. *Centralize it.*
- **Kadu / the drained:** the politics are noise; shut the reactors down before the next world dies. *End it.*
- **Merrick:** just wants to not be murdered by the people he ran to and the people he betrayed alike, and — if pushed — to finally tell the truth and let it cost what it costs. *Confess it.*

Ryker's verbs are the whole toolkit: who he talks to, which terminals he reads and what he copies where, who he kills or spares, which doors he opens. The endings are which set of those he commits to.

## Factions & power structure

- **Nuradyne** (power). Holds the deadliest secret and the least control of the story it started. CEO **Lenore Ashfield** ordered the cover-up and the terror framing and now watches the UCO turn her own fire against her. **Harmon** knew and chose the lights. **Voight** is competent, frightened, and hunting a witness to a crime she doesn't know is genocide. Nuradyne's goal: contain, stay quiet, survive the receivership, keep siphoning.
- **Atlas Subsystems** (shielding). Genuine rival, genuinely wronged (its equipment died in the blast), genuinely being used. **Sela Anand** thinks she's running a play; she's a piece. Goal: break Nuradyne's grid monopoly via Continuity review. Doesn't know about the multiverse.
- **Union Continuity Office** (enforcement/arbitration). The referee that decided to win. **Chief Marshal Ada Corvin** engineers the terror narrative and the board deadlock to trigger the dormant **Continuity Mandate** and rule directly. Doesn't know about the multiverse — and doesn't need to. The true beneficiary of the "terrorist attack."
- **Cyrene Media** (comms) and **Meridian Transit** / **Halcyon Residential** — the swing seats. Cyrene is Corvin's megaphone (witting or paid). Halcyon runs the warehouse and profits from the sealed timeline. Meridian owns Aire Gardens and the tunnels. Minor players who tip whichever way the crisis blows.
- **The slums / syndicates.** **Halloran**'s Ash Market controls the underside; **Wren** sells passage; **Della** and the warehouse are the human cost. They have no seat and no say, which is the whole indictment of the CU.
- **The FMA.** Ryker's employer and, it turns out, an institution that would rather let a multiverse die serially than admit its own lost tech caused it. **Kesler** is the handler running Ryker as an eraser. Goal: seal the incident, destroy FMA-derived data, silence Merrick, deny everything.
- **The drained universe(s).** Represented by **Kadu**, sole survivor of a completed genocide, trying to stop the next. The reactors' retarget logic means there is always a next.

## Key characters

- **Liam Ryker** — the wildcard every faction mistakes for an instrument. Blank affect, military competence, morally flexible; the player decides whether "just business" or "ends justify means" hardens into something with a spine by the end.
- **Nadia Kesler** — the warm partner voice that curdles into a handler. The betrayal is delivered entirely over infolink, so the player feels the temperature drop in real time.
- **Chief Marshal Ada Corvin** — the antagonist who never raises her voice or her weapon. Patient, plausible, arguably correct. The scariest thing about her is that she's blind to the real horror and her plan works anyway.
- **Lenore Ashfield / Director Harmon / Kira Voight** — Nuradyne's three faces of complicity: the executive who ordered it, the engineer who allowed it, the cop who enforces it without knowing what "it" is.
- **Sela Anand** — the competent rival who learns she's a pawn, and gets one real choice: bury the truth to save Atlas, or break the board open.
- **Merrick** — the causal root (canon) as a haunted witness, not a mastermind. The only man who both understands the machine and has no power at all.
- **Kadu** — grief with a mission. Refuses to be a villain even as the city hunts them as one. The moral weight the political story keeps trying to ignore.
- **Tobin & Della** — the reason any of it matters; the overheard truth and the ungovernable gratitude.

## The central twist(s)

**1 — The Continuity Mandate (political, new to this project).** The "terrorist attack" framing's true beneficiary is not Nuradyne, who invented it to hide the siphon, but **Marshal Corvin and the UCO**, who took the fire and used it to burn down the board. Corvin manufactured an existential-threat narrative (fed to Cyrene against Nuradyne's wishes) and a governance deadlock (the planted Atlas receivership motion) to trigger a dormant charter statute that dissolves the corporate-seat board and installs the enforcement office as sole executive. A coup that never fires a shot because it arranged for everyone else to. And the sharpest edge: the mastermind of the political board is the one player who never needed to know the reactors are multiversal — her weapon was only ever the *word.* Planted via: Tobin's overheard "the Marshal wants it big"; Voight's memos raging at whoever's feeding Cyrene; Anand's back-channel receivership memo; the Aire Gardens continuity-audit relay probe; and finally Corvin's own pre-drafted proclamation, read off her terminal.

**2 — The FMA eraser (hidden-agenda invitation, playable).** Ryker was never an investigator. When the FMA's own thirty-year-lost liaison tech surfaced running a serial genocide in a primitive universe, the FMA sent a deniable off-grid asset to make it never have happened: destroy the origin data, sanction the witness (Merrick), write off the murdered universes as "non-recoverable," and come home to a debrief for the last loose end. Institutional cowardice, not conspiracy for profit. Delivered entirely through Kesler's shifting infolink orders and the vault documents — the moment Ryker copies the design core, his partner becomes his handler.

**3 — The serial machine (siphon horror, planted throughout).** The reactors don't drain a universe. They drain universes, plural, *in sequence* — when one hits its floor and dies, the tap auto-retargets to the next. Harmon's suppressed "abnormal readings" are retarget events: worlds finishing. Every dome in the Corporate Union runs on a rotating slaughter, thirty years deep, and only three people alive understand it, none of whom sit on the board fighting over it. Kadu is the survivor of a completed cycle who crossed over to stop the next. Planted via the datacube, the blank "retarget?" ticket, Merrick's testimony, Harmon's vault logs, and Kadu.

## Player-choice structure & endings

Ryker's late-game commitments are built from ordinary verbs: who he talks into what, which evidence package he copies to which terminal, who he kills or spares, whether he lets a broadcast or a proclamation go out. Four ideological endings, each a coherent answer to "who should hold the machine, and should anyone know what it is?"

**A — Continuity (authoritarian order).** Ryker stands aside, or actively delivers Nuradyne's negligence proof into the deadlock at the right moment. The Mandate triggers; the board dissolves; Corvin rules Umbermont directly. She opens the sector, empties the warehouse, breaks Nuradyne's monopoly — real relief, real competence — and the grid, genocide machine and all, passes to one accountable hand that will never let the truth out because a single ruler has no rival to expose it to. The FMA is satisfied (a unified authority is one throat to hold). Stability bought with the murdered universes as permanent, silent capital. Ryker's coldest ending: *just business.*

**B — The Board Reforms (pluralist compromise).** Ryker backs Atlas openly, hands Anand the negligence proof, and exposes Corvin's Mandate scheme to the board itself — the referee's coup dies when the players see the fix. Nuradyne's grid goes into trusteeship; the board survives, chastened and plural. But unless Ryker also forces the siphon truth on them, the reactors keep running under a committee instead of a monopoly — one cartel traded for a slightly larger one, the cosmic crime buried under governance reform. The most "realistic," least clean ending. (Variant: Ryker also tells Anand the truth, and she must choose whether Atlas can survive making it public — folding this toward C.)

**C — The Broadcast (radical transparency / rebellion).** Ryker refuses every handler. Using Cyrene's own transmitter (the weapon Corvin built, turned), Merrick's testimony, Harmon's vault logs, and Kadu as living proof, he puts the whole thing on every screen in the Union: the multiverse is real, the domes run on serial genocide, the terror was manufactured, the coup was a fix, the FMA covered it. It costs everything — the CU's legitimacy shatters, the power that keeps the air clean is now known to be built on murder, and no one has a plan for the morning after. But the drained universes get warned, the next world in line gets a fighting chance, and the lie ends. Defies the FMA outright; Ryker becomes the loose end that got loud. The idealist's catastrophe.

**D — The Eraser (institutional obedience).** Ryker follows Kesler. He destroys the origin data, kills Merrick, leaves the reactors running and the universes to die "non-recoverable," lets the board fight resolve however it resolves, and walks back through the portal to a debrief. The scandal never happened; the FMA's hands stay clean; the machine keeps eating worlds; Umbermont never learns anything. The bleakest ending and the one the game's opening quietly promised — the mission Ryker was actually hired for. Whether the debrief is a handshake or a bullet is left to the last door he opens. *Ends justify the means, if you never ask whose ends.*

(A fifth, hidden resolution rewards the player who both **shuts the reactors down with Kadu** — accepting that Umbermont's own air now fails, evacuating the domes into the poison to save the multiverse — and refuses every faction. The ultimate ends-justify-means inversion: sacrifice this world to stop it murdering all the others. Reachable only by trusting Kadu over every human agenda.)

## Themes

- **Whoever names the crime owns it.** The "terrorist attack" is a fiction, and the story is a fight over who gets to hold that fiction. Nuradyne makes the word, loses the word, and the office built to answer the word takes the city with it. Framing is power; the referee always wins.
- **The fight is over the wrong thing.** Every schemer on the board is playing a brilliant game around a machine none of them understand. The largest crime in the setting is invisible to everyone with the power to stop it, precisely because they're too busy fighting over its wheelhouse. Politics as spectacular displacement.
- **Cowardice scales.** From Harmon (it's above my pay grade) to the FMA (better a dead multiverse than a live scandal) to Corvin (better one ruler than a messy board), evil here is mostly people declining to look at what they're standing on. No cackling villain — just a chain of reasonable men and women choosing not to know.
- **Order vs. truth vs. mercy.** The four endings are three incompatible virtues — stability, transparency, and the lives of the unseen — and the flat, deniable outsider is the only one positioned to choose between them. The blank slate is the point: the player supplies the conscience the institutions all outsourced.
