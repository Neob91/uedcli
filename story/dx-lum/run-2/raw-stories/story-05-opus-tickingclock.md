# DX_LUM — The Ebb Tide

**Run 2 | Writer: opus | Emphasis: siphon cascade / ticking clock**

---

## Logline

A slum reactor doesn't just explode — it starts *bleeding*, and the bleed is spreading sector to sector across Quinmore, failing the domes one seal at a time. Ryker is sent to find a saboteur and finds a wound instead: Nuradyne's reactors have been draining a mirror world, another sealed dome-city dying of poisoned air exactly like this one — and now that world's survivors are trying to climb through the hole in the sky before their side goes dark. The clock isn't counting down to a bomb. It's counting down to a decision nobody wants: which world breathes, and who gets to close the door.

**How it reads.** Cold and procedural, present-tense, clock-driven. Section headers are cascade phases and seal-integrity readings. The horror arrives quietly — in a child repeating a phrase he doesn't understand, in a containment log Ryker isn't cleared to see — and the last act is a triage board where every option is a body count. Deus Ex with a drowning-refugee-boat sitting under the whole thing.

---

## What I take as fixed vs. what I invented

**Fixed (canon, kept intact):** Liam Ryker as blank-slate low-affect ex-military merc, morally flexible. The FMA policing the multiverse; the faint transversal anomaly from U-7134; Ryker covert, no cover, no contacts. Quinmore, the Corporate Union, the poisoned atmosphere, sealed dome sectors (lose power → seals fail → toxic air floods in, time to evacuate then uninhabitable). Nuradyne = power, Atlas = dome shielding; corporate jurisdiction lets Nuradyne cordon its own blast zone. The central secret: reactors siphon energy from parallel universes, devastating them; only Nuradyne's inner circle knows; CU board approved them as "energy tech." The inciting incident: a drained universe traced the siphon, enlarged the aperture, sent a force-field operative through; the operative killed Nuradyne security, fled into the slums, and the blast killed Atlas's shielding and its backup. Nuradyne frames it as terrorism, cordons the district, screens evacuees for the operative, dumps them in a relocation warehouse. The stranded FMA liaison who sold Nuradyne the tech and was cut loose. Director Harmon (reactor project head, stonewalls, suppressed the abnormal readings). Kira Voight (Nuradyne Chief of Security). The FMA partner at the outpost. The slum mother whose son was detained after overhearing something. Prologue beats (FMA HQ → cloaked outpost → Northgate → train downtown). The datacube in the vent. Player-verb limits.

**Invented:** The nature of the cascade (a two-way *harmonic bleed*, not a one-off blast) and its phased spread across named sectors as the ticking clock. The drained world as a **mirror dome-city dying of the same poisoned air** — U-7134's own condition run backward — and the operative as a refugee **scout for an exodus**, not merely a retaliator. The FMA's hidden agenda as **border-control monopoly** — it exists to keep unlicensed doors between worlds *shut*, and would rather a world drown than let an uncontrolled gate stand. Harmon reframed as a man secretly *throttling* the siphon to slow the bleed, paralyzed between shutdown and exposure. Named cast: partner Dana Corwin, liaison Elias Kline, operative Sael, kid Milo and mother Dahlia, Nuradyne CEO Lena Sokol, an Atlas rep, a containment scientist Ohms. Aire Gardens used per `Ideas.md`. The endings.

## The clock

The reactors don't run on fuel; they run on a *pull* — a standing aperture between U-7134 and one drained world, held open by the reactor and metered by Nuradyne to draw exactly as much energy as Quinmore's domes need. The operative's charge didn't destroy the aperture. It *tore* it, and a torn aperture doesn't close — it resonates. Nuradyne's own term, buried in containment logs, is **harmonic bleed**: the tear widens on a curve, and every reactor tied into the same aperture net starts drawing unevenly, browning out the sectors it feeds. Lose the draw, lose the power. Lose the power, lose the seals. Lose the seals, and the outside air comes in.

The whole story sits on that curve. I mark it as the reader goes.

> **BLEED 04% — SLUM DISTRICT (CINDER ROW): SEALS FAILED. SECTOR LOST.**

That already happened before Ryker landed. The number only goes up.

## ACT I — The wound

### Mission 1 — FMA Headquarters (Prologue, canon)

Recalled from leave. A low-level clerk points Ryker to the briefing room. The room gives him the shape of the thing: an anomaly out of U-7134, faint, erratic, unprecedented — could be unlicensed travel, could be worse. His handler, **Dana Corwin**, will run him from the outpost by infolink. Medical clears him. The armory kits him for underground work and toxic air. Optional orientation: a returning field team trades gossip in the mess about a border sweep two universes over that "went loud" — a throwaway line the player forgets. (It isn't. The FMA has been sealing doors elsewhere. Planted here, paid off in Act III.)

Ryker portals to the cloaked wasteland outpost with Corwin. Sensors chew on the residual signature. Corwin triangulates it — not to a lab, not to a portal frame, but to a *power reactor* in Quinmore's slum district. "That's wrong," she says, mostly to herself. "That's a lot of energy for a door nobody's using." She flags it and moves on. (Also planted. She already suspects it isn't travel. She doesn't tell Ryker that, because her orders don't tell *her* everything either.)

Cloaked aircraft to **Northgate**. A relay attack spoofs the aircraft's authentication so it reads as a scheduled Atlas maintenance flight — if the player pokes at the outpost console, they learn the trick (per `Ideas.md`). Northgate is a mid-tier residential dome, cheap and crowded. On the walk to the downtown train, three men jump Ryker for his coat and his kit. He leaves them breathing or he doesn't; the player sets the tone. On the train, the ad-boards loop Nuradyne's corporate calm — *POWERING EVERY BREATH* — over footage of a smiling dome at dawn. Between stops, the lights flicker once, hard, and come back. A woman across the aisle mutters that the brownouts started three days ago. Corwin, in Ryker's ear: "Note that."

> **BLEED 04% — HOLDING. NORTHGATE: FLICKER.**

### Mission 2 — The cordon and the mother (canon)

The slum district — **Cinder Row**, Ward 7 — is a black hole in the middle of the city. No lights, no seal, no air. Nuradyne security in breathers work the checkpoints, funneling the last evacuees through face-scanners into a corporate relocation warehouse three blocks up. The official line runs on every board: *TERRORIST DETONATION. CRIME SCENE. INVESTIGATION ONGOING.*

At the checkpoint gate a woman is arguing with an officer. Her name is **Dahlia**. Her son **Milo** was out playing in Ward 7 when the evacuation ran, in a spot nobody knew about, and he never came out. The officer won't log it, won't look, won't let her past. He turns to wave Ryker off — *no entry* — and Ryker produces credentials. (Forged, hacked, or talked into existence; the player chooses the infiltration verb.) The officer waves him through. Dahlia catches his sleeve. *Find my boy. Please. Nobody else will.* Ryker can promise, deflect, or say nothing. Objective logged either way: **the kid** rides in the back of his mind now, because Corwin heard it too and won't let it go.

The relocation warehouse, if the player detours in, is the story's thesis in one room: four thousand people who lost their air overnight, sleeping on pallets under a banner that reads *TEMPORARY*. A Nuradyne caseworker recites relocation timelines nobody believes. This is what a failed seal looks like from the inside. Remember the room.

### Mission 3 — The reactor site (canon + the first crack)

Past the cordon, into Ward 7 in a breather. Cinder Row is a corpse of a neighborhood — dark, silent, the outside sky visible through a torn dome panel, the air a faint chemical shimmer. The reactor hall is worse. Logs erased. Nuradyne security dead where they fell — not shot, most of them. *Burned through*, cleanly, like something walked past them radiating heat. (The operative's force-field. The player clocks the wound pattern without a name for it.)

Ryker searches. In a vent near a dead scientist, a **datacube** (canon, verbatim beat):

> *"Security doubled on our floor since yesterday. Can't reach anyone outside the facility, they cut my comm access. Reactor readings are way outside normal operating parameters. Reported it to Director Harmon, told me to forget about it. Something's not right. If anyone finds this—"*

Two leads: **Harmon**, and the **abnormal readings**. But there's a third thing the scientist didn't put on the cube, and Ryker finds it on a half-fried maintenance terminal he multitools open — a live gauge still ticking. The reactor is dead, blown, cold. And the gauge is still *drawing*. Something is still pulling through this hall, through the slagged aperture ring at the reactor's heart, even with the machine destroyed. Corwin, when Ryker relays it: "That's not residual. That's not decay. Ryker — that's *open*." She goes quiet for a second too long. "Find Harmon."

On the way out, Ryker passes the aperture ring up close. For half a heartbeat the torn air inside it isn't dark. It's *grey* — a low grey sky over dark rooflines, seen as if through dirty glass, and gone. The player will not know what they saw until Act II. But they saw a city.

> **BLEED 09% — NORTHGATE: BROWNOUTS. AIRE GARDENS: FLICKER.**

## ACT II — The mirror

### Mission 4 — Nuradyne HQ, and Director Harmon (canon)

Nuradyne HQ downtown — basement labs and range, upper floors for execs, security, and intelligence. Ryker gets in on his infiltration verb of choice. Harmon is exactly where a man with nothing to hide would be: at his desk, running the reactor project, business as usual. He stonewalls. The datacube? A frightened junior misreading routine variance. The readings? Within tolerance. The explosion? A tragedy, a terrorist act, under investigation, out of his hands.

What Harmon *says* is rehearsed. What he *doesn't* say sits on his desk in plain sight if the player reads his terminal (multitool the drawer, or catch it unlocked): a private throttle schedule, unsigned, that has been quietly *cutting* the reactor net's draw for six days — starving the domes of margin, causing the brownouts — on a curve that mirrors the bleed. Harmon isn't hiding negligence. Harmon is *fighting* something, alone, with the only lever he has, and it isn't enough. He notices Ryker's eyes on the screen and kills it. "You have no idea what you're standing on," he says, and it isn't a threat. It's exhausted. He has Voight's people show Ryker out.

Corwin, after: "He's throttling the reactors. Deliberately. A man covering up a bomb doesn't do that. A man covering up a *breach* does." First time she's said the word to Ryker. She doesn't say the rest.

### Mission 5 — The holding cell, and the child (canon + first hinge)

Ryker's route to intelligence takes him through a Nuradyne holding block nobody logs. In a cell: **Milo**. Grabbed by security under standing orders to detain anyone found in the sealed district, thrown here, forgotten. Dahlia's boy. Ryker gets him out — quiet or loud, the player's call.

The kid is fine, mostly. Scared, hungry, running his mouth the way kids do. And in the run of it he says a thing he doesn't understand. He says the burned man was in the next cell for a while — the man who *glowed* — and the man talked to himself, and Milo repeats it the way a child repeats a nursery rhyme in a language he's never heard: a few syllables, wrong-sounding, and then one sentence in plain English that the burned man must have said to the guards, or to himself, or to no one:

> *"Your side's still got sky. On our side the air's already in."*

Milo doesn't know what it means. Ryker does, halfway. Corwin, listening, goes cold. "*Our side.* He's not from a rival corp. He's not from Atlas. Ryker, the operative thinks of somewhere as *our side*." She pauses. "And their air's already in. Their seals already failed."

Two dying dome-worlds. The player doesn't have the whole shape yet, but the shape is arriving. Get Milo back to Dahlia (a mid-mission humanity beat, and a favor Ryker can bank) or leave him with the evac coordinator — either way the phrase is now in Ryker's head, and it doesn't leave.

> **BLEED 17% — AIRE GARDENS: BROWNOUTS. RESERVOIR SECTOR: FLICKER.**

### Mission 6 — Aire Gardens, and the man who sold the sky (canon liaison, per `Ideas.md`)

Nuradyne intelligence points two ways. One thread — the readings — runs into a Nuradyne containment lab under the Reservoir sector, hardened, high-clearance. The other thread — *who could possibly know what this reactor really is* — runs down, into the slums under **Aire Gardens**.

Aire Gardens is the story's cruelest joke: a luxury dome, chandeliers of grow-light, ad-holos selling private air-shares and imported garden views to people who will never fail a seal. Ryker arrives via chopper in the landing area, takes the train-station concourse, and rides a glass inclinator down — down past the manicured levels, past the service floors, down where the light stops — to the subway. One long descent through the whole class structure of a dying city, the outside grey visible the whole way through the dome wall (per `Ideas.md`: no level cut; one real elevator).

In the subway warrens Ryker finds **Elias Kline**. Broken, twitchy, older than his years, living in a maintenance alcove with a chemical lamp and a knife he can't really use. The stranded FMA liaison. He clocks Ryker's gear in one look — *FMA cut, that's an FMA breather rig* — and nearly bolts, because the FMA showing up is the one thing he's feared more than Nuradyne for twenty years.

Kline, talked down (converse; he responds to being treated as the only expert alive, which he is), gives Ryker the spine of the whole thing:

- He traded Nuradyne everything he knew about multiversal energy, years ago, for a life inside the domes and the hope they'd build him a door home. They never did. They built the *siphon* instead, and once their engineers understood it, they threw him away.
- The reactors don't make power. They *take* it — through a standing aperture — from another universe. He watched them tune it. He begged them to pick a dead world, an empty one. They picked the one that answered easiest to the frequency. He didn't ask what was there.
- And the thing that answered easiest: **another sealed dome-world, another Quinmore, another Earth that poisoned its own air and hid under glass.** A world already living on the exact margin Quinmore lives on. Because a world already balanced on that knife is the *easiest* to pull from — its whole grid is one shove from tipping. Nuradyne didn't drain a wilderness. They drained a mirror. They've been browning out someone else's domes to light their own, for years, and calling it *cutting-edge energy.*

Kline is shaking by the end. "You want to know why the readings went bad *this* time? Because *their* side finally ran out of margin. You can only skim a drowning man for so long before you're holding him under. Their seals started going. And a drowning man will do *anything*." He means the operative. He means the tear.

And then the thing that ties Act III's knot, delivered as an old man's terror, not exposition:

"The FMA doesn't fix this. Don't you understand what you *are*? The FMA doesn't open doors. The FMA *closes* them. That's the whole religion. An unlicensed aperture between two worlds, held open, uncontrolled — that's the one thing they burn the world down to stop. You call your handler and tell her there's a live door in Cinder Row and people trying to come through it, and she will not send help. She will send a *torch*. I *was* the FMA. I know exactly what order you're about to get."

Corwin's channel is silent through all of this. Ryker will notice, later, that she was listening the whole time and said nothing.

### Mission 7 — The containment lab (the readings, the curve, Harmon's ghost)

The Reservoir containment lab, hardened, is where Ryker turns Kline's testimony into proof — the reveal delivered by *reading computers*, as the verbs require. Ryker breaches it (multitool, hack, or fight past Voight's detail). Inside:

- **The containment logs.** Term for the phenomenon: **harmonic bleed.** A tear in a standing aperture doesn't close; it resonates and widens on a curve. Attached: the projection. The bleed spreads reactor to reactor across the shared aperture net, browning out and then *failing* sectors on a timeline. The curve names the order: Cinder Row (gone), Northgate, Aire Gardens, Reservoir, and last, downtown — the Nuradyne HQ core sector itself. A city eating itself seal by seal.
- **Harmon's suppressed reports.** Not a cover-up of a bomb. A six-day record of one man throttling the siphon to *slow the widening*, begging leadership for a full shutdown, and being overruled. Because a full shutdown means Quinmore goes dark — every dome, all at once, no power to any seal — and Nuradyne leadership will not do to Quinmore what Quinmore has been doing to the mirror. Not out of mercy. Out of arithmetic: a controlled brownout that kills the slums quietly is survivable PR; a total blackout that kills the paying sectors is the end of the company. Harmon is trapped between a breach he can't close and a board that won't let him pull the plug.
- **The board thread.** The overrule comes from CEO **Lena Sokol** and the inner circle. And one email, forwarded and half-redacted, shows the truly cynical read: leadership has known for *days* that the bleed is terminal for at least the outer sectors, and the checkpoints screening evacuees "for the bomber" are doing double duty — Nuradyne is quietly *deciding which sectors to write off* and slow-walking their evacuations, because you can't relocate everyone and you can save the paying core if you let the edges drown on schedule. The triage has already started. It just isn't Ryker's yet.
- **The other side, once.** A single containment capture — not surveillance of *people*, a physics readout, an aperture spectrum log Ryker can open on the console — carries an embedded telemetry ghost from the far end: atmospheric composition, seal-integrity, population-density estimates. It reads like Quinmore's own dashboard. Someone over there built the exact same instruments to watch the exact same death. The lab tech who logged it, **Ohms**, wrote one line in the notes: *"It's not a power source. It's a city."*

Reading the capture trips a triggered infolink — but not from Corwin. From **Harmon.** He's been watching this lab's access log (he owns it) and he knows someone with FMA gear just read the one file he's spent six days trying to bury. He doesn't threaten. He offers a meeting. "You've seen it now. Come talk to me before you call your people. Please. There's a version of this where fewer of them die, and it needs both of us, and it needs you to *not make the call you're about to make.*"

> **BLEED 38% — NORTHGATE: SEALS FAILING. AIRE GARDENS: BROWNOUTS. RESERVOIR: BROWNOUTS. HQ CORE: FLICKER.**

## ACT III — Which world breathes

### Twist 1 (paid off here) — the enemy is a refugee, and the drained world is a mirror of Quinmore

Between the lab and the meeting, the operative finds Ryker first. **Sael.** The force-field walks out of a service tunnel radiating that clean killing heat, and doesn't attack. Sael has been hunting for the aperture ring's control side — the reactor was destroyed, but the *tear* is what matters now, and Sael needs someone who can reach the containment side. Sael talks (converse; the force-field is a translation-and-survival rig, and it's failing).

Sael is not a soldier of a rival power. Sael is a **scout.** Their world — call it the far side of the aperture — is three domes from total seal failure. Their reactors never siphoned anyone; they simply ran out of a world to burn and got skimmed, for years, by a source they couldn't identify until one physicist traced the theft to a frequency, and the frequency to a door, and blew their own aperture wider to shove one person through before their side collapsed. Sael didn't come to retaliate. Sael came to find out if the far side — *this* side, Quinmore — had *room.* Whether there was air over here to share. Whether the thieves were monsters or just another dome full of frightened people doing the arithmetic. The killing at the reactor was a panic in a language nobody spoke, a scared person in a burning building. "I came to knock," Sael says. "I didn't know how to knock."

And the tear is a door now. Widening. On Sael's side, thousands are massing at their end of it, because a widening aperture is the only exit their dying city has left. The bleed that's failing Quinmore's seals is the *same event* as the opening of an escape hatch for a drowning world. You cannot have one without the other. Close the tear and you save Quinmore's remaining sectors and you seal a hundred thousand people into a dome with no air. Hold it open and you keep the drowning world's exit — and the bleed keeps widening until Quinmore's own seals go, one paying sector at a time, until the arithmetic comes for the core.

That's the ticking clock's true face. Not a bomb to defuse. A door to a lifeboat that is also a hole in your own hull.

### Twist 2 (paid off here) — the FMA's agenda is the wall, not the door

Ryker's objectives from Corwin have been narrowing all through Act II, and now the real order lands — and the player earns it *within the verbs*: reading it, not being told it clean.

Path A: Ryker breaks into Nuradyne intelligence's inter-corp comms cache (already in-mission) and finds Corwin's *own* traffic — the outpost has been transmitting to FMA command on a channel Ryker was never given, and command's replies are cached here because Nuradyne, unknowingly, has been logging the very frequency the FMA rides. Ryker reads his handler's real briefings next to the sanitized ones she fed him.

Path B: Ryker returns to the outpost (or Corwin, rattled, patches him a "clarification") and multitools the directive file behind her console.

Either way the FMA's doctrine comes out cold and complete. The FMA did not send Ryker to *investigate an anomaly.* Command flagged the signature days before the briefing and read it correctly the first time: not travel — a *standing unlicensed aperture,* the one category of event the FMA treats as existential. The mess-hall gossip in the prologue — the border sweep two worlds over that "went loud" — was the FMA doing exactly this, somewhere else, last month. The Authority does not police the multiverse to *protect* the worlds in it. It polices the multiverse to keep the *doors* under its exclusive control. An uncontrolled, self-widening bridge between two Earths, with a hundred thousand people trying to walk across it, is not a humanitarian event to the FMA. It is a breach in the wall. And the FMA's standing answer to a breach is not evacuation. It is **cauterization.** Collapse the aperture. Deny the crossing. No survivors of the crossing, because survivors are witnesses to a door the FMA didn't sanction. Leave no evidence the Authority was ever in U-7134.

Ryker's true mission, the one Corwin was steering him toward without ever saying it: locate the aperture's live control side, and be the hand that closes it. She fed him the sanitized version — *investigate, assess* — because a blank-slate merc who thinks he's chasing a bomber will walk the exact path a witness-scrubbing torch needs to walk, and won't hesitate at the end. Kline was right to the word. "I *was* the FMA. I know exactly what order you're about to get."

Corwin, if confronted (converse/infolink), doesn't deny it. She's not a monster; she's a believer, and tired. "You've seen one uncontrolled door. I've seen what happens to a cluster of worlds when there's a door in it nobody owns. Wars that cross universes. Plagues that cross universes. Harvests that cross universes. The wall is ugly. The wall is the only thing that's kept the ugly from being *everywhere.* Close it, Ryker. I'll get you home. Nobody down there is yours." The last line is the whole FMA in six words.

### Mission 8 — The meeting, and the surgeon's arithmetic

Harmon's meeting is the hinge where the choices assemble. He lays out what he knows and what he can do, and he's honest because he's out of moves.

He can't close the tear — the reactor that anchored it is slag; the *aperture control* is on the far side now, or in the ring itself, and reaching it needs Sael's force-field to survive the field, and needs the containment lab's throttle to feed it, and needs Ryker to be the one who chooses, because Harmon can't choose and Sokol won't and Voight only takes orders. He has one plan of his own — a **controlled cascade**: overload the reactor net in a precise order to *pinch the tear shut cleanly*, cauterizing it the way the FMA wants, but on *his* timing, so the sectors he's already written off take the surge and the core survives. It's the FMA's outcome by a company man's math: seal the door, save the paying city, let the edges and the far world drown, do it quietly, call it a tragedy. "It's the fewest deaths I can get to," he says. "I've done the number a hundred times. It's the fewest *countable* deaths." He knows what the word *countable* is doing. He says it anyway.

Voight is in the room, or arrives to it. **Kira Voight** has spent the whole story as the enforcer hunting Sael, and somewhere in Act II she read the same logs Ryker did — she's Chief of Security; she has clearance he had to steal. She knows the "terrorist" is a refugee. She knows her CEO is slow-walking evacuations to pick which sectors drown. Voight is the story's swing vote, and the player's conduct decides which way she swings: play straight with her, spare her people, get Milo home, and she'll break from Sokol at the end. Burn through her officers and she stays loyal to the only structure that ever paid her. She is not good or evil. She is the muscle the ending needs, going to whoever earned her.

> **BLEED 61% — NORTHGATE: SECTOR LOST. AIRE GARDENS: SEALS FAILING. RESERVOIR: SEALS FAILING. HQ CORE: BROWNOUTS.**

### Mission 9 — Cinder Row, the aperture ring, the board

Everything converges back where it started: the dead reactor hall in Ward 7, the torn aperture ring still drawing, still widening, the far side's grey sky pressing at it now like a held breath — and on the far side of that grey, close now, shapes. People at a door. The bleed has climbed past sixty; the core sector's brownouts have started; Quinmore is hours, not days, from the number that ends it.

Ryker has, in hand or in reach, every lever: the containment throttle (Harmon), the field-survival to touch the ring (Sael), the security muscle (Voight, if won), the truth in a form the CU board and the public could receive (the logs, the telemetry ghost, the FMA cache — all readable, all real), and the FMA's torch order (Corwin, waiting for him to be the hand). The clock is the last character in the room. The player chooses.

## Endings — four triage dilemmas

Each is a genuine choice about *which world breathes*, and each buys its good outcome with a real body count the player is made to see.

### Ending A — **The Torch** (FMA doctrine / Quinmore-first, cold)

Ryker follows the order he was really given. With Sael's field to reach the ring (Sael cooperates, believing they're stabilizing the door — or Ryker takes the field by force from Sael's body), Ryker cauterizes the tear. The aperture pinches shut. The bleed stops on the curve; Northgate and the slums are already gone, but Reservoir, Aire Gardens, and the core hold. Quinmore breathes.

The far side does not. The grey sky and the shapes at the door wink out mid-crossing — the ones already through the ring are the only survivors of a hundred thousand, and Corwin's cleanup means even they don't get to be witnesses. The FMA extracts Ryker clean. No evidence the Authority was ever here. Nuradyne keeps the reactors — retuned to a *new* frequency, a *new* mirror world that answers easiest, because the machine still needs to eat and the board still needs the power. The horror doesn't end. It relocates. Ryker goes home having closed a wall and started a fresh drain, and the last infolink is Corwin: "Good work. This is the job. It's always this job." Just business.

### Ending B — **Hold the Door** (open border / share the air, idealist and ruinous)

Ryker refuses the torch and keeps the aperture open, using Harmon's throttle not to pinch the tear but to *stabilize* it — bleed the siphon down to power-neutral so the widening stops, and route Quinmore's remaining margin to hold the crossing. It works, barely, and only by triage the player performs by hand: there isn't power for every dome. To hold the door and keep the core breathing and let the far world climb through, Ryker (with Voight, if won, running the evacuations straight this time) must **choose which Quinmore sectors go dark** — pull Aire Gardens' luxury air-shares, or gut Reservoir, or write off Northgate's evacuees for good — to free the power that keeps the lifeboat open. Two dying cities become one crowded surviving one, and the seam of it is a scar: refugees and evacuees in the same relocation warehouses, the same *TEMPORARY* banner over both peoples now. The FMA marks U-7134 as a breach it failed to close and does not forgive it — the last infolink is Corwin, not angry, just certain: "You made a door the wall can't hold. They'll come for it. Maybe not this year." Ryker chose more people alive and a worse, harder world, with an Authority now aimed at it. The most human ending, and the one that costs Quinmore its comfort and its safety at once.

### Ending C — **Blow the Whistle** (truth / burn the arrangement down)

Ryker doesn't touch the aperture. He takes the logs, the telemetry ghost, Harmon's suppressed reports, and the FMA cache, and he *broadcasts* — to the CU board, over Aire Gardens' own ad-holos, into the relocation warehouse, everywhere a computer can be made to speak. The city learns in one hour that Nuradyne murders worlds for wattage, that its own leadership was choosing which sectors to drown, that there are *other universes* and one of them is dying at their door — and that an Authority they thought long gone is standing in the wings with a torch. Sokol's inner circle falls; Harmon, exposed, either turns state's evidence or eats a bullet depending on how Ryker played him; the arrangement between Nuradyne and the CU that built the domes cracks in public.

But truth doesn't close a tear. The bleed keeps climbing while the city screams. In the chaos the crossing becomes a stampede — the far world pours through an unstabilized, still-widening door into a Quinmore that has no plan and a failing grid, and whether that ends in a shared survival or a double collapse the ending leaves *genuinely* open, gambled on the crowd. The FMA, its secret loose in an entire universe, has a much bigger problem than one door now, and withdraws to decide what to do about a world that knows. Ryker ends it having refused to be anyone's hand — not the torch, not the throttle — and handed the choice to everyone at once. The truth was owed. The truth was not safe.

### Ending D — **The Surgeon's Cut** (Harmon's controlled cascade / the middle knife)

Ryker takes Harmon's plan: the controlled cascade. Not the FMA's clean scrub and not the open door — a *chosen* cauterization on the company's math, pinching the tear shut through a precise overload that routes the surge into the sectors already written off, saving the core and whatever's been evacuated in time, and letting the far world drown on a timer Ryker set himself rather than one the FMA set for him. It is the fewest *countable* deaths, exactly as Harmon promised. Ryker gets to save the most people he can *touch* and has to stand in the ring and watch the door full of shapes go dark to do it — no FMA lie that it didn't happen, no broadcast that spreads the cost around, just one man choosing to close a lifeboat with people in it because the alternative arithmetic was worse.

Harmon survives if the player kept him alive to run the throttle, and the two of them share the last scene without a word, because there's nothing to say that isn't the number. Sael, if alive, is one of the handful through before the pinch — a single survivor of a world, which is either mercy or the cruelest possible thing to be. The FMA gets its closed door and never learns Ryker chose the timing; Corwin signs off satisfied, not knowing she was disobeyed in the only way that mattered. Quinmore breathes, quieter than in Ending A, because the man who saved it knows exactly what it cost and will carry the far side's grey sky behind his eyes for the rest of his flat, low-affect life. Ends justify the means. The means were a world.

## Factions & power structure

- **Nuradyne** — power, and therefore the whip hand on the CU board. Inner circle knows the reactors siphon; CEO **Lena Sokol** and the board run the cynical triage (which sectors to write off, checkpoints doubling as a sorting of who drowns on schedule). **Harmon** is inside but sideways: a true believer in the machine turned into its lonely, failing brake. **Voight** is its muscle, loyal to structure and pay, swingable by Ryker's conduct.
- **Atlas Subsystems** — dome shielding. Publicly blames Nuradyne's lax security for the blast; privately suspects a Nuradyne power play and is *right for the wrong reason* — it's not a play, it's a leak from another universe. Atlas is the corporate lever for Ending C's fallout: give Atlas the truth and Nuradyne's grip on the board shatters. Atlas doesn't care about the refugees; it cares about winning the seat.
- **The Corporate Union board** — no civilian government, just domains. Approved the reactors as "energy tech," ignorant of the siphon. In the disaster they're the audience Ryker can play to (C) or the machine that overrules Harmon (A/D).
- **The slums / syndicates** — the human ground floor: Dahlia, Milo, the relocation warehouse, the Aire Gardens subway warrens where Kline hides. They have no power and the entire cost lands on them first, in every ending.
- **The FMA** — revealed as the multiverse's border wall, not its protector. Doctrine: uncontrolled doors get cauterized, witnesses included. **Corwin** is its patient, tired, human face. Ryker is its unwitting hand.
- **The far side (the mirror world)** — a sealed dome-city dying of the same poisoned air, skimmed to death by Nuradyne's siphon, now trying to climb through the tear. Represented by **Sael**. Not villains. Not saints. A drowning city doing arithmetic, exactly like Quinmore.

## Key characters

- **Liam Ryker** — the hand everyone wants. His flat affect is the mechanism: he can stand in the ring and do the number where a feeling person couldn't, which is precisely why the FMA chose him and precisely what each ending asks him to spend.
- **Dana Corwin** — handler, believer, liar by omission. Fed Ryker the sanitized mission because the wall needs a hand that doesn't hesitate. Not cruel. Convinced.
- **Director Harmon** — reframed from stonewaller to the story's tragic surgeon: the man secretly throttling the siphon to slow the bleed, trapped between a board that won't blackout the paying city and a breach he can't close. His plan is Ending D.
- **Kira Voight** — Chief of Security, the swing vote. Learns the truth from her own clearance; goes to whoever earned her.
- **Elias Kline** — the stranded liaison. Sold Nuradyne the tech, was thrown away, hides in the Aire Gardens subway. Delivers the mirror-world truth *and* the FMA-torch warning — the one man who's seen both secrets and been destroyed by knowing them.
- **Sael** — the operative recast as a refugee scout. Came to knock and didn't know how. The face of the far world at the door.
- **Milo & Dahlia** — the child who carries the clue (*"on our side the air's already in"*) and the mother whose small human plea threads through the citywide arithmetic. The story's insistence that the number is made of people.
- **Lena Sokol** — Nuradyne CEO, the clean cynical arithmetic: save the paying core, let the edges drown on schedule, keep the machine eating.
- **Ohms** — the containment tech whose one log line — *"It's not a power source. It's a city."* — is the whole horror in seven words.

## The central twists, called out

1. **The disaster is a mirror, and the enemy is a refugee (new).** The reactors aren't draining a faceless void — they've been skimming *another sealed dome-world dying of the same poisoned air,* because a world already balanced on the knife is the easiest to pull from. The "cascade" (harmonic bleed) and the "escape hatch" are the *same widening tear*: you cannot save Quinmore's seals without sealing a hundred thousand people into a domeworld with no air, and you cannot hold their lifeboat open without letting your own city drown sector by sector. The operative isn't retaliation — he's a scout who came to ask if there was room. Delivered within the verbs: Kline's testimony, the containment logs and telemetry ghost (read on computers), Milo's overheard phrase (conversation), Sael's own account (conversation), and the grey-sky glimpse at the ring.

2. **The FMA is the wall, not the shield (FMA-agenda invitation).** The Authority didn't send Ryker to investigate — it read the aperture correctly on day one and dispatched a blank-slate hand to *cauterize* it, witnesses included, and leave no trace it was ever here. Its purpose is monopoly on doors, not protection of worlds; it will let a universe drown to keep an unlicensed gate from standing. Planted (prologue mess-hall "border sweep," Corwin's day-one unease, her silence during Kline) and paid off within the verbs: Ryker *reads* his handler's real traffic in Nuradyne's comms cache and multitools the directive behind her console. Kline's line — "I *was* the FMA. I know exactly what order you're about to get" — is the fuse.

Supporting reframe: **Harmon**, the canon stonewaller, is secretly the city's failing brake — his "suppressed abnormal readings" hide a rescue, not a negligence, and his controlled-cascade plan is the fourth ending.

## Themes

Triage as the true face of power: every authority in the story — Nuradyne, the FMA, Harmon, and finally Ryker — is doing the same arithmetic, deciding which people are *countable*. Borders and lifeboats: the door in the sky is a drowning-refugee boat at a sealed harbor, and "close it to save ourselves" is offered without a cartoon villain to make it easy. The mirror: Quinmore poisoned its own air and hid under glass, and it has spent years doing to another world exactly what the war did to it — the monster and the victim are the same city seen from two sides of one tear. And the cost of a flat, useful man: the very absence of feeling that makes Ryker able to do the number is the thing the whole world is counting on him to spend, one way or another, before the clock runs out.
