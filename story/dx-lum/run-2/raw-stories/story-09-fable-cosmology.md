# The First Sky

Run 2 | Writer: fable | Emphasis: multiverse metaphysics / the anomaly

---

## 1. Logline

An FMA agent sent to a poisoned corporate dome-city to trace a faint transversal anomaly discovers
that the "energy" its reactors steal from other universes is not energy at all — and that the dying,
chemical-choked world he's standing in is not one universe among many, but the buried root every
other universe grew from. His employer has known for eighty years. That is why they kept a bunker
here. That is why they sent him. And that is why, if he does his job too well, they will burn this
world to save all the others.

**How this version reads:** a slow-rising cosmological horror story wearing a corporate-conspiracy
thriller as a coat. Act one is pure Deus Ex — checkpoints, forged badges, a dead scientist's
datacube, a stonewalling director. Act two turns the crime inside out: the theft isn't what anyone
thinks, and the victim worlds aren't strangers. Act three inverts the mission itself: the last
hostile facility Ryker infiltrates is his own side's outpost, and the final choice is not *who runs
Quinmore* but *what the multiverse owes its origin — and what the origin owes its children.* The
cosmic never floats free: every metaphysical claim arrives through a lottery ticket, a doubled
photograph, a boy's recurring dream, a man who was knocked for once and never answered the door.

---

## 2. Fixed vs. invented

**Taken as fixed (canon, untouched):** Ryker's blank-slate low-affect profile and outsider status;
the FMA as multiversal police detecting a faint, unprecedented, erratic anomaly from U-7134; the
prologue chain (HQ recall → briefing → medical → armory → portal → cloaked wasteland outpost →
partner triangulates the slum reactor → cloaked aircraft to Northgate → train downtown); the
Corporate Union, the poisoned atmosphere, the dome sectors and their failure mode; Nuradyne (power)
vs. Atlas Subsystems (shielding) mistrust; the multiversal siphon reactors known only to Nuradyne's
inner circle; the drained universe that bombed the aperture wide and sent a force-field operative;
the slum reactor explosion, the terrorist framing, the cordon, checkpoint screening, the relocation
warehouse; the stranded liaison who sold FMA knowledge to Nuradyne and was discarded; Director
Harmon's suppressed abnormal-readings reports; Kira Voight, Chief of Security; the mother at the
cordon and her missing son found in a Nuradyne holding cell having overheard something; the erased
logs, dead security, the vent datacube naming Harmon; androids as non-revolting servant flavor; all
player-verb limits (conversation, one-way infolink, kill/incapacitate, multitool doors, readable
computers; no player surveillance-tape review, no timestamp-hacking).

**Invented:** everything about what the siphon's payload actually is ("lum"); the tree cosmology
(umbilic scars, the root world, the canopy); the FMA's Vigil program and Root Directive; the First
Light congregation; partner Cass Renn's arc; the operative Sef Adaya and the drained world Adan;
liaison Dr. Aldous Wren's name and arc; Odessa Marr; Tommy and Renata Vidal; Nuradyne reactor names
(Lantern Three, Crown, Northgate Deep); Atlas officer Petra Ives; Adjudicator Osei; the four endings.

## 3. The truth underneath (spoilers — the cosmology the story excavates)

Laid out here so the reader can watch the plants go in. In play, Ryker digs this up piece by piece,
strictly through conversations, computers, and infolink.

**Universes are not siblings. They are generations.** A universe, over deep time, *buds* daughters —
spending something of itself to do it. The FMA's classified physics calls that something **the
Lume**: not joules, not mass-energy, but the deep stock a universe draws on to be *undetermined* —
to have weather that surprises, mutations that invent, histories that could have gone otherwise.
A world rich in Lume is generative. A world poor in it becomes **inevitable**: everything that
happens is the only thing that could have, and it grinds downhill in straight lines.

**Budding leaves a scar.** Every universe carries exactly one inbound **umbilic** — the healed
puncture where it was budded from its parent — and however many outbound umbilics it has spent
itself making. Transversal travel is only possible along umbilics. The FMA's whole jurisdiction, its
"multiverse," is one family tree, and it has always privately wondered where the trunk was.

**U-7134 is the trunk.** The oldest surveyed universe. Thousands of outbound scars; no inbound scar
ever found. FMA designation, in files Ryker was never meant to read: **R-0. Root candidate —
confirmed.** It is poor because it is generous: it spent its Lume budding the canopy, over spans that
make geology look hasty. Its lethal sky is a war wound, but the war itself was a symptom — a
Lume-starved world is *predictable*, and the Vigil's models watched both sides walk a straight line
into mutual atmospheric suicide decades before it happened, forecast to the season. The FMA did not
warn its contacts. A quiet root is a safe root.

**Nuradyne's reactors drill the scars.** The siphon does not reach "sideways" into random neighbors.
Physically it *can't*. It pumps through U-7134's own outbound umbilics — which means every drained
universe is a daughter of this one. Quinmore's domes are lit by the futurity of the root's own
descendants. And the payload the intake manifolds log as "carrier content, structured, non-thermal"
is Lume itself: the reactors take the could-have-been and burn it as power. Drained worlds do not go
dark first. They go *certain*.

**The anomaly.** Steady-state siphoning is invisible to FMA sensors, which are built to read
*transit* — this is why nine years of draining raised no flag, and it is the FMA's quietest
nightmare, because it means draining could be happening anywhere and they would never know. What
their grid finally caught, faint and erratic and unprecedented, was the siphon *breached*: Adan's
aperture bomb blowing a scar dilated, backwash flooding up the borehole into the slum district.
The anomaly is the siphon — but only the moment it screamed.

**The Root Directive.** Vigil physics holds that when a root universe finally collapses, its
outbound scars equalize: a dying trunk pulls sap back, draining every daughter at once. The whole
canopy dims with it. So the FMA has kept an eighty-year deathwatch on U-7134 — the "listening post"
in the wasteland sits on the densest outbound scar cluster, and it is not a listening post. It is a
scalpel. The contingency, if the root ever destabilizes: **prune** — collapse the scar cluster,
sever the trunk from the tree, and let U-7134 die alone in the dark rather than take its children
with it. Nuradyne's siphon has advanced the root's projected collapse from millennia to decades.
The Directive is now live. Ryker's partner is holding it.

## 4. The story, mission by mission

### Prologue — Recall

*Canon beats, played straight, with three small burrs left under the skin.*

Ryker is pulled off leave by a junior FMA clerk who won't meet his eyes, briefed on a faint
transversal anomaly out of U-7134 — "barely detectable, unprecedented signature, we're not sending a
representative, we're sending you." Medical evaluation, armory, optional orientation loops through
FMA HQ. The burrs, for the attentive:

- The medical officer runs one scan not on the standard sheet. If asked: "Scar-field exposure
  baseline. Where you're going, the walls are old." She won't elaborate. Her terminal, if Ryker reads
  it while she's called away, shows his chart flagged **CLASS NULL DESTINATION — VIGIL NOTIFY**,
  routing codes he can't parse yet.
- The armorer, an old-timer: "U-7134. Huh. Didn't know we still flew that board. My first posting was
  monitoring that dead rock — twenty years of nothing. You know what's strange? Nothing's *supposed*
  to be a relief."
- The briefing withholds shape, not just detail. The adjudicator's exact words: "Assess whether the
  anomaly is travel, technology, or *category error*." Nobody explains the third option.

Portal to the cloaked wasteland bunker with partner **Cass Renn** — mid-forties field analyst, dry,
kind in a rationed way, the person who will read Ryker's telemetry for the whole mission. Renn
triangulates residual transversal energy to a reactor in Quinmore's slum district and stays behind
"to run independent analysis." Cloaked aircraft to Northgate. On approach, one infolink line the
player will reread later: *"Whatever's down there put out the strangest signal I've ever charted,
Ryker. It didn't read like something arriving. It read like something* answering."

### Mission 1 — Northgate

*Epigraph, spray-stenciled in a Northgate underpass:* **THE FIRST SKY WAS OURS.**

The Aire Gardens multiport: chopper pad, glass inclinator descending past luxury advertising —
Nuradyne's slogan everywhere, **"POWER WITHOUT END"**, a smiling family under a dome lit gold; Atlas
Subsystems' counter-campaign, **"THE SHIELD REMEMBERS."** A porter android takes coats it will never
give back to evacuees who never checked them; if pressed, Ryker can learn from a bored technician how
the pad's aircraft authentication can be fooled by relay — flavor now, an infiltration tool later.

The inclinator drops to the subway level. Thugs jump Ryker at the platform — a Northgate crew called
the **Tolling Boys** shaking down anyone without a corp badge. Fight, sneak, or pay. Their fixer, if
talked down, gives the district's temperature: the slum blast, the cordon, "Nuradyne screening every
face like they lost a wallet, not a reactor," and a first strangeness offered as a joke: "Warehouse
people all having the same dream, they say. Same *dream*. City with a white sky and two rivers.
Wish I dreamt at all."

On the train downtown, Ryker shares a car with a street preacher of the **First Light** — a slum
congregation, symbol a white tree with domes for fruit. The preacher's patter sounds like standard
apocalypse cult until it doesn't: *"They tell you the domes keep the poison out. The domes keep the
light* in. *Borrowed light, brother. And everything lent comes home."* The First Light formed nine
years ago — the player can later notice that is exactly when Lantern Three came online — among
families housed nearest the reactor, out of their dreams.

### Mission 2 — Lantern Three

*Epigraph, from a recovered datacube:* **"Reactor readings are way outside normal operating
parameters. Reported it to Director Harmon, told me to forget about it. Something's not right. If
anyone finds this"**

The slum district entrance, exactly per canon: **Renata Vidal** arguing with a Nuradyne officer about
her missing son, Tommy; the officer rounding on Ryker; Ryker's forged clearance getting him through;
Renata catching his sleeve — *find my boy, he's eleven, he was only playing.* Optional promise. The
player's first ledger entry in a story that will end with a ledger for a whole world.

Inside the cordon: a dome sector with its sky off. Emergency lighting, contaminated-air warnings,
Nuradyne forensics teams in sealed suits. The reactor — **NDR-3, "Lantern Three"** — is a burst
lantern indeed: containment petals peeled outward, the intake hall a cathedral of slag. Logs erased
or corrupted. Security personnel dead — and readable terminals in the forensics staging area note,
in a pathologist's draft email never sent, that the wounds are wrong for a bombing: close-quarters
kills, blunt trauma through armor, "as if the assailant's own body were the delivery system for
something that didn't touch him back."

In a vent near a dead scientist: the canon datacube, verbatim. Two leads — Director Harmon, and
abnormal readings.

But Lantern Three offers a second layer to anyone who searches instead of sprinting. Small wrong
things, all delivered through objects and testimony, never spectacle:

- A dead guard's personal effects bag holds **two identical family photographs** — same frame, same
  crease across the daughter's face. The evidence clerk's terminal: "Duplicate logged in error?
  Item was inventoried once."
- A maintenance android in a scorched alcove, undamaged, running a loop: it recites what its log
  calls *ambient acoustic capture, source unresolved* — a soft phrase in no catalogued language,
  timestamped from the night of the blast, repeating every three minutes into an empty room.
- A survivor being treated at the triage point — a Nuradyne pipefitter, sedated, talking through it:
  "It sang. Before every big draw, the hall sang, we all knew it, you'd feel your fillings. That
  night it sang *backwards.* Then the light came up out of the floor. Light's not supposed to have a
  *direction*, man."

Renn, on infolink, once Ryker's suit sensors have walked the intake hall: *"Residual's the wrong
shape, Ryker. Transit events are a spike — in, done. This is a* standing wave. *Whatever crossed
here crossed through something that was already open. Already open for years."* First crack in the
briefing: the anomaly wasn't the arrival. The arrival used a door that predates it.

### Mission 3 — Business as Usual

*Epigraph, Nuradyne lobby inscription:* **POWER IS A PROMISE KEPT.**

Nuradyne HQ, per canon geography: gun range, armory, research labs, and forensics in the basement;
CEO, offices, Security Division, Intelligence upstairs. Ryker gets in by any of the three doors the
sim offers — forged contractor credentials, a social-engineered escort from an overworked facilities
manager, or the hard way through the loading dock with multitools.

**Director Elias Harmon** is at his desk, because of course he is — the head of the reactor project
has no reason to be anywhere else, and disappearing would be a confession. The conversation is a
fencing match the player can steer but not win. Harmon is rehearsed, courteous, bloodless. He calls
the explosion "the event," never "the attack" he's publicly blamed on terrorists, never "the
malfunction" the datacube implies. He dismisses the dead scientist by name before Ryker gives the
name. What he avoids saying draws the outline of what he knows.

One unrehearsed moment, available only if Ryker presses on the phrase *abnormal readings*: Harmon's
hand stops over his desk, and he says, quietly, as if quoting someone he's tired of arguing with —
"Instruments at the edge of any new physics produce artifacts. Echoes. You learn which noises are
noise." Then the shutters come down. *Echoes.* The player files it.

The basement pays better than the man. Forensics terminals hold the suppressed pathology thread.
An Intelligence-division memo, readable on a floor supervisor's machine, sets checkpoint doctrine
for the cordon: **"Screening priority: subject will not scan as local. Biometric baseline will not
resolve against any CU registry. Detain, do not report on open channels, notify Voight directly."**
Nuradyne is not hunting a bomber. It is hunting someone who isn't from here — and knows it. Reading
this memo triggers Renn's infolink and the next objective, per the canon mechanism: the intake
telemetry the datacube's scientist saw must exist somewhere Harmon couldn't scrub — Security keeps
its own mirrors. Voight's annex.

### Mission 4 — The Boy Who Heard

*Epigraph, child's handwriting on a holding-cell wall:* **I WANT TO GO HOME. WHICH ONE.** *(The
second sentence in different, adult writing, much older.)*

The Security annex is a converted transit substation two blocks from HQ — cells, interrogation
rooms, evidence lockup. Mid-infiltration, exactly per canon, Ryker finds **Tommy Vidal** in an
unlogged holding cell: eleven, filthy, furious, and — once he decides Ryker is not Nuradyne —
a flawless little witness. He was playing in a dead cable duct when the sector evacuated; came out
to an empty world; got grabbed by sweep teams with standing orders. Nobody logged him. Nobody cares.

What Tommy overheard, through the cell vent, he reports with a child's fidelity and zero
comprehension — and Ryker, per canon, understands:

- Two technicians, scared, off-shift: *"The intake was live when it blew. You get that? It was
  mid-draw. The payload came back* up. *"*
- Voight, cold, in the corridor, to Harmon on a call: *"You told the board terrorists. I've seen the
  intake logs, Director. Don't manage me."*
- And the phrase Tommy repeats because it frightened him without meaning anything: the technicians
  kept saying **"the donor."** *"Like it was a place,"* Tommy says. *"Like you'd say 'the coast.'
  'It's bad on the donor side.' What's a donor side?"*

Tommy also has his own data, offered shyly, as if it might be a symptom: since the blast he dreams
the same dream every night. A city under a white sky. Two rivers. A ferris wheel that never turns.
"Mom has it too. She won't say so, but she says the rivers' names in her sleep."

Getting Tommy out (stealth escort, a bribed duty officer, or a cell-block blackout — player's
choice) and back to Renata at the **relocation warehouse** opens the story's human anchor level: a
corporate-run hangar of cots and chemical toilets in a central district, four thousand evacuees,
First Light meetings by the loading doors, service androids distributing calorie bricks with
apologetic politeness settings. Renata's gratitude is the warmest thing in the game. Her neighbor is
the reward: **Odessa "Dess" Marr**, ex-Nuradyne reactor operator, five years on Lantern Three's
intake floor, quit after what the crews privately called **the echo shift** — a night three years ago
when every gauge on the draw manifold ran backward for nine seconds and two operators woke up
weeping and couldn't say why. She filed a report. It went to Harmon. It went nowhere. "You want the
real instruments," Dess says, "you want what Security mirrors for blackmail purposes. Voight keeps
receipts on everybody. Even the physics."

### Mission 5 — The Intake Logs

*Epigraph, from Security mirror archive NDR-3/IN-TEL, header line:* **CARRIER CONTENT: STRUCTURED /
NON-THERMAL / DO NOT CHARACTERIZE FURTHER (DIR. HARMON)**

Kira Voight is the story's hinge NPC, and the player chooses how to open her: burglary (multitool
into the annex archive while she works a cordon inspection), leverage (bring her the forensics
thread proving her own department's pathology was overridden by Harmon's office), or the front door
(tell her a truth — any real truth — and watch her trade). Voight is not inner-circle by conviction;
she's inner-circle by clearance. She has watched the checkpoint footage — *she* can, the player
never does — and she describes it flatly to Ryker if allied: a figure walking out of a burning intake
hall through small-arms fire that stopped a hand's width from its skin. "I have spent nine years
securing a power company," she says. "I would like someone to tell me what I actually secure."

The intake logs, however obtained, are the mid-game detonation, read raw off a terminal:

- The reactors do not meter their draw in joules. The primary channel is labeled **carrier
  content** — *structured, non-thermal* — with an energy yield listed as a *byproduct fraction*.
  The machine's own bookkeeping admits the power is the exhaust. The cargo is something else.
- Draw targets are indexed **D-1 through D-14** — *fourteen donors* — with per-donor "viability
  curves" trending down, and D-7's curve flatlined eleven months ago with the annotation
  **"donor exhausted — retarget."**
- Harmon's suppressed incident file, mirrored in full: six prior "echo" events across nine years —
  backflow transients, gauges running in reverse, and after each one, a personnel-health annex
  Harmon marked NOT FOR DISTRIBUTION: operators reporting *identical dreams*. He assumed, in his
  own words, "the affected universe is attempting interference. Precautions taken. Stabilizers
  sufficient." He was proofing the pipe against sabotage. It never occurred to him the pipe was
  *carrying* something on its own.

Renn's infolink, after Ryker transmits: a long silence, then, in a voice the player hasn't heard
from her — *"Ryker. Don't relay this to HQ yet."* A beat. *"That's not procedure. I know. Please."*
The player has just watched a loyal FMA analyst commit her first crime. Most will assume she's
protecting Ryker. She is not. She has recognized the donor indexing schema — because she's seen the
notation before, in files with a Vigil header, and she is beginning to understand what her real
posting is.

### Mission 6 — The Man Who Stayed

*Epigraph, handwritten on the flyleaf of a water-swollen physics text:* **Property of A. Wren.
If found, do not return.**

The logs' oldest design schematics carry a foreign notation — units and operators from no CU
curriculum. Dess Marr recognizes the handwriting culture if not the math: "The ghostwriter. Old
Nuradyne joke. The founding documentation nobody was allowed to photocopy." Tracing paper this old
means the slums' grey market for corporate salvage, and it leads, through a fence and a locked
storage cage and one genuinely sad bribe (the man sells his own past a folder at a time to eat), to
**Dr. Aldous Wren** — the stranded FMA liaison, exactly the broken figure canon promises: bitter,
terrified, alive only because killing him costs paperwork.

Wren's scene is the story's quiet centerpiece, all conversation. He confirms what he sold Nuradyne
and why — decades of hoping they'd crack *travel* and send him home; they cracked *extraction*
instead, "and that was good enough for them." But Ryker's real prize is what Wren *couldn't* sell,
because he never understood it himself:

- Every FMA operations file for U-7134 he ever handled was stamped **CLASS NULL — NO EXTRACTION,
  NO EXPERIMENT, NO DISCLOSURE.** He was posted to a universe with no multiversal technology, no
  strategic value, a doomed geopolitics — and it carried the highest restriction class in the
  service. "I asked once what Class Null meant. My handler said: it means the house rules are
  different here, and you don't want to know why."
- The pre-war outpost predates his posting by decades. "Coordination with the Americans" was its
  cover *inside the FMA*. He never learned its function.
- And his own wound, offered last, cheap and enormous: when the war turned terminal, he begged for
  extraction. Denied — dated, he swears, *before* the final strikes, before his contacts died.
  "They say they searched for me after. They knocked once." He laughs like a cough. "You know what I
  think, agent? I think they wanted this world quiet. I was noise."

Wren will not leave the slums — "I've defected twice; a man only has so many countries" — but he
keeps the FMA field notation alive in his head, and that makes him, in the endgame, one of three
people on the planet who can read a Vigil console. Plant set.

### Mission 7 — The Pilgrim

*Epigraph, transmitted in Adan's dead-drop cipher, translated:* **We followed the theft home and
found the house we were born in.**

Nuradyne's cordon tightens: checkpoint doctrine escalates to "detain on failure to resolve," and
Voight (ally or not) lets slip that sweep teams have twice cornered the fugitive in the sealed
sector and twice carried their own men out instead. Ryker goes in — suit sealed against the flooded
toxic air, a mission through the drowned district where the only light is his own, past the
apartment blocks where the First Light was born, dreaming.

He finds the operative not by hunting but by being permitted to arrive: **Sef Adaya** — late
thirties, wiry, wrapped in a field harness whose emitters have burned scars into their own straps —
waiting in a flooded chapel the congregation abandoned, under their painted white tree. The player
can force combat; Sef is a nightmare to fight (the force field spends itself visibly, and killing
Sef is *possible*, closing routes later). Or talk. Sef has been waiting weeks for anyone worth
talking to, and delivers, in a ruined voice, the drained world firsthand — the payload twist landed
through pure human detail:

"You want to know what your city runs on. Not power. We had power to the end; the dams never
stopped. What left us was — *otherwise.* The word my daughter's teacher used. The lottery drew the
same numbers three weeks running and nobody rioted, that was the worst of it, nobody could even be
surprised. The chess clubs closed; every game had become the same game. The weather service
published a year ahead, *accurately*, and apologized for it. No new songs, agent. Eleven years, and
the last new song anyone wrote was a funeral setting of an old one. Our scientists called it
determinacy creep. Our priests called it the withering of providence. My daughter called it —" the
only pause Sef takes — "she said: *Papa, today keeps being yesterday.* Then the light started going.
Noon like dusk, dusk like a lid. Whatever your machines drink, energy is only what it *casts.*
You are burning the could-be of living worlds to keep your lamps yellow."

Then Sef gives Ryker the thing no one in Quinmore could: **direction.** Adan's cosmologists, tracing
the drain, mapped its topology before Sef came through. "The pipe does not go sideways, agent. Every
model said the same thing and we shot the modelers' funding twice, because it reads as theology.
The pipe goes *down.* Our universe has a navel — one scar, older than our stars, that we were
*budded* from. The theft comes up through it. Do you understand what I am telling you about the
place you are standing?" Sef looks up at the painted tree, at the drowned chapel, at the poisoned
sky beyond the dead dome, and the pilgrim's horror in the character's face is the scene's whole
payload: "We traced the thief to the *first world.* The root of the tree of everything. I crossed
expecting an empire. I found — this. A dying grandmother eating her grandchildren. And your masks.
And your checkpoints."

Sef is dying — transit through a scar without proper technology is a weeks-long death sentence, the
same clock the toxic air gives an unmasked local, and the symmetry is deliberate. Sef's terms: help
finish the mission — every multiversal reactor dead — or stay out of the way. Renn breaks in on
infolink, shaken, mid-scene, having heard everything through Ryker's link: *"Ryker. Come back to the
outpost. Not aircraft comms, not relay. In person. There are things in this bunker I was never
cleared to open, and I've stopped caring."*

### Mission 8 — The Vigil

*Epigraph, Vigil file V-0001, first line:* **This office exists because every tree dies from the
root.**

The act-three inversion: the last facility Ryker infiltrates is his own. The outpost has gone
strange — new FMA telemetry gear portaled in, crates stenciled with a codeword, **ROOT DIRECTIVE**,
and Renn caught between her oath and her arithmetic. Depending on the player's prior choices she is
a co-conspirator who opens the doors, or a torn officer Ryker must talk past, or — if the player has
been transmitting everything to HQ against her pleas — a locked-down handler under orders, and the
level plays as a heist against FMA security systems with multitools and nerve.

The Vigil archive, read screen by screen, reassembles the mission from the beginning — every prior
scene recolored:

- **U-7134 = R-0.** Root candidate, confirmed. Oldest surveyed universe; thousands of outbound
  umbilic scars; no inbound scar. The wasteland the outpost sits in is the densest scar cluster in
  the known multiverse — Vigil slang for the region: **the Orchard.** The bunker predates the war
  because the deathwatch predates the war. "Listening post" was the cover *inside the FMA itself.*
- **The Lume.** The classified extraction physics, in the exact donor-indexing notation from
  Nuradyne's founding schematics — Wren's fingerprints, full circle: the FMA has always known what
  a universe's deep stock is, and Class Null exists because the root's stock anchors everyone's.
- **The war files.** Vigil predictive models forecasting U-7134's chemical suicide decades out, to
  the season — *because a Lume-poor world is predictable* — with the withdrawal order for all FMA
  personnel dated before the final strikes. The knock that came once, for Wren, logged and closed.
  Ryker reads the sentence the whole story has been walking toward: **"Intervention would
  destabilize a quiescent root. Recommend dark posture. A quiet root is a safe root."**
- **The anomaly, re-read.** FMA sensors detect transit; siphoning isn't transit; the grid never saw
  Nuradyne's nine years of draining and never would have. What tripped the alarm was the breach —
  Adan's bomb dilating a scar, backwash flooding the borehole. Appended, flagged to the
  Adjudicators: *"Standing extraction is sensor-dark to the grid. Implication: extraction may be
  ongoing elsewhere in the canopy, undetected, at any scale."* The dread is left un-cashed — a
  window onto a bigger night than this story lights.
- **The Directive itself.** When a root collapses, its scars equalize — the dying trunk pulls back
  what it lent, and every daughter dims at once. Nuradyne's siphon has moved R-0's collapse
  projection from millennia to *inside a century.* The contingency: collapse the Orchard, sever
  root from canopy, and let U-7134 die alone — reactors dead in an hour, grid dark, dome seals
  failing sector by sector, evacuation to nowhere. The Vigil's own casualty annex is honest:
  it names the number. The file's term for the operation is **pruning.** The final order, already
  transmitted to Renn: *"Prepare Root Directive. Asset Ryker not cleared at this level. If asset
  destabilizes, retire asset."*

The scene with Renn, after, is the story's ethical hearth, all conversation: she has the arming
codes and the arithmetic — one poisoned, half-dead world against the slow dimming of every world
that ever budded from it, hers included, everyone's included. "I've been running the numbers since
you sent me the intake logs," she says. "The numbers don't care that I've met Tommy Vidal over your
shoulder for a month. Tell me what makes them wrong, Ryker. I want them to be wrong."

There is one crack in the Directive's math, and the player can find it planted in three places
(Harmon's stabilizer specs, Adan's aperture-bomb telemetry via Sef, Wren's extraction physics): the
scars conduct *both ways.* Draw can be inverted. A reactor can be run as a **return pump** — pushing
Lume back down the umbilics, re-thickening the root's stock, moving the collapse date the other way.
The cost is written in the same specs: a return pump consumes grid power monstrously and forever.
Quinmore — perhaps every Nuradyne-lit dome city — would have to go dim to keep the tree lit.

### Mission 9 — Root and Canopy

*Epigraph, First Light liturgy, final verse:* **And the tree said: I do not regret my leaving
light. I regret only that my children learned to carry knives.**

Convergence. Nuradyne's two surviving multiversal reactors — **NDR-1 Crown** under the financial
district and **NDR-2 Northgate Deep** — spin up to emergency draw to cover Lantern Three's loss;
every extra hour of draw is a shovel of earth off the root's grave. The CU board convenes over
Atlas's formal accusation (Petra Ives, Atlas's liaison officer, has been feeding Ryker cordon gaps
all game out of pure corporate spite, and now wants her payoff: proof it was never terrorists).
Voight stands at the fork of her life with a security force that answers to her and a corporation
that lied to her. Harmon — confronted with the Vigil physics that explains nine years of "echoes" he
filed as noise — breaks in one of the story's best optional scenes: the man who heard the multiverse
answer and decided it was instrument error, asked to read, on Ryker's stolen files, what the answers
*said.* He can end the game dead defending Crown's control room, or alive at its console doing the
only penance that means anything: inverting his life's work. Sef is dying on schedule, and wants to
see one gauge run backward before the end. Wren must decide if a man with no countries left can
still have a species. Renn holds the codes. Tommy and Renata and four thousand warehouse evacuees
hold nothing, which is the point; every ending is priced in their sector's light.

The player assembles their endgame from these pieces. Four resolutions, mutually exclusive at the
final choice, all executed through the established verbs — consoles read and used, allies talked
into place or fought through, doors multitooled, one-way infolink counsel from whoever remains.

## 5. Factions & power structure

- **Nuradyne.** Power monopoly; its inner circle knows the reactors drain other universes but *not*
  the tree cosmology — Harmon indexed fourteen donors without ever asking why the pipe only reached
  those fourteen. Institutionally committed to the terrorist fiction; internally split between
  Harmon's guilt, Voight's curdling loyalty, and a CEO (Corvin Dray) who would drain heaven itself
  to keep the board seat.
- **Atlas Subsystems.** Shield monopoly; publicly blames Nuradyne's security, privately suspects a
  power play, correctly suspects the official story is false, and is wrong about everything else.
  Useful to Ryker precisely because its spite is honest. Atlas wants the truth *small* — a scandal
  it can win a board war with — and is the faction most horrified when the truth turns out huge.
- **The Corporate Union board.** Approved "cutting-edge energy technology" it never understood;
  governs by dividing the city into fiefdoms of infrastructure. In the Broadcast ending it fractures
  along exactly those seams.
- **The slums, the syndicates, the warehouse.** The Tolling Boys, the salvage fences, the First
  Light congregation — the people the light is *for*, who get none of it. The First Light is the
  cosmology's folk shadow: nine years of Lume backwash dreams distilled into liturgy, wrong in every
  detail and right in the one that matters.
- **The FMA — surface.** Multiversal police; sent Ryker to chart a faint anomaly.
- **The FMA — Vigil.** The deathwatch inside the police: keepers of Class Null, the Orchard, and the
  Root Directive. Not a cartoon — its triage math is real, its casualty annexes honest, its sin not
  greed but the eighty-year habit of letting the root world bleed because a quiet root is a safe
  root.
- **Adan (FMA index U-7841).** The drained daughter that fought back: one bomb, one dying operative,
  and a cosmological map its own funders refused to believe. Thirteen sibling donors remain, D-1
  through D-14 minus the exhausted D-7 — a silent choir behind every scene set near an intake hall.

## 6. Key characters

- **Liam Ryker.** Canon blank slate, low affect, morally flexible. This story never explains his
  flatness (run 1 owned that move); instead it *uses* it — Sef, Renn, and the First Light preacher
  each independently remark that Ryker seems like a man from somewhere the could-be ran thin, and
  the game declines to say whether that is trauma, temperament, or a joke the multiverse is telling.
- **Cass Renn.** FMA partner and handler; analyst who discovers her real posting is executioner's
  clerk. Arc: procedure → complicity → the arming codes and a face like arithmetic. The endings
  route through her more than anyone.
- **Director Elias Harmon.** Canon stonewaller, expanded into the tragedy of the disciplined mind:
  nine years of echoes filed as noise. His stabilizers — built to keep the drained from interfering
  — are, in the cruelest turn, the exact hardware a return pump needs.
- **Kira Voight.** Chief of Security who has finally asked what she secures. Ally, enemy, or — in
  the Custodian ending — co-regent. She never lies to Ryker once, which in Quinmore makes her exotic.
- **Dr. Aldous Wren.** The man who stayed. Sold the multiverse to Nuradyne for a bed and was
  discarded; carries Class Null's shape in his head without its meaning; learns at the end that he
  spent forty years homesick inside the only home any universe has. His knock-once grievance is the
  Vigil's whole ethic in one anecdote.
- **Sef Adaya.** Adan's operative: pilgrim, executioner, bereaved parent of a daughter whose todays
  kept being yesterdays. Dying at the toxic air's own pace. The story's voice for the drained.
- **Tommy & Renata Vidal.** The missing boy and his mother; the ledger the endings are priced in.
  Tommy's white-sky dream is D-7 — the exhausted donor — still echoing in the pipes.
- **Odessa Marr.** Ex-intake operator; the echo shift's living witness; the working-class door into
  Nuradyne's physics.
- **Petra Ives.** Atlas's liaison; spite as an intelligence service.
- **Adjudicator Osei.** The Vigil's voice on Renn's orders; never met, only read — deliberately: the
  FMA's conscience should arrive as prose on a screen, the way everything arrives in this story.

## 7. The central twists, called out

1. **The payload is not energy** *(new to the project; the multiverse-siphon horror).* The reactors'
   power output is a byproduct fraction; the cargo is the Lume — a universe's stock of
   undeterminedness. Drained worlds become *inevitable* before they become dark. Planted: "carrier
   content, structured, non-thermal"; the echo-shift dreams; the doubled photograph; the android's
   unresolved acoustic capture; the pipefitter's backwards song — then landed in one speech through
   lottery numbers, closed chess clubs, a year-ahead weather report, and a child's "today keeps
   being yesterday." Fully playable: logs, emails, conversations.
2. **The multiverse is a tree, and U-7134 is the root** *(the central reveal; new to the project).*
   Universes bud daughters through umbilic scars; travel and theft alike run only along the family
   line; every drained donor is this world's descendant; the poisoned, spent, *predictable* Earth of
   canon is the origin of everything — its war itself a symptom of Lume poverty. Planted: Class
   Null; the inexplicable pre-war outpost; the First Light's borrowed-light liturgy nine years old;
   the armorer's twenty years of significant nothing; "the pipe goes down." Landed twice: Sef's
   pilgrim horror in the chapel, then the Vigil files' clinical confirmation — R-0.
3. **The FMA's hidden agenda: the Vigil and the Root Directive** *(the canon-invited FMA twist).*
   Ryker's employer is not merely police; inside it lives an eighty-year deathwatch that let this
   world's war happen ("a quiet root is a safe root"), stationed the "listening post" on the scar
   cluster it was built to sever, and has now armed the pruning of U-7134 — with Ryker's own partner
   as executioner and Ryker himself marked "retire if destabilized." Playable end to end: the
   medical flag, Renn's drift across infolink, and the outpost's readable archive.
4. **The anomaly re-read** *(supporting twist).* The siphon ran nine years invisible — FMA sensors
   read transit, and theft isn't travel. The famous faint anomaly was the breach scream, and the
   file's appended implication (*extraction may be ongoing elsewhere in the canopy, undetected*) is
   left deliberately un-cashed: the night is bigger than this story's lantern.

## 8. Player-choice structure & endings

Throughout, the sim tracks a handful of load-bearing states rather than a karma meter: whether Sef
lives; whether Voight was opened by burglary, leverage, or truth; whether Harmon breaks or dies;
whether Renn was protected from HQ or burned; whether the warehouse (Tommy, Renata, Dess, the First
Light) knows what Ryker knows. These gate which endings can be *staffed* — every ending needs hands
and codes, not just a button.

**Ending 1 — The Pruning** *(order).* Ryker sides with the arithmetic: helps Renn execute the Root
Directive — or, if Renn has defected, takes the codes from her. The Orchard collapses; every scar
seals; Crown and Northgate Deep die mid-draw; Quinmore's grid browns out sector by sector on a
countdown the player walks through once, past the warehouse, going dark. The canopy is safe forever.
Epilogue: FMA HQ, a commendation, the same medical officer, the same extra scan. "Readings nominal."
Nobody says the word Quinmore. The armorer has been reassigned. *The tree lives; the root is never
spoken of; Ryker's affect, for once, is indistinguishable from everyone else's.*

**Ending 2 — The Gift** *(sacrifice).* The return pump: Harmon's stabilizers, Sef's aperture data,
Wren at a console reading his own forty-year-old notation, Voight holding the perimeter of Crown
against her own corporation. The gauge runs backward; Sef sees it and dies in the chair, smiling at
an instrument. Quinmore dims — permanent rationing, sectors consolidated, the domes kept sealed on a
survival budget while the root feeds its children back their could-be. The Directive stands down:
a recovering root doesn't need pruning. Epilogue in the warehouse under half-light: Tommy reports,
solemnly, that the dream changed — the white-sky city's ferris wheel is turning. *The first world
learns to be a parent again, and it costs exactly what parenthood costs.*

**Ending 3 — The Waking Root** *(truth).* Ryker refuses both the FMA's scalpel and Nuradyne's
silence. With Voight's security, Ives's board access, and Dess Marr on the intake floor, he runs
Crown one last time — not to draw, but to *transmit*: the Vigil files, the intake logs, and a
message pushed down all thirteen living scars at once and simultaneously onto every CU public
channel. The daughters learn where their withering came from and what the FMA planned for the
grandmother world; U-7134 learns it is the First World and that it has been eating its
grandchildren; the CU board fractures live on air; the FMA finds its deathwatch deterred, because
you cannot quietly prune a root that thirteen universes are now watching. Epilogue: chaos with the
lights on — embassies without portals, the First Light's chapel suddenly, unbearably crowded, Renn
court-martialed and unrepentant. *The family, awake at last, begins the argument that should have
started eighty years ago. Nothing is safe. Everything is possible — which, in this cosmology, is
the same as being alive.*

**Ending 4 — The Custodian** *(power).* The Deus Ex dark-throne option: Ryker and Voight take
Nuradyne's inner circle from the inside — Dray retired at gunpoint, Harmon kept on a leash of his
own guilt — throttle the draw to Harmon's "sustainable" curve, rotate donors to spread the
withering thin, and feed the FMA a falsified all-clear through a compromised Renn. The domes stay
golden. The children dim slowly instead of quickly, and never learn why. Quinmore's new master of
light is the man who was sent to find the thief. Epilogue: Ryker at Harmon's old desk, reading an
abnormal-readings report, marking it NOT FOR DISTRIBUTION. On the terminal, an unresolved acoustic
capture, three minutes apart, patient. *The root keeps eating, politely now. Something down the
pipe keeps answering. Ryker learns which noises are noise.*

## 9. Themes

**What a parent owes, what a child is owed** — the whole cosmology is generational debt made
physics: the root spent itself making the canopy, and the canopy's brightest city responded by
inventing a straw. **Possibility as the substance of life** — poverty in this story is not
darkness but inevitability; the slums are drained the way Adan is drained, and the warehouse
dreaming one shared dream is what hope looks like when it has to be smuggled through a pipe.
**The ethics of triage at scale** — the Vigil is the story's real antagonist and its most defensible
character; its math is never mocked, only priced. **Knowledge as leakage** — every secret in the
story escapes as residue: dreams, liturgy, echoes, a child's misheard word; the truth behaves
exactly like the Lume, refusing to stay in its container. And under it all, the immersive sim's own
credo, here promoted to cosmology: *a world is exactly as alive as the number of ways it could go.*
