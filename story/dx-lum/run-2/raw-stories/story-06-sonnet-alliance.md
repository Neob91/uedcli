# The Alliance of the Drained

Run 2 | Writer: sonnet | Emphasis: drained universes / first contact

## Logline

Nuradyne isn't draining one universe — it's draining dozens, at different rates, and three of them have found each other before finding U-7134. Ryker goes looking for a bomber and instead becomes the first human any of them have ever spoken to, caught between a coalition that wants a treaty, a remnant that wants Quinmore erased, and a dying world that just wants to be heard before it goes dark — while the FMA's own chain of command turns out to have known the coalition existed for longer than Ryker has been alive.

## Fixed vs. invented

**Fixed (canon, unchanged):** Ryker as blank-slate operative; the FMA and its outpost; U-7134/Quinmore under the Corporate Union; Nuradyne's siphon reactors and Atlas's dome shielding; Director Harmon suppressing prior abnormal readings; Kira Voight as Nuradyne's Chief of Security; the stranded FMA liaison who sold Nuradyne the tech; the slum mother and her missing son; the prologue and arrival beats exactly as scripted (FMA HQ → outpost → Northgate → train → checkpoint → reactor → datacube → Harmon → the kid); the player-verb constraints.

**Invented:** the operative, Sena Voss-Ferrant, given a name, a voice (via a translator rig, not English) and a mission that is diplomatic, not just violent; two more drained universes with their own delegations already active in Quinmore before Ryker arrives (a coalition seeking negotiation, and a faction that has given up on negotiation); a fourth, nearly-extinguished universe that communicates only through decaying, garbled bursts and turns out to be the actual origin of the FMA's "faint anomaly" reading; the revelation that the FMA's founding charter itself was written using multiversal law drafted in consultation with an early drained universe, meaning the FMA has always known siphon-drain was a category of crime and has a standing (unenforced) doctrine for it; a new FMA character, Liaison-Auditor Priya OKonkwo, who has been quietly building a case file on Nuradyne for two years through channels Ryker's partner didn't know existed; the "Chorus," a data-relay device the coalition operative carries that lets Ryker actually converse with a drained universe's council rather than just fighting or listening to exposition.

## Act One — What Ryker Doesn't Know He's Walking Into

### Prologue & Arrival (as bible-established)

FMA HQ recall, medical, armory. Ryker's new partner for this op is **Auditor-liaison Priya OKonkwo** — younger than his usual handlers, meticulous, and (this matters later) not from the Enforcement track. She's Compliance. Ryker doesn't think about what that means yet. Portal to the outpost, triangulation to the slum reactor, cloaked flight to Northgate, thugs, train downtown, the checkpoint, the mother and her missing son, the cordoned reactor, the dead scientist's datacube naming Harmon.

Nothing here departs from canon. The departure starts in what Ryker notices at the reactor site that the bible doesn't script: **three different points of forced entry**, not one. The scorch pattern at the aperture site is consistent with a single violent breach — but there are second and third sets of scuffed boot prints in the ash outside the cordon, arriving separately, hours apart, neither matching Nuradyne security tread patterns. Someone else came to look at this reactor before Ryker did.

OKonkwo's briefing voice in Ryker's ear is drier than his usual handlers', more precise, always asking him to describe rather than conclude — "just tell me what you're seeing, not what you think it means" — a tic Ryker files away without weight. It reads as procedural fastidiousness. It's actually a Compliance auditor building a record she can defend later, to people Ryker hasn't met.

### Mission 1 — Nuradyne HQ, Finding Harmon

Bible-standard: Harmon stonewalls, rehearsed answers, dismisses the datacube. New material: on the way out, Ryker can read an internal Nuradyne memo (terminal in the Intelligence wing, basement per `Locations.md`) flagging "repeat unauthorized access, Sector 4 reactor housing, non-Nuradyne personnel, twice in six weeks, prior to incident." Security wrote it off as slum scavengers. It wasn't. This is Ryker's first hint that someone has been watching the reactor from the other side for a while — not just the operative who blew it, but scouts, before the blast.

### Mission 2 — The Missing Kid

Bible-standard beats: Ryker finds the boy, Elias, in an unlogged Nuradyne holding cell. New material in what Elias overheard: not just "something he shouldn't have" in the abstract. Guards outside his cell were arguing about "the second one" — not the operative who caused the explosion, a *different* intruder, caught and quietly let go because Voight herself ordered it, off the books, no report filed. Elias doesn't know what any of it means. Ryker does, eventually. This is the seed for Act Two: Voight has already made unauthorized contact with someone from the other side, and she's hiding it from Harmon.

Elias is returned to his mother (or not, depending on player choice — she can be found at the relocation warehouse; failing to reunite them is possible and consequential, not blocked). Reunited or not, this is the moment the game establishes its emotional floor: whatever Ryker learns about universes and councils and drain-rate tables in the acts ahead, it stays measured against one kid in a cell and a mother arguing with a guard, so the geopolitics never gets to float free of the people paying for it.

## Act Two — First Contact

### Mission 3 — The Second Set of Footprints

Following the boot-print lead (multitool into a maintenance sub-level Nuradyne sealed off, not logged on any Voight-approved cordon map) Ryker finds a **slum squat converted into a hide**: thermal blankets, ration wrappers with no Quinmore packaging, and a device — fist-sized, warm to the touch, humming faintly even powered down. This is the **Chorus**, though Ryker doesn't know the name yet.

Reading a computer here (a scavenged Nuradyne terminal the squatter jury-rigged) triggers the bible's designed hook: an incoming infolink, not from OKonkwo, but garbled, off-protocol, on a frequency the FMA doesn't use. It resolves into a single sentence, translated roughly: *"We are not the ones who broke your reactor. We would like to speak with whoever finds this."*

Ryker's actual first-contact conversation happens here, in-person, verb-legal (Ryker enters the squat; a figure is present or arrives, triggering dialogue): **Kestrel**, an envoy, not a soldier — no force-field armor, no violence, unarmed but for the Chorus. Kestrel's universe (they call it **the Ledger**, though the Chorus's translation is approximate) has been drained by Nuradyne's reactor for eleven years subjective, slow enough that they had time to trace it, study it, and organize a response instead of a strike. Kestrel is here to negotiate a stop to the siphon, not avenge it. They know about the operative who caused the explosion — a different, harder faction from a different universe entirely — and they're afraid of being blamed for that operative's methods before anyone will listen to their own message.

**First strong beat of the emphasis:** the player learns, from Kestrel directly, that Nuradyne's reactor network is drawing from *multiple* universes simultaneously, at different rates, and those universes have — independently, over years — detected each other's distress signals bleeding through the same aperture architecture and begun talking to each other before any of them talked to U-7134. There is, in effect, an alliance already, on the other side of the wormhole, that formed in response to being drained. The Ledger is its diplomatic wing.

Kestrel doesn't perform grief for Ryker's benefit, and doesn't perform hope either — through the Chorus their register comes across closer to a field report than a plea. "We counted the drain before we understood the cause. When we understood the cause, we counted the others being drawn on the same lines. We are not the first delegation your world has met. We intend to be the last one that has to come armed with anything but a translator." Ryker, true to his low-affect baseline, doesn't offer reassurance he can't back — he asks what the Ledger actually wants, concretely, and Kestrel's answer ("the draw to stop, not restitution, not apology — the draw to stop") becomes the plain, achievable-sounding ask the rest of the story keeps testing against how hard that actually is to deliver.

### Mission 4 — Voight's Secret

Armed with what Elias overheard and what Kestrel just told him, Ryker pursues Voight. This mission plays multiple ways (stealth into her private office in the Security Division upper floor per `Locations.md`, social engineering a meeting, or direct confrontation). What Ryker finds/extracts: Voight has known about the Ledger delegation for weeks. She let "the second one" — a Ledger scout, not Kestrel — go free after capturing them, in exchange for information: proof the reactor's true nature, corroborating what she already suspected from watching Harmon bury the abnormal-readings reports. Voight isn't a villain here in the simple sense — she's Nuradyne's security chief slowly realizing her own company is committing something closer to genocide-by-utility-bill, and she has no clean way to blow the whistle without being disappeared by her own board seat's legal cover.

Voight becomes a conditional ally. She won't defect publicly. She will feed Ryker access, if Ryker doesn't burn her. Her line to Ryker, if he confronts her directly rather than extracting the information covertly, is the closest the story gets to a corporate character explaining herself without a villain monologue: "I run security for a company that's been quietly killing other worlds to keep the lights on in this one, and I found that out the same way you did — by noticing the paperwork didn't add up. I don't get to resign. I get to decide how much damage I do on the way down." It's not absolution. It's the story refusing to let her be simple.

### Mission 5 — The War Faction

Kestrel warns Ryker: the Ledger isn't the only delegation in Quinmore. The operative who blew the reactor came from a different universe — Kestrel calls them, translated bluntly, **the Cinder Compact** — a coalition of survivors from a universe drained almost to total collapse before they even identified the cause. By the time they traced it, deliberation was a luxury they'd already lost the resources for. They don't want a treaty. They sent Sena Voss-Ferrant, their most capable operative, force-shielded, to do exactly what she did: force the aperture, hit the reactor hard enough that Quinmore would feel real cost, and vanish into the population to keep hitting it.

Ryker tracks Sena through the flooded slum sector (toxic-air constraints apply: timed sections, filtered gear, Atlas emergency equipment as a resource). This is the bible's "operative fled into the slums" thread, paid off directly. When Ryker finds her, the encounter is playable multiple ways — she will fight if cornered, but she will also *talk*, briefly, if Ryker approaches without a weapon raised and uses the Chorus (recovered from Kestrel's hideout, now functioning as translator). This is the second major in-verb conversation with a drained-universe character, and it recontextualizes her from "the bomber" the bible names her as, to a soldier from a world with nothing left to lose, sent by a council that voted, correctly by their own arithmetic, that one more dead reactor technician was an acceptable cost against the alternative of quietly dying unheard.

Sena doesn't surrender or die here by default — she escapes again unless the player commits to lethal force, giving her recurring presence through Act Three, and this is a deliberate branch point: kill her now and the Cinder Compact escalates faster later; let her go and she resurfaces as a wildcard in the endings.

What she says, if Ryker talks instead of shoots, is deliberately unpolished — the Chorus's translation of her is choppier than Kestrel's, more clipped, a soldier's economy rather than a diplomat's: "Your reactor took eleven years off a world with maybe forty left in it when we found the leak. We didn't have eleven years to send someone polite. You get to be angry that I killed your security guards. I don't get to be anything but glad my daughter has forty fewer years of this to live through than she would have." Ryker has no verb in his toolkit that fixes that sentence. The design intent is that he isn't supposed to have one yet — the Compact's grievance is meant to sit in the player's gut unresolved until whatever the chosen ending does or doesn't do about it.

## The First Twist — The FMA Already Has a Doctrine For This

### Mission 6 — What OKonkwo Was Really Sent to Audit

Ryker reports the existence of not one but (at least) two drained-universe delegations operating inside Quinmore to OKonkwo over infolink. Her reaction is wrong for someone hearing this for the first time. She's too fast with follow-up questions that presuppose structure — "which faction is Kestrel's, the Ledger or the Waystation remnant" — a name Ryker never gave her.

Confronting her (infolink conversation, then in person when Ryker's recalled briefly to the outpost, or she portals in — a scripted, verb-legal conversation) unspools the FMA's actual hidden agenda, distinct from any of run 1's five twists:

The FMA's founding charter, drafted generations ago, was co-written with input from **an early drained universe** — one whose energy-siphon crisis predates U-7134's by decades and was in fact one of the founding case studies that got siphon-based extraction classified, on paper, as a Class-1 inter-universal atrocity. The FMA has *always* had a doctrine for exactly this situation. What it has never had is the political will to enforce it against a member universe with resources, because enforcement means embargoing or quarantining an entire Earth's worth of people for a crime committed by a handful of executives who will simply outlive the embargo in comfort. So the doctrine sits, technically active, functionally dead letter — and OKonkwo's actual assignment, kept from Ryker (and from his usual Enforcement-track handlers) by her Compliance-division superiors, is to build a paper trail solid enough to force the Council to invoke it for the first time in FMA history. Ryker isn't just investigating an anomaly. He's been used, without consent, to gather evidence for an internal FMA political fight that predates the mission by two years.

This is the twist that satisfies the "FMA has a hidden agenda" invitation, distinct from run 1's Licensing (cartel) and Null Suppressor (Ryker-as-product) twists: **the agenda isn't sinister toward Ryker or toward U-7134** — it's institutional cowardice given a name. The FMA has known the moral status of siphon-drain for longer than it's had a Ryker to send, and has done nothing, repeatedly, across however many prior incidents at other reactors on other worlds, because doing something is expensive and slow-moving bureaucracies default to not-doing.

OKonkwo doesn't frame it as a confession so much as a correction of Ryker's assumptions, delivered with the same procedural calm she's used all mission: "You've been treating this as a first contact. It's the third one on record and the first one anybody bothered giving a field agent enough rope to actually solve instead of just quarantine. I didn't lie to you about your objectives. I just didn't tell you there was a vote riding on whether you completed them." Ryker's available responses here range from cold professional acceptance ("Then let's give you your vote") to real anger at being used as evidence without consent — both verb-legal, both consequential for whether OKonkwo is trusted, exposed, or cut loose in the endgame.

Ryker can push OKonkwo on how many "prior incidents" — she names two, both quietly resolved by the FMA paying off/relocating the drained universe's council rather than sanctioning the perpetrator universe. Quinmore would be the third. This seeds distrust: is OKonkwo here to help Ryker stop Nuradyne, or to help her own faction win an internal FMA vote, with Quinmore as evidence rather than as a people to save?

## The Second Twist — The Fourth Voice

### Mission 7 — The Aire Gardens Descent

Using `Ideas.md`'s planned level (Aire Gardens multiport, elevator down to the subway), Ryker follows a lead from Voight: Nuradyne has a secondary, unofficial aperture-monitoring station beneath the transit hub, built off-book by Harmon's team specifically to watch for exactly the kind of contact Ryker's now making. It's here, deep in the subway-level station, that Ryker intercepts the actual raw feed the FMA's original "faint transversal anomaly" sensor reading came from.

It isn't the Cinder Compact's forced breach. It isn't the Ledger's diplomatic signal. It's a third, much older, much weaker bleed-through — intermittent, badly degraded, coming from a universe so thoroughly drained that its own physics are failing at the aperture end. The Chorus can barely parse it. What comes through, fragment by fragment across this mission and the next, is not a message from a council or a delegation. It's closer to a distress beacon left running by people who may no longer be alive to hear a reply — **a universe called, in the best translation the Chorus can manage, the Waystation**, referenced obliquely by Kestrel back in Mission 3 as "the remnant," which Ryker had assumed meant destroyed-and-gone, not destroyed-and-still-technically-transmitting.

**This is the emphasis's central new twist, distinct from anything in run 1:** the FMA's "faint, erratic" anomaly reading was never primarily the Cinder Compact's violent breach (that was loud, unambiguous, easy to source) — it was this fourth, dying signal, present for far longer, that the FMA's sensors barely registered because it's degrading, not intensifying. Nuradyne's reactor network has been bleeding the Waystation for longer than either Sena's or Kestrel's universe has been drained. The Waystation is running out of universe. The bureaucratic horror of it: Ryker's entire mission exists because the FMA's instruments are tuned to catch loud, deliberate signals like force-shielded incursions — and are nearly blind to the quiet, terminal kind. A universe can be killed slowly enough that the people meant to police exactly this crime never notice until it's a whisper.

Whether the Waystation is a warning of what the Ledger will become if talks fail, a rebuke of the FMA's negligence, or (in the ending branches below) something Ryker can still act on in time, is left to the endgame.

The design intent behind burying this on the subway level rather than announcing it as a headline reveal: the player should have to go looking, the same way Nuradyne's own instruments never went looking, and the discovery should feel like finding something that was never meant to be found rather than something the plot was building toward. Kestrel, told about the Waystation afterward, has no diplomatic language ready for it — the Chorus renders a long pause before anything translates at all, and what finally comes through is not policy but grief: "We thought we were the quiet ones. We didn't know there was a quieter one under us."

## Act Three — Three Delegations, One Reactor, No Clean Answer

### Mission 8 — The Cinder Compact Escalates

Sena (if alive) resurfaces, this time not alone — a second Cinder Compact operative has come through a fresh, smaller breach Harmon's team detects and is trying to contain quietly rather than report (fearing the CU board learning the reactors are compromised at all). The Compact's position hardens: negotiation got their universe nowhere for a decade; force got Quinmore's attention in a week. They intend to hit a second reactor, a live one still powering an inhabited dome sector — not a slum reactor this time, a downtown one, guaranteeing mass civilian casualties on the dome-failure model already established in canon (lose power, seals fail, toxic air floods in, minutes to evacuate).

Ryker must choose how to handle this in real time: stop the Compact's strike by force (siding, functionally, with Nuradyne's security response, alongside Voight); try to broker Kestrel and the Ledger into talking Sena's faction down (a social/diplomatic path using the Chorus, playable within converse-verb limits, difficult and not guaranteed); or let it play out and use the chaos as cover to hit Nuradyne's real target — the reactor control core — himself.

The diplomatic path is deliberately the hardest to pull off and the most narratively rewarding: it requires Ryker to have kept Kestrel's trust, kept Sena alive from Mission 5, and to physically bring the two of them into the same room with the Chorus running, betting that a soldier who's already spoken to him once will listen to a fellow drained-universe envoy before she'll listen to him. If it lands, Sena doesn't defect or apologize — she stands down "under protest, under review," a phrase the Chorus renders with what reads like bitter irony, and the Compact's second operative pulls back to observe rather than strike. It's a cease-fire, not a peace, and the story treats it that way going into Act Three's endgame.

### Mission 9 — Harmon's Ledger (Nuradyne's Real Files)

Whatever the previous mission's outcome, Ryker gets access (Voight's help, or forced entry, or Voight's death making her override codes usable posthumously) to Harmon's actual internal reports: not just "abnormal readings," but a **drain-rate table**, cataloguing every universe on the other end of Nuradyne's reactor network, current output percentage, estimated time-to-collapse. The Ledger (Kestrel's universe) is listed. The Compact's origin universe is listed, past 90%. And the Waystation is listed too — flagged, in Harmon's own annotation, "signal degrading, diminishing returns, scheduled for decommission and reallocation of draw to Sector 7," meaning: Nuradyne knows the Waystation is dying and its plan is to squeeze it for whatever's left, then move on to draining someone else more efficiently once it's fully spent.

This is the mission's gut-punch, playable as a straightforward read-a-computer beat that triggers Ryker's understanding, and Kestrel's reaction (infolink or in-person) when told: the Ledger already knew the Compact's universe was worse off than theirs. They did not know about the Waystation. Nobody outside Nuradyne did. Even the Compact didn't know.

This reframes every faction's information asymmetry: the drained universes have been fighting for the FMA and Ryker's attention without knowing there's a fourth party worse off than either of them, being quietly finished off in the margins of a spreadsheet.

### Mission 10 — The Board

Ryker gets one shot at the Corporate Union apparatus itself: a CU board session (Nuradyne's seat, contested by Atlas's rep, who has been suspecting a Nuradyne "power play" per canon and will act as a useful, self-interested lever) where the drain-rate table can be surfaced publicly, forcing the board to act, refuse to act, or bury it — depending on player choices banked across the previous missions (Voight's survival/cooperation, whether Harmon was killed/captured/exposed, whether the Compact's strike was stopped or allowed to land).

This mission is also where OKonkwo's thread resolves one way or another: she either gets the internal FMA evidence she needs regardless of what Ryker chooses to do with the board (meaning the FMA's own dormant Class-1 doctrine finally moves, off-page, as a consequence independent of Quinmore's politics), or Ryker denies her the clean case file by acting unilaterally before the board session concludes, leaving the FMA's institutional cowardice fully intact for whatever comes after U-7134. Either way, the story is explicit that the FMA fixing itself and Quinmore surviving intact are not the same victory, and the player doesn't get to assume winning one means winning the other.

## Factions & Power Structure

- **Nuradyne** — power monopoly, inner circle (Harmon + a handful of engineers) knows the siphon's true nature and its victim list; rank-and-file don't. Institutionally incentivized to keep squeezing every universe on the drain-rate table as long as the board doesn't ask hard questions.
- **Atlas Subsystems** — dome shielding, publicly blames Nuradyne, privately suspects a power play, useful as leverage against Nuradyne on the board but has zero interest in the multiverse angle — purely a corporate rival wanting Nuradyne's seat weakened.
- **The Corporate Union board** — collectively deniable, individually self-interested; will act only if acting is cheaper than not acting.
- **The slums / Elias's mother / the relocation warehouse residents** — the human cost, unchanged from canon, given weight by keeping their thread (the kid, the mother, the warehouse) fully resolved regardless of which multiversal ending fires, so the "human cost in the slums" throughline isn't lost to the geopolitics above it.
- **The FMA** — not monolithic. OKonkwo's Compliance faction wants enforcement of the dormant Class-1 doctrine; Ryker's usual Enforcement-track chain of command (represented by a recalled senior handler in the endgame) wants a contained, quiet resolution that avoids setting a precedent that could implicate the FMA's two prior buried incidents.
- **The Ledger** — Kestrel's universe, drained slowly, organized, diplomatic, willing to trade technical cooperation (they understand aperture physics Nuradyne doesn't) for a negotiated shutdown.
- **The Cinder Compact** — Sena's universe, drained fast and hard, out of patience, willing to escalate to force because force is the only language that's gotten U-7134's attention so far — and they're not wrong about that.
- **The Waystation** — barely a faction at all; a dying signal, no council to negotiate with directly, a moral weight rather than an active player, whose fate depends entirely on whether Ryker's endgame choice includes shutting down the reactor draw fast enough to matter.

## Key Characters

- **Liam Ryker** — as canon.
- **Priya OKonkwo** — FMA Compliance auditor, Ryker's partner, ethically convinced she's doing the right thing the slow, legal way; the player can end the game trusting her, breaking with her, or exposing her internal agenda to Ryker's Enforcement-track handler, each with consequences for the ending.
- **Kestrel** — Ledger envoy, diplomat, carries the Chorus, becomes Ryker's primary cross-verse ally if kept alive and trusted.
- **Sena Voss-Ferrant** — Cinder Compact operative, the bible's "operative," given full interiority: not a monster, a soldier from a world that voted to stop waiting.
- **Kira Voight** — Nuradyne Chief of Security, conditional ally, morally cornered by her own company, can defect, die, or entrench depending on how she's handled.
- **Director Harmon** — as canon, expanded: not a mustache-twirler, a man managing a spreadsheet of dying universes the way a utility manager manages brownouts, convinced the alternative (losing the domes' power) is worse.
- **Elias and his mother** — kept exactly as the bible's human-scale thread, resolved independently of the geopolitical endings.
- **The stranded FMA liaison** — findable in the slums (a new, short side-thread): broken, guilt-ridden, the direct cause of the entire chain of siphon technology existing; his knowledge, if extracted, explains *how* Harmon's team built a system that can drain multiple universes selectively (a targeting/throttling mechanism the liaison unknowingly helped design), which becomes the technical key to any ending that involves shutting the reactor down cleanly rather than catastrophically.

## The Central Twists, Named

1. **The FMA has always had a doctrine for this and has always flinched.** (Mission 6.) Not a conspiracy against Ryker — a decades-old institutional cowardice, made personal because OKonkwo is using Ryker's fieldwork, without his informed consent, as evidence in an internal FMA political fight he was never told he was part of. Delivered entirely through a converse-verb confrontation, no forbidden surveillance/hacking devices used.
2. **The dying fourth universe nobody, including the other drained universes, knew about.** (Mission 7–9.) The Waystation reframes the entire Ledger/Compact conflict as a fight over attention neither of them knew was being lost by default to a third party too faint to be noticed — delivered through the Aire Gardens/subway station intercept (a read-a-computer/device-interaction beat) and Harmon's drain-rate table (a read-a-computer beat triggering an ally infolink from Kestrel), both fully inside player-verb limits.

## Player-Choice Structure & Endings

Convergent variables banked across the game: Sena alive/dead; Voight ally/dead/hostile; Harmon exposed/killed/protected by the board; OKonkwo trusted/exposed/broken with; the drain-rate table surfaced publicly or not; whether the reactor's throttling mechanism (from the liaison) was recovered.

**Ending A — The Accord.** Kestrel's Ledger and a chastened Cinder Compact (only reachable if Sena survived and was talked down in Mission 8) are brought together with Nuradyne's throttling mechanism to negotiate a managed shutdown: Quinmore loses significant power capacity, rationed hard, but the siphon stops and the Waystation's remaining draw is redirected to recovery rather than further extraction. Costly, slow, morally clean-ish. The CU board treats it as a PR-manageable "energy transition." OKonkwo gets her enforcement precedent. Ryker becomes, unofficially, the first human envoy in FMA history to have negotiated rather than policed a multiversal incident.

**Ending B — The Compact's War.** If Sena was killed or the strike in Mission 8 wasn't stopped, the Cinder Compact escalates past any single operative — more breaches, more force, and Quinmore's board responds by militarizing the reactor district, turning it into an open, undeclared war zone. The FMA, unwilling to be seen enabling an inter-universal conflict on a member Earth's soil, orders Ryker to withdraw. The Waystation's fate is never resolved — its signal just stops, off-page, sometime after the credits. Bleakest ending; a genuine "walked away and let a world die of neglect" note, distinct from run 1's endings.

**Ending C — The Quiet Fix.** Ryker, distrusting both OKonkwo's institutional agenda and the political mess of a public accord, uses the liaison's knowledge to sabotage the reactor's siphon function directly and covertly — throttling it to zero without Nuradyne's, the board's, or even the FMA leadership's sign-off. Quinmore keeps its lights on at reduced capacity via emergency protocols; nobody outside a handful of people ever learns the multiverse was involved at all. The Ledger and the Waystation are saved from further draw; the Cinder Compact gets nothing they can point to as a won concession, and may still see it as a victory or as another erasure depending on player-facing epilogue text. Ryker burns his relationship with OKonkwo (he went around her case) and with the FMA's Enforcement track (he acted without sanction) — a personally isolating ending that best fits the "moral flexibility, just business" characterization the bible wants.

**Ending D — Exposure.** Only reachable with the drain-rate table surfaced at the CU board (Mission 10) and Voight alive to corroborate it publicly: the siphon's true nature becomes known, not to the general Quinmore population (too dangerous, too destabilizing, the bible's secrecy framing holds), but to the CU board and Atlas specifically, who use it to force Nuradyne's board seat into a humiliating restructuring — power generation reassigned, Harmon scapegoated and prosecuted for the terrorism cover-up rather than the actual crime nobody outside the room can be told about. A cynical, very Deus-Ex-flavored ending: justice arrives, but only the legible, corporate-politics-shaped kind, and the multiversal victims get a throttled reactor and no acknowledgment they ever existed.

## Themes

Attention as a moral resource — the Waystation's horror isn't malice, it's that dying quietly gets you drained faster than dying loudly. Institutions with the right doctrine and no will to use it. Diplomacy and war as two rational responses to the same unanswered signal, neither of them wrong given what each side knew. And the specific loneliness of being the first, and possibly only, point of contact a species gets — Ryker never asked to be an envoy, and nothing in his training prepared him to be one; the story is about a low-affect tactician made, against his design, into the only person three-going-on-four civilizations can currently talk to.
