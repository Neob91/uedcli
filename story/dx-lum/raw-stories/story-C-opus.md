# DX_LUM — The Man Who Sold the Sky

Writer: opus | Emphasis: the stranded liaison & Nuradyne's inner circle

---

## Logline

A covert multiverse agent chases a faint anomaly into a poisoned dome-city and finds no bomber, no enemy universe worth fearing — only a broken old man in the slums who once sold his own agency's secrets for a warm place to sleep, and the corporation that used those secrets to quietly murder worlds it will never see. Every reactor Quinmore runs on is a slow-motion genocide, the leak that made it possible traces back through the liaison to the FMA itself, and the FMA sent Ryker not to stop the siphon but to make sure no one else ever learns the FMA's own hand is in the wound.

**How this version reads:** a downward spiral built around one tragic informant. Ryker starts thinking he's hunting a terrorist, then a bomber's true target, then a corporate cover-up — and each layer peels back to the same shape: a man named Ezra Coyle, and the knowledge he leaked. The corporate-thriller surface (Harmon, Voight, suppressed readings) is real and fully playable, but it's the shell. The core is a chamber piece about complicity: Coyle's, Nuradyne's inner circle's, the FMA's, and finally Ryker's own, because it was the FMA's abandonment of Coyle that armed Nuradyne in the first place.

## What I take as fixed vs. what I invented

**Fixed (canon, kept intact):** Liam Ryker as low-affect ex-military blank slate; the FMA and its cloaked U-7134 outpost; the transversal anomaly from U-7134; the toxic-atmosphere / dome-sector world; the Corporate Union board and corporate jurisdiction law; Nuradyne (power) and Atlas Subsystems (shielding); the multiverse **energy-siphon** reactors and the inner-circle secret; the drained universe that traced the siphon and sent a force-field **operative** who blew the slum reactor, killed security, and fled; the terrorist framing, cordon, checkpoints, and relocation warehouse; the **stranded liaison** who traded FMA/multiverse knowledge to Nuradyne, was cut loose, and now rots broken in the slums (FMA thinks him dead); **Director Harmon** (reactor project head, stonewaller, suppressed the abnormal readings); **Kira Voight** (Nuradyne Chief of Security); the FMA **partner** at the outpost feeding objectives by infolink; the **slum mother** and her **missing son** detained by Nuradyne after overhearing something; the datacube naming Harmon and "abnormal reactor readings"; the prologue beats; the Aire Gardens station/elevator/subway idea; the player-verb constraints; androids as non-revolting flavor.

**Invented (gap-filling):** the liaison's name and history — **Ezra Coyle**; the missing kid **Toby Marsh** and his mother **Della**; Nuradyne's inner circle beyond Harmon and Voight — **CEO Adrienne Sable**, reactor-physics lead **Dr. Priya Anand**, and the dead scientist of the datacube, **Marcus Feld**; the FMA partner **Nadia Okonkwo** and her handler back home, **Deputy Director Sarn**; the operative's name-in-translation **"Vessel-9"** and the drained universe's designation **U-7134's shadow, catalogued by the FMA as U-2209**; the FMA's hidden **Ledger Protocol**; the slum fixer **Mother Sorrel** and the syndicate **the Hollow**; Atlas's on-site liaison **Sara Kwon**; the specific reveal chain, twists, and endings. All within canon's stated shapes.

## Factions & power structure

**Nuradyne (power generation).** Owns the grid, therefore owns the board. Its inner circle — CEO **Adrienne Sable**, reactor-project director **Harmon**, chief of security **Voight**, and physics lead **Dr. Priya Anand** — are the only four people alive in U-7134 who know the reactors siphon from other universes. Everyone below them thinks the reactors are exotic clean fusion. Nuradyne's agenda: keep the secret, keep the grid, keep the seat. The siphon is not a means to an end for them; the grid *is* the end, and other universes are an externality they've chosen not to price.

**Atlas Subsystems (dome shielding).** Depends on Nuradyne's power, resents it, and suspects the slum blast was a Nuradyne power play — a manufactured crisis to justify emergency grid centralization. Atlas's on-site rep **Sara Kwon** will feed Ryker real intelligence if he gives her leverage against Nuradyne. Atlas doesn't know about the multiverse; it just smells a corporate murder and wants the board seat that would follow proving it.

**The Corporate Union board.** No civilian government. Approved the reactors as "cutting-edge energy tech," never told the truth. The board's agenda is stability and dividends; it will bury anything that threatens the grid, including the truth, if telling it means the domes go dark.

**The slums / the Hollow.** The cordoned district's displaced, plus a smuggling syndicate (**the Hollow**) that runs multitools, filter masks, black-market clearance chips, and people through the checkpoints Nuradyne can't fully close. Fixer **Mother Sorrel** knows where the broken men hide — including Coyle. Agenda: survive, profit, and settle scores with the corp that just gassed their neighborhood.

**The FMA (Ryker's employer).** Polices inter-universe travel and crime. Sent Ryker on a covert investigation of a faint anomaly. Its *stated* agenda: assess the threat. Its *real* agenda (the hidden-agenda twist): contain the leak of its own secrets — because the anomaly's source is knowledge the FMA itself let escape when it abandoned Ezra Coyle.

**U-2209, the drained universe.** No dome-city; a dying world that felt its stars go cold as Nuradyne fed. It has crude multiverse tech — enough to trace the drain and punch one operative, **Vessel-9**, through the aperture before it closed. Vessel-9 is not a terrorist. It is a survivor of a murdered world sent to kill the murder weapon.

## Key characters

- **Liam Ryker** — the player. Ex-military, morally flexible, no cover, no contacts. Learns the world as the player does.
- **Ezra Coyle** — the stranded FMA liaison, ~sixty, ruined. The pivot of the whole story. Sold the multiverse to Nuradyne to stay alive; watched them turn it into a slaughterhouse; got discarded when the engineers no longer needed him. Terrified, guilt-rotted, self-medicating, and the single human being in U-7134 who understands exactly what the reactors do. Ambiguous to the end: victim, traitor, only possible witness, and — in one reading — the true architect of every death.
- **Nadia Okonkwo** — Ryker's FMA partner at the outpost. Warm, competent, feeds objectives by infolink. Loyal to Ryker. Does not know the Ledger Protocol; becomes Ryker's crosscheck on his own agency.
- **Deputy Director Sarn** — Nadia's handler back home, and the story's cold hand. Author of the Ledger Protocol.
- **Adrienne Sable** — Nuradyne CEO. Elegant, unbothered, the intelligence behind the cover-up. Treats genocide as a cost line.
- **Director Harmon** — reactor-project head. Not a monster by temperament; a manager who saw the abnormal readings, was told to bury them, and did, because the alternative was his own destruction. The datacube names him. He is the crack in the wall.
- **Kira Voight** — Nuradyne Chief of Security. Ran the cordon, the checkpoints, the frame-up. Competent, ruthless, and the one hunting Vessel-9 in parallel with Ryker. She detained Toby.
- **Dr. Priya Anand** — reactor physicist, inner circle. The one who actually understands the siphon and the death toll, and the one whose conscience is finally cracking.
- **Marcus Feld** — dead scientist, author of the datacube. Killed for raising the readings.
- **Della Marsh & Toby Marsh** — slum mother and her missing son. Toby overheard the inner circle discussing "the other side" while detained.
- **Sara Kwon** — Atlas rep, opportunist ally.
- **Mother Sorrel** — Hollow fixer, keeper of the slum's broken men.
- **Vessel-9** — the U-2209 operative. Force-field survivor of a drained world.

## ACT ONE — The Anomaly

### Mission 1 — Prologue: FMA Headquarters *(canon)*

Ryker is recalled from leave. A low-level clerk routes him to the briefing room, where a duty officer lays out the mission: a **faint, unprecedented transversal anomaly** from U-7134, barely detectable, intent unknown. Standard practice is a diplomatic representative; this one gets a covert agent because the signal is too strange to trust. Medical clears him for toxic-atmosphere ops. The armory kits him for underground infiltration. Optional orientation side-quests introduce the infolink, multitools, hacking, and the kill/incapacitate verbs. **Playable seed for later:** in a records annex the player *can* read a terminal — an archival FMA personnel file, most fields redacted, flagged **STATUS: PRESUMED DECEASED (U-7134, war loss)**, name withheld. Reading it triggers a one-line infolink from Nadia: *"Old ghost. Ignore it — nobody's been live in 7134 for thirty years."* The player forgets it. It's Coyle.

### Mission 2 — The Outpost *(canon)*

Ryker portals to the cloaked U-7134 bunker with **Nadia Okonkwo**. Sensors pick up residual multiversal energy; Nadia triangulates it to a **reactor in Quinmore's slum district**. She stays behind to research; he takes the cloaked aircraft to **Northgate**. In Northgate — thugs (Hollow muscle testing a stranger) give the first combat/stealth/social fork. Ryker trains downtown. First look at Quinmore: not futuristic, weathered, corporate logos stamped over an old American town under glass.

### Mission 3 — The Cordon *(canon)*

The slum reactor has already **exploded**. Dome shielding is dead, the sector's flooded with toxic air, and **Nuradyne Security** has cordoned it with checkpoints, publicly hunting "the bomber." Evacuees funnel through screening into a relocation warehouse. At the slum-district entrance, **Della Marsh** argues with a Nuradyne officer about her missing son; the officer waves her off and blocks Ryker, who claims clearance (forged / hacked / talked). Della catches his sleeve: *find my boy.* First objective branch established: get inside.

Three infiltration routes, player's pick:
- **Forge** — the Hollow (Mother Sorrel) sells a Nuradyne clearance chip for a favor owed later.
- **Hack** — a checkpoint terminal, multitool, and a spoofed credential.
- **Social** — impersonate a CU safety inspector; Nuradyne can't refuse a board audit without looking guilty.

### Mission 4 — The Reactor Site *(canon)*

Inside the dead sector, filter mask on, Ryker reaches the blast crater. **Logs erased, security dead.** Nuradyne's public line is bewilderment; their real behavior is a manhunt. In a vent by the body of **Marcus Feld**, Ryker finds the **datacube** *(canon text)*: security doubled, comms cut, *"Reactor readings are way outside normal operating parameters. Reported it to Director Harmon, told me to forget about it."* Two leads: **Harmon**, and the **abnormal readings**. Reading Feld's still-powered console triggers Nadia's infolink: the residual energy signature here isn't a travel wake — it's a *drain* wake, sustained over years. She's confused; travel is a spike, this is a river. First crack in the "it was a bomber" story: something here has been running a long time.

## ACT TWO — The Cover-Up

### Mission 5 — Nuradyne HQ: Harmon *(canon + escalation)*

Ryker reaches Nuradyne HQ (Locations.md layout: basement labs/armory/forensics; upper floors CEO/offices/security/intel). **Harmon** is at his desk — staying visible is his alibi. He stonewalls, gives rehearsed answers, dismisses the datacube as a paranoid tech's nerves. But the gap between what Harmon says and what he *won't* say is loud. If Ryker reads Harmon's office terminal (multitool the drawer safe or shoulder-surf a password from a sticky prompt via social route), he finds **an internal memo he was cc'd on and then ordered to delete** — an incident-response draft referencing *"non-terrestrial reactor feedback, prior occurrences, containment sufficient"* signed off up the chain. Reading it triggers Nadia: the phrasing is FMA-adjacent. Where would a U-7134 corp learn to phrase multiversal feedback in *our* vocabulary? Objective: find who taught Nuradyne to speak this language.

### Mission 6 — The Missing Kid *(canon)*

Mid-investigation, in a Nuradyne holding wing (basement or a satellite facility), Ryker finds **Toby Marsh** in an unlogged cell — grabbed for being in the sealed district. Toby overheard guards and, through a wall, two executives arguing: about *"the other side,"* about *"how many"* and *"we can't turn it off without the whole grid going with it,"* and a woman's voice saying *"then it stays on."* Toby doesn't understand it. Ryker does — barely. This is the first direct confirmation that Nuradyne's leadership knows the reactors reach *somewhere else* and that turning them off is unthinkable to them.

**Branch:** Ryker can free Toby now (combat/stealth) and lose the thread of who he overheard, or plant a way for Toby to stay put and *keep* listening (colder, ends-justify-means), or extract Toby *and* pull the cell-block duty log to identify the two voices. Freeing Toby and returning him to Della opens a slum ally network (Della talks to Mother Sorrel on Ryker's behalf). The duty log names a private sub-level meeting attended by **Sable, Harmon, Voight, and Anand** — the inner circle, now a face list.

### Mission 7 — Atlas, and the First Real Theory *(invented, canon-consistent)*

**Sara Kwon** of Atlas contacts Ryker (she's been watching the "CU inspector" or heard through the Hollow). Atlas is furious: their shielding didn't merely fail, the *backup* fried too, which shouldn't be possible from a single blast unless the surge came *through the power feed* — through Nuradyne's own line. Atlas's theory: Nuradyne staged or exploited the incident. Kwon trades Ryker a decrypted fragment of the reactor's pre-blast telemetry (Atlas monitors the feed for load) in exchange for anything that implicates Nuradyne on the board. The telemetry shows the reactor's output **inverting** in the seconds before the blast — for an instant it was *pulling*, hard, from the reactor side, not pushing power out. Nadia, hearing this: that's not an explosion signature. That's an **aperture forced open from the far side.** Something came *through.*

This reframes the whole case for the player: not a bomber Nuradyne is hiding, but an *intruder* Nuradyne is hunting — and the reactor is a door.

### Mission 8 — Voight's Manhunt & Vessel-9 *(invented)*

Ryker keeps crossing Voight's search grid. Voight isn't looking for a terrorist; she's looking for something that walks through walls and doesn't breathe the air. Ryker can shadow the manhunt (stealth) or bait it (social: pose as a security contractor Voight's desperate enough to use). Either way he reaches **Vessel-9** before Voight does — cornered in the flooded sector, force-field flickering, dying slowly because even that field can't keep U-7134's air out forever.

Vessel-9 doesn't speak Ryker's language cleanly; communication is fragmentary, via an infolink translation Nadia patches through. What comes across: *your world eats mine. the lights went out one at a time. we followed the theft home. i am the last thing we could afford to send.* Vessel-9 didn't come to bomb a slum — it came to reach the reactor core and collapse the aperture from this side, permanently. The blast that flooded the sector was the aperture tearing, not a chosen atrocity.

**Branch (major):** Ryker can kill Vessel-9 (it's what Voight wants; "just business," and it clears his path with Nuradyne), incapacitate and hide it, or protect it and let it try to finish its mission. Vessel-9, dying, gives Ryker the thing that breaks the case open: it can *feel* the drain, and the drain didn't start with Nuradyne's science. It started when *"one of yours"* handed them the key. *"a man who smelled like your machines. still alive. below."* Below = the slums. One of Ryker's own. Objective: find the man the FMA left behind.

## ACT THREE — The Man in the Slums

### Mission 9 — Finding Ezra Coyle *(the pivot)*

Through Mother Sorrel (paid via the Toby favor, or the Hollow debt from Mission 3), Ryker is led to a flophouse in the un-cordoned edge of the slums, where the Hollow quietly shelters the city's discarded — including a man they call "the Professor," who talks to himself about doors and other skies and whom nobody believes.

**Ezra Coyle.** The stranded FMA liaison. He recognizes the FMA-issue tells on Ryker before Ryker says a word and nearly collapses — thirty years of terror, half of it that Nuradyne would finally kill him, half of it that the FMA would finally *find* him. This is the story's centerpiece scene, and it's all conversation (a legal player verb): a long, branching interrogation where Coyle, drunk and terrified and desperate to finally be *heard*, gives Ryker the entire truth, or as much as Ryker digs for.

What Coyle reveals:
- He was the FMA's pre-war liaison. The war killed his government contacts; the FMA went dark and never came back. He was stranded, no portal, breathing borrowed air.
- To survive, he sold Nuradyne what he knew of FMA multiversal theory — not travel (he couldn't build a portal), but the *principle* of tapping the energy gradient between universes. He told himself they'd eventually build a portal and send him home. **They never wanted the door. They wanted the current.** They built the siphon.
- He watched the readings. He *knew* what a drain does to the far side — he'd read the FMA's own ethics briefings on it decades ago. He told Harmon, early, once, what the abnormal readings meant: *another world is dying, and it's dying because of us.* Harmon took it up the chain. Sable's ruling: **the grid stays on.** Coyle was cut loose the same quarter — no longer needed, too much paperwork to kill, unbelievable if he talked.
- **The twist he doesn't realize he's delivering:** the vocabulary in Harmon's memo, the "prior occurrences, containment sufficient" — that's Coyle's language, FMA language, because Coyle taught it to them. Every death in U-2209 runs back through this ruined old man to a leak the FMA created by leaving him here.

Coyle has one hard asset: an old **Nuradyne inner-circle datacache** he smuggled out when they revoked his clearance — encrypted, but it's the reactor project's true design docs and the *suppressed reading logs*, including the ones proving Nuradyne's leadership knew the drain was fatal to the far side and chose to keep pulling. Proof that turns "energy tech" into premeditated multiversal genocide. He never used it — no one to give it to, and giving it to anyone got him killed. He gives it to Ryker. Or tries to.

### Mission 10 — The Vault of Suppressed Readings *(invented, canon-anchored to "suppressed reports")*

Coyle's cache is real but incomplete — it's keyed to a live Nuradyne credential he no longer has, and decrypting it needs a matching key from the inner circle. Two playable paths, both legal-verb:
- **Anand.** Ryker approaches **Dr. Priya Anand**, the physicist whose conscience is failing. She's the softest of the four. Through conversation (and by showing her Vessel-9's existence or Toby's testimony or the Atlas telemetry — whatever Ryker collected), Ryker can turn her: she gives him her key and corroborates the death toll on record. She becomes a witness.
- **Harmon.** Or Ryker breaks **Harmon** — confronts him with the datacube, the memo, the manhunt, the child in the cell. Harmon isn't loyal; he's afraid. He gives up his key to buy his own survival, insisting he *reported* it, he *told* them, he only followed the ruling. (He is telling the truth, and it damns him anyway.)

Decrypted, the cache is the smoking gun: **years of reading logs Nuradyne buried, showing the inner circle knew each siphon cycle was extinguishing life in a drained universe, and formally decided to continue.** Reading it on any networked terminal triggers Nadia's infolink — and this is where the *second* floor drops out.

### Mission 11 — The Ledger Protocol *(the FMA hidden-agenda twist)*

Nadia goes quiet after Ryker transmits the cache. Then a *different* voice comes over the FMA infolink: **Deputy Director Sarn**. Warm, reasonable, and instructing Ryker to secure the cache, secure Coyle, and **erase the reactor site's residual signature** — and to bring Coyle in *alive and silent,* or ensure he never talks. New standing objective: **contain the leak.**

The twist, assembled from things the player has already legally touched (the redacted PRESUMED DECEASED file in the prologue; Nadia's earlier confusion; Sarn's word choices):

The FMA didn't send Ryker to *stop* the siphon or save U-2209. The FMA runs a quiet doctrine — the **Ledger Protocol** — for exactly this failure: a leak of FMA multiversal knowledge into a naive universe. Under the Ledger, the priority is not the drained world's lives; it's **deniability**. If it ever became known across the federation that an FMA liaison's abandonment armed a genocide, the FMA's legitimacy — its whole claim to *police* the multiverse — collapses. The anomaly investigation was a **containment op wearing an investigation's clothes.** Ryker was picked *because* he's an outsider with no contacts and no cover: a man who can be disowned, whose findings can be sealed, who was never officially here.

Nadia — who was never read into the Ledger — realizes it live, on the same channel, and quietly sides with Ryker: *"They're not going to shut it off. They're going to make sure nobody ever knew it was on."*

Now every faction's true agenda is on the table:
- **Nuradyne** wants the grid and the secret; kill Coyle, kill Vessel-9, kill Ryker.
- **The FMA (Sarn)** wants the leak erased; the drained world is an acceptable loss, and Coyle is the loose thread — the man who proves it was *their* leak.
- **Coyle** wants, finally, for it to be *known,* whatever it costs him.
- **Vessel-9 / U-2209** wants the reactor dead.
- **Atlas / the board** want Nuradyne's seat and have no idea any of the above exists.

### Mission 12 — Aire Gardens & the Core *(canon location, climax staging)*

The finale runs through the **Aire Gardens** luxury station (Ideas.md): Ryker arrives up top (chopper landing area), and the **elevator/inclinator** descends — past the obscene luxury advertising of a dying world, then out of the dome and down the shaft with the poisoned city visible through the glass — to the **subway**, and along the maintenance spine to the **primary reactor core** (not the blown slum unit — the main one, still siphoning, still killing). Voight's security, Nuradyne's manhunt, and the FMA's containment window all converge here. Coyle can be brought along (fragile, a liability, the only voice who can *narrate* to the world what the core is) or left in the slums (safer for him, weaker as proof).

At the core, the choices resolve.

## The central twists (called out)

1. **The bomber is a survivor; the reactor is a door.** The "terrorist attack" is Nuradyne's cover for an aperture forced open from a *murdered universe* by its last operative. Delivered playably via Atlas's telemetry (invert/pull signature) + the conversation with Vessel-9. *(Siphon-horror twist.)*

2. **The genocide was armed by the FMA's own abandoned liaison — and Nuradyne even speaks in FMA vocabulary because of him.** Coyle isn't a side victim; he is the causal root. The suppressed readings prove the inner circle *chose* to keep killing worlds. Delivered via the Coyle conversation + the decrypted cache. *(Liaison / inner-circle cover-up twist — the emphasis.)*

3. **The FMA's investigation is a containment op; Ryker is the disposable cutout; the Ledger Protocol values deniability over the drained world's lives.** The FMA would rather erase the evidence — including Coyle — than admit its leak caused a multiversal genocide. Delivered via Sarn's infolink takeover, foreshadowed by the prologue's redacted file and Nadia's dawning realization. *(FMA-hidden-agenda twist.)*

4. **The quiet gut-punch:** Coyle told them *once,* at the start, that the far side was dying — and kept the receipt for thirty years without ever being able to use it. Harmon *reported* it and buried it anyway. The horror isn't that no one knew. It's that everyone who mattered knew early, and the machine ran because stopping it was more expensive than a world.

All four are reachable through the legal verbs: **conversations** (Coyle, Anand/Harmon, Vessel-9, Kwon, Toby), **reading computers** (prologue file, Harmon's memo, the cache — each triggering an ally infolink and the next objective), **incoming infolink** (Nadia, then Sarn), and **kill/incapacitate/multitool** for access. No surveillance-tape review, no timestamp-forensics — the readings and telemetry are handed over by NPCs (Kwon, Anand, Coyle) or read as documents.

## Player-choice structure & endings

Choices accumulate across the game — how Ryker treats Vessel-9, whether Toby and Della are helped, whether he turns Anand or breaks Harmon, whether Coyle lives to the core, and whether he obeys Sarn. At the core, four ideological endings, Deus Ex style, each a stance on *who gets to know and who gets to decide.*

**Ending A — Contain (obey the FMA).**
Ryker completes the Ledger Protocol. He erases the residual signature, seals the cache, and silences Coyle — turned over to FMA custody (a quiet death, or a memory-wipe; the game leaves the mercy ambiguous). Vessel-9 dies or is disposed of; U-2209 keeps dying, unrecorded. Nuradyne, warned off by the FMA to keep the peace, tightens its secret. The domes stay lit. Ryker goes home decorated, having protected the *institution* that polices the multiverse — at the price of becoming exactly the thing it pretends to prevent. *Order preserved by erasing the crime.* Coyle's last line, if he's present: *"You're the second FMA man to leave me here. At least you knew my name."*

**Ending B — Expose (side with Coyle / the truth).**
Ryker gets the decrypted cache and Coyle's testimony out — to Atlas and the CU board, or broadcast wide via the Hollow's networks. The siphon becomes public. The board, facing proof the grid runs on genocide, is forced to choose in the open; the FMA's hand is exposed to its own federation. But truth has a body count: if the reactors go dark, the domes fail and Quinmore chokes. The ending forks on Ryker's earlier prep — did he arm Atlas/the Hollow to build alternative shielding/power capacity, or is exposure a bomb with no landing? Coyle, if alive, finally *witnesses* on the record — and it is the first time in thirty years he is believed. *Truth at the cost of stability, and possibly the city.*

**Ending C — Collapse the door (side with Vessel-9 / U-2209).**
Ryker helps Vessel-9 (or finishes its mission himself) and collapses the aperture at the primary core — permanently severing the siphon. U-2209 stops dying. But the main reactor is Quinmore's spine: the domes go dark on a timer. The ending becomes an evacuation — how many sectors Ryker's allies (Atlas, Sorrel, Anand) can carry to safety depends on the groundwork he laid. A hard utilitarian trade: *one dying world saved, one living city gambled.* Coyle can choose to stay at the core and hold the collapse stable with his own understanding of the machine — the informant's final, chosen act of atonement, dying to close the wound he opened.

**Ending D — Take the board seat (become the new inner circle).**
The "morally flexible" ending. Ryker uses the cache not to expose Nuradyne but to *own* it — leverage against Sable and Voight to install himself (or Anand, or a chosen faction) as the reactors' new master. The siphon keeps running, quietly rationed, "responsibly" managed, the death of U-2209 slowed but not stopped, the truth buried under new management. He tells himself he'll do it better. Sarn and the FMA are content — the leak is contained by a man who now has as much to hide as they do. Coyle is paid off and silenced comfortably this time. *The most stable ending, and the most damning: the machine finds a better-mannered operator.* It is exactly what Nuradyne did to Coyle, done once more, by Ryker.

Cross-cutting minor variables color every ending: Vessel-9 alive/dead; Toby returned to Della; Anand a witness or a corpse; Harmon a witness or fled; and — the throughline — **whether Coyle dies unheard, dies heard, or lives.** The story's moral scoreboard is not Ryker's body count; it's whether the man the world discarded was, at the end, *listened to.*

## Themes

- **Complicity is a chain, not a villain.** No single monster. Coyle sold to survive; Harmon buried to survive; Sable ruled by the ledger; the FMA abandoned, then covered up; Ryker is invited to add his link. Guilt distributes; the machine runs on everyone's small reasonable choice.
- **Knowledge as the deadliest export.** The most dangerous thing that crossed universes wasn't an army or a bomb — it was one stranded man's expertise. The FMA polices *travel*; the catastrophe came from *ideas*, which no cordon stops.
- **The externalized world.** Genocide as an accounting entry. The drained universe is invisible, therefore unpriced, therefore expendable — a mirror for every real cost a system chooses not to see.
- **The witness nobody believes.** Coyle held the truth for thirty years and it was worthless because no one would hear it. The story asks whether being *right* matters at all without being *heard*, and makes "was he listened to" the true measure of every ending.
- **Institutions protect themselves first.** The FMA's legitimacy rests on never being the arsonist. Confronted with proof it was, it reaches not for justice but for the eraser — and needs a disposable man to hold it. Ryker's arc is whether he stays disposable.
- **Atonement is a choice made too late, and worth making anyway.** Coyle can die closing the wound he opened. It doesn't undo U-2209. It's the only clean thing left, and the game lets the player let him have it.
