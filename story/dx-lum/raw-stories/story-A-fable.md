# The Meter — a DX_LUM story

Writer: fable | Emphasis: the drained universe & the operative

---

## 1. Logline & how this version reads

**Logline:** Sent to a poisoned Earth to investigate a whisper of illegal multiversal activity,
FMA agent Liam Ryker discovers that the city's life-support reactors are strip-mining the energy
of a neighboring universe — a near-twin of this one — and that the "terrorist" he's hunting is
that universe's last ambassador, carrying an ultimatum and a doomsday device in the same case.
Then he learns why the anomaly's signature looked familiar to his employers: the FMA's own
civilization runs on the same physics, politely, at scale, and his real mission was never to stop
the crime — it was to repossess it.

**How it reads:** A noir procedural that curdles into cosmic horror. The horror is never
tentacles; it is accounting. Somewhere a universe is being billed for Quinmore's dinner hour, and
the invoice has been paid in winters. The operative gives that horror a human face — not a
monster loose in the slums but a grieving, disciplined soldier from a world that is almost this
one, hunted by a woman who is almost her. Ryker stands where he always stands in this genre: the
last person in the room with working eyes, deciding whose lights stay on.

---

## 2. Fixed vs. invented

**Taken as fixed (canon, untouched):** Ryker's blank-slate ex-military characterization; the FMA
and its detection of a faint transversal anomaly from U-7134; the prologue beats (HQ recall,
briefing, medical, armory, portal to the cloaked outpost, partner triangulates the slum reactor,
cloaked aircraft to Northgate, train downtown); Quinmore, the toxic atmosphere, the dome sectors,
the Corporate Union board, Nuradyne (power) vs. Atlas Subsystems (shielding); the multiversal
siphon reactors known only to Nuradyne's inner circle; the slum reactor explosion caused by an
operative sent through an enlarged aperture by a drained universe; the terrorist framing, cordon,
checkpoints, relocation warehouse; the stranded liaison who sold FMA knowledge to Nuradyne and was
discarded; Director Harmon and the suppressed abnormal-readings reports; Kira Voight, Chief of
Security; the slum mother and her detained son; the datacube from the dead scientist; the Aire
Gardens station/elevator level; androids as non-revolting servant flavor; all player-verb
constraints.

**Invented (my additions):** Everything about the drained universe — its name in Harmon's ledger
(**Donor One**), the congruence principle (the siphon couples best to near-twin universes), the
nine-year "Dimming," the Counterlight Directorate, the ultimatum-and-Recoil mission design, and
the operative's identity as Kira Voight's twin. The liaison's name (**Elias Wray**) and his prior
career certifying "fallow" universes for the FMA. The FMA's hidden agenda: the **Draft** (the
licensed tithe that powers the portal network) and **Cinder Protocol** (the recovery-and-sanitation
directive under Ryker's mission). The partner's name (**Idris Vale**) and his arc. The **Deepwell**
reactor overdraw. The Greyline syndicate, Dana and Theo Osei (the mother and son), Atlas
executive **Ruth Okonjo**, and the four endings.

## 3. Factions & power structure

**Nuradyne.** Power monopoly of the Corporate Union, and the only institution on this Earth that
knows what its reactors actually burn. Its inner circle is maybe eleven people. Its institutional
position after the blast: deny, cordon, capture the operative alive if possible (her force-field
harness is a technology sample worth more than the reactor she destroyed), and cover the sector's
power deficit by running the remaining reactors — above all the deep-city plant, **Deepwell** —
at 140% draw. Nuradyne's answer to being caught stealing is to steal faster.

**Atlas Subsystems.** Builds and maintains the dome shielding; publicly blames Nuradyne's
security, privately suspects a Nuradyne power play, because Atlas's own blast telemetry doesn't
match any bomb chemistry they know. Atlas wants that telemetry to mean leverage on the CU board,
not truth for its own sake. Represented by VP of Integrity **Ruth Okonjo**, who will trade with
Ryker but never for free.

**The Corporate Union board.** Approved the reactors as "cutting-edge energy technology" and does
not want to learn otherwise, because every seat at that table is warmed by Nuradyne's grid. The
board is not a conspiracy; it is worse — a customer.

**The slums, the warehouse, and the Greyline.** The evacuated district's people, dumped in a
relocation warehouse and screened like cattle. The **Greyline** syndicate smuggles filters, food,
and people through the cordon's dead subway; it is apolitical and mercenary, and therefore the
only institution in Quinmore that will sell to anyone — including a fugitive from another
universe who pays in flawless, serial-numberless gold.

**The FMA.** Ryker's employer: the multiversal police, the treaty power, the adults in the room.
Its overt agenda is real — unlicensed transversal activity genuinely is dangerous. Its covert
agenda is the story's spine: the FMA's portal network is powered by the **Draft**, a regulated
energy tithe drawn from universes certified "fallow" (non-progenitive: no life, no future worth
protecting — a certification that is, as the liaison knows firsthand, a paperwork fiction).
Siphon physics is not new to the FMA. It is their founding secret, and Nuradyne is running an
unlicensed copy of it. The mission tasking Ryker carries a sealed annex he was never meant to
read: **Cinder Protocol** — locate the leak, recover the technology, sanitize every witting
person.

**The Counterlight Directorate (Donor One).** The drained universe's answer. Donor One is a
near-twin of U-7134 that never fought the chemical war: an open sky, a living Earth, its own
Quinmore with trees down the median of the same streets. Nine years ago its grid began browning
out; then batteries stopped holding charge; then the crop belts failed as photosynthetic yield
fell; then the winters stopped ending. They called it the Dimming and thought it was the
universe's own decay — until a physicist noticed the loss curve had a *schedule*. The drain
modulated with a 24-hour load cycle that was not theirs. Demand spikes at someone else's dawn and
dinner hour. They could read Quinmore's day in their own dying sun. Two billion people froze or
starved while, one universe over, the lights stayed on. Counterlight is what remains of their
Nuradyne-equivalent, repurposed from utility to weapon program: they reverse-engineered the
aperture from nine years of being on the receiving end, spent a city's annual power budget to
blow one channel wide for eleven seconds, and sent through the only thing that would fit — one
person, one shielded harness, one case. They are not monsters and not an army. They are a
bankrupt civilization's entire foreign policy, wearing one woman's face.

## 4. Key characters

**Liam Ryker.** As canon: flat-affect, ex-military, elite, morally flexible. This story leans on
one trait — he reads meters. Every horror in the plot arrives as a number on a gauge before it
arrives as a corpse, and Ryker is the man who looks at gauges.

**Idris Vale** — Ryker's partner, at the outpost. Steady voice on the infolink, does the
off-screen analysis the player can't (tapes, logs, signal work). Vale is carrying the sealed
Cinder annex and hating it more every day; his arc is whether he opens the portal for the
compliance team or holds it shut.

**Director Marcus Harmon** — head of the reactor project. Not a cackling villain: an engineer who
saw the abnormal readings, understood exactly what they meant — *the far side is pushing back* —
and filed them under "contained." He keeps the **Donor Ledger**: candidate universes graded by
yield, congruence, and "response risk," with the exhausted ones marked, in his own neat hand,
*quiet*. His defense, when it comes, is the most honest line in the story: "I kept four million
people breathing. Show me the moment I should have chosen differently, and who dies in it."

**Kira Voight** — Nuradyne Chief of Security. Outside the inner circle: she believes the
terrorist story because she wrote its security response. She lost six of her own people at the
reactor. Her hunt for the operative is personal, professional, and — when she finally sees the
fugitive's face — impossible. Voight is the story's swing character: shown the ledger, she can
become Ryker's most dangerous ally or, if handled badly, the finale's most dangerous obstacle.

**The operative — "Warden," Counterlight Directorate.** Donor One's soldier. In her universe she
held Voight's job — chief of security of the same plant, in the same city, before the Dimming
turned utility work into triage. She is not here to blow up reactors; the slum reactor explosion
was the aperture breach, not her goal. Her mission has three tiers: **confirm** the source,
**deliver** an ultimatum to whoever governs here, and — failing that — **plant the Recoil**, a
channel-inverter that will make the next full-power draw run backwards, paying nine years of debt
out of U-7134's grid in about six hours. Her tragedy: the aperture sealed behind her. Like the
liaison, she is stranded; unlike him, she chose it. She does not know if home is still alive.

**Elias Wray** — the stranded FMA liaison, now a broken man in the slums. My addition to his
canon backstory: before his posting he worked FMA certification — he surveyed and signed off
"fallow" universes for the Draft. He knows the tithe is real and the certifications are theater,
because his signature is on some of them. What he sold Nuradyne wasn't abstract theory; it was
the FMA's own portal-tap physics, from memory. He is therefore the one man alive who can look at
both Nuradyne's reactors and the FMA's portal network and say the quiet part: *they are the same
machine.*

**Dana Osei** and **Theo Osei** — the mother at the checkpoint and her missing son. Theo, twelve,
was playing in a dead maintenance crawl when the district evacuated; Nuradyne security threw him
in an unlogged holding cell. What he overheard through the cell wall — and repeats to Ryker
without understanding a word of it — is the story's cheapest and best intel: *"Deepwell to
one-forty until Slumside's rebuilt, Director's orders,"* and *"fields don't stop her — she walked
through the fence."* Dana, once Theo is home, becomes the warehouse's spine — and, in one ending,
the distribution network for the truth.

**Ruth Okonjo** — Atlas VP of Integrity. Holds the blast telemetry that proves "no known bomb did
this." Sells it for leverage, not justice — and is genuinely shaken when she finally learns what
her dome shields have been an accessory to.

## 5. Full plot, mission by mission

### Mission 0 — Prologue: FMA Headquarters *(canon, lightly extended)*

Ryker is recalled from leave; a low-level clerk routes him to the briefing. The tasking: a faint,
unprecedented, erratic transversal anomaly from unmonitored U-7134; covert insertion; investigate
and assess. Medical eval (the doctor logs a "baseline cognitive scan — standard for covert
insertions," a line that pays off in the Cinder reveal: sanitation has a softer option than a
bullet). Armory. Optional orientation wing.

Two planted foreshadows, both inside player verbs: in the HQ commissary a returning team from
another universe grouses within earshot about pulling **"Draft escort rotation"** — meaningless
now; and an unlocked terminal in the orientation wing shows a logistics email about **"tithe
station maintenance, Fallow Sector 9"** — reading it triggers nothing yet. The FMA hides its
secret the way real institutions do: in plain sight, as boring words.

Ryker portals to the cloaked U-7134 outpost with **Idris Vale**. Sensors hold residual
multiversal energy; Vale triangulates it to a reactor in Quinmore's slum district. Cloaked
aircraft to Northgate; Vale stays behind, on the infolink.

### Mission 1 — Northgate & the train down *(canon)*

Northgate: dome-filtered light, corporate saints on the billboards, androids sweeping the
platforms with the patience of furniture. Ryker is jumped by thugs near the station (the tutorial
fight); a Greyline tout watches him win and files his face away. Train downtown: the city
teaches itself to the player through window glass — sealed sectors, checkpoint queues, a
relocation-warehouse recruitment ad ("TEMPORARY HOUSING. YOUR CORPORATION CARES.").

### Mission 2 — The cordon and the reactor *(canon, extended)*

The slum district is sealed: dead dome, flooded with toxic air, Nuradyne checkpoints funneling
the last evacuees. At the entrance, **Dana Osei** is arguing with a Nuradyne officer about her
missing son; the officer turns to block Ryker; Ryker's forged clearance (or a hacked pass, or a
social-engineered escort — player's choice, per canon) gets him through. Dana stops him: find my
boy. First side-objective.

The reactor site, in rebreather gear: erased logs, dead security. Ryker finds the canon
**datacube** in a vent near a dead scientist — abnormal readings, comms cut, "Reported it to
Director Harmon, told me to forget about it." Two leads: Harmon, and the readings.

My additions on-site, all discoverable by looking and by infolink: a security corridor where six
bodies lie with wounds no gun makes — blunt force through armor, one man thrown *through* a
partition wall; scorching around the aperture chamber that radiates inward, not outward (the
blast came *to* the reactor, not from it); and a fist-sized punch-hole in a blast door with a
faint residue Vale samples remotely. Vale, infolink, too casual: *"That field residue matches
nothing in the U-7134 catalog... hang on. It matches something old. Something of ours. I'll
dig."* First hairline crack in the FMA's story.

### Mission 3 — Nuradyne HQ: Harmon, the basement, and the boy *(canon, extended)*

Nuradyne HQ, per the canon location sheet — basement (range, armory, research labs, forensics),
upper floors (CEO, offices, Security Division, Intelligence). **Harmon** receives Ryker (whatever
cover Ryker's running — auditor, CU liaison, insurance forensics) and stonewalls flawlessly:
terrorist attack, ongoing investigation, the datacube is "a stressed employee's note." What he
says and what he avoids saying are two different things; the player's dialogue choices can pry
loose exactly one slip — Harmon refers to the fugitive as "her" before the terrorist has any
public description.

The real haul is the basement. On a forensics terminal, an email chain (reading it triggers
Vale's infolink and the next objective, per the canon verb design): fragments of the operative's
recovered kit — an alloy nobody can date, a dead harness cell "with a charge density we can't
explain" — and Voight's standing order: **capture alive; the harness is priority one.** On a labs
terminal: the load directive — **Deepwell to 140% draw until Slumside is replaced.** Vale,
quietly: *"They lose a reactor and their answer is to pull harder. Ryker — if the anomaly was the
draw, they just made it louder."*

Deeper in, past the holding block's unlogged cells: **Theo Osei**. Ryker can bypass the cell with
a multitool, sneak him out, fight him out, or blackmail him out with what's on the forensics
terminal. Theo, shaken, parrots what he heard through the wall — Deepwell, one-forty, Director's
orders; and *she walked through the fence, fields don't stop her.* He doesn't understand it.
Ryker does: the overdraw is provocation, and the fugitive is shielded.

### Mission 4 — The warehouse, the Greyline, and Elias Wray

Returning Theo to Dana at the **relocation warehouse** — an overcrowded corporate hellhole of
cots, ration lines, and a PA system that keeps calling the inmates "guests" — buys Ryker the
slums' trust and the mission's human floor. Here the player sees what the cordon is *for*: every
face scanned at the exits, "screening for the bomber," actually screening for her.

The Greyline tout from Northgate resurfaces: someone's been buying rebreather filters and
ration-dense food through the dead subway, paying in flawless unmarked gold, and the syndicate
would love to know who before Nuradyne's bounty makes the question dangerous. The trail Ryker
actually follows leads sideways: the buyer asked the Greyline one strange question — *who in this
city knows about doors?* — and the Greyline sold her a name. A slum address. **Elias Wray.**

Wray's scene is the story's first full reveal, delivered as pure conversation (canon verb). A
broken, bitter, terrified man who recognizes Ryker's kit the moment he walks in — *"Standard
issue's gotten lighter. Tell them their liaison sends his regards"* — and, once he stops shaking,
gives the player the liaison backstory whole: stranded by the war, traded everything he knew to
Nuradyne for a dome address, discarded when their engineers caught up. And then the line that
detonates under the FMA: *"You think Nuradyne invented that reactor? Son, that's portal physics.
**Their** physics. The tap that keeps your portal network lit. I didn't teach Nuradyne how to
build a reactor — I taught them what the FMA already burns."* He won't say more — he wants out,
passage to any universe that isn't this one, and he'll trade the rest for it.

And: she found him first. The buyer of filters sat where Ryker is sitting, three nights ago, and
asked him one question — not how the reactors work. **Who gives the orders here.** Wray sold her
the same thing he sells everyone: a map of power. He can broker a meeting.

### Mission 5 — Aire Gardens, and the woman under the city

The meet is under the cordon. Ryker takes the train to **Aire Gardens** — the canon
station/atrium level: chopper landing pads above, luxury advertising ("BREATHE EASY™ — AIR SO
CLEAN YOU'LL FORGET"), and the long elevator ride down the dome's inner face to the dead subway —
the Greyline's smuggling artery beneath the sealed district.

The operative — **"Warden"** — chooses the ground: a subway platform with sightlines, her harness
shimmering faintly, six of Voight's best already dead behind her over the past week. The scene is
a conversation the player enters knowingly (canon verb: initiate by entering the area), and it is
the story's centerpiece. She is exhausted, precise, and human. What she tells Ryker, in her flat
soldier's cadence, is the Dimming — the grid failing, the batteries dying, the crop belts
browning, the winters that stopped ending, the physicist who found the *schedule* in the loss
curve. *"We knew when you cooked dinner. Your evening meal was our blackout."* Two billion dead.
And the discovery that broke her people worse than the death toll: when they finally imaged the
far side of the drain, they saw their own city. Same streets. Same river. A world that was them,
with the lights on.

Her mission, tier by tier, plainly: confirmed — done. Ultimatum — that's Ryker. She cannot walk
into a CU boardroom; he can. The terms are simple: **full stop on all multiversal draw, held for
seven consecutive days** — Counterlight reads the drain like a telegraph; the stop *is* the
reply. The deadline: forty days from her insertion, of which twenty-nine remain. After that,
Counterlight assumes the ultimatum died with her and spends everything they have left on the
**Last Winter** — an uncontrolled overload through the standing channels, hers included. She
doesn't know if it will work. She doesn't know if home is even still alive. She shows Ryker the
case anyway: the **Recoil**, the controlled version, buildable into Deepwell's aperture chamber,
convertible into their justice if his diplomacy fails.

She is also, visibly, not what Nuradyne's wanted posters say. She has killed only armed security,
walked around every civilian, and bought her food. *"I did not come to do to you what you did to
us,"* she says. *"Yet."*

### Mission 6 — The Donor Ledger, and the two Voights

Ultimatums need proof. Vale (infolink) and Wray agree on what would move a boardroom: Harmon's
own records. Ryker goes back into Nuradyne HQ — upper floors this time, Intelligence and the
Director's vault, the hardest infiltration in the game — and pulls the **Donor Ledger**: the
candidate universes, the yield curves, the congruence grades, the "response risk" column, the
worlds marked *quiet*. And the top line — **Donor One, congruence 0.97** — with Harmon's marginal
note from the first abnormal-readings incident: *"Far-side interference. Precautions adequate."*
He knew someone was alive over there. He graded their resistance as noise.

Reading the ledger triggers the mission's optional pivot: **Voight**. The player can walk into
her office with it (conversation), leave it where her own people will find it, or skip her
entirely — and the choice matters, because this mission ends with her kill-team finally cornering
Warden in the subway. If Ryker has turned Voight, she's on-site when it happens and sees the
fugitive's face — her face, nine years older, hungrier, from a world where her job meant keeping
a dying grid alive instead of guarding the machine that killed it. The scene is staged as an
in-game confrontation the player enters mid-crisis; how it resolves — dead operative, dead
kill-team, or Voight standing her men down while two identical women stare at each other across a
train platform — depends on the player's positioning, prior choices, and how fast they are with
words or weapons. A turned Voight becomes the finale's inside woman. An unturned Voight becomes
its second act.

Either way, mission's end delivers the second horror beat by pure infolink: Vale finishes the
residue analysis he started in Mission 2. *"The harness field signature. It matches our portal
containment tech — three generations back. Ryker, her people didn't invent this from nothing.
They reverse-engineered it from the drain itself. From what was being done to them. Which means
everything on both sides of this — Nuradyne's reactor, her shield, our network — it's all the
same machine. I'm going to look at something I'm not supposed to look at."*

### Mission 7 — The outpost: Cinder

Vale calls Ryker home to the outpost, off-schedule, voice wrong. On Vale's terminal — Vale left
it unlocked; that's the point — Ryker reads the sealed annex (canon verb: read computers;
reading it triggers HQ's automated infolink, which is how the FMA learns the seal is broken):

**CINDER PROTOCOL.** Mission tasking, true version. The anomaly's signature was flagged at
detection as **Draft-pattern physics** — the FMA knew from day one this wasn't travel. Objectives,
in order: confirm unlicensed energy-draft technology in U-7134; recover all technical materials;
**sanitize all witting persons** — a list that has been growing in the file as Ryker's own
reports came in: Harmon. The inner circle. Wray ("previously believed deceased — priority").
The operative ("technology sample — recover harness"). And an annotation against Ryker's own
name: *cognitive baseline on file; field agent to be normalized post-mission.* The medical scan
from the prologue was the FMA fitting him for a memory he'd be given back edited.

Below it, the context that makes it policy rather than villainy: a Draft briefing paper. The
tithe. The fallow certifications. The network load figures — every portal jump costed in drawn
energy, including both of Ryker's. And one flagged internal memo, decades old, questioning
whether Fallow Sector certifications were being conducted "with sufficient rigor," annotated by
some ancient bureaucrat: *out of scope.* Wray's signature is on two of the certifications in the
appendix.

Vale's confrontation is quiet. He's a believer who has run out of belief: *"I signed up to police
the roads. Turns out we own the toll booth, and the toll is somebody's sun."* He tells Ryker the
rest: reading the annex tripped a flag; HQ's **compliance team** is already staging; Vale
controls the outpost portal and can slow-walk them maybe thirty hours. What comes through that
portal will execute Cinder with or without Ryker — the question is what U-7134 looks like when
it arrives.

Twenty-two days left on Warden's clock. Deepwell at 140%. Everything now converges.

### Mission 8 — Deepwell *(finale hub)*

Deepwell: Nuradyne's deep-city plant, a cathedral of draw shafts sunk below the oldest dome,
running white-hot to cover the Slumside deficit — which means its aperture is wide, hot, and
exactly what Warden needs. Everyone arrives within hours of everyone else: Ryker, with whatever
allies his play has earned (Warden; a turned Voight opening doors from inside; Okonjo's Atlas
telemetry and board access; Wray, terrified, as the only living expert; Dana's warehouse network
outside the fence); Harmon, personally supervising the overdraw; Voight or her successor running
security; and, on Vale's thirty-hour fuse, the FMA compliance team.

The finale is a hub, not a corridor: the aperture chamber, the control gallery, Harmon's site
office, the CU board's emergency uplink room, and the portal-side service dock where the
compliance team will breach. The player's accumulated choices determine who is alive, who is
allied, and which doors open — and then the player chooses an ending.

## 6. The central twists, called explicitly

**Twist 1 — the siphon horror: *the best donor is your twin.*** The siphon's coupling efficiency
scales with congruence — the more a universe resembles yours, the better it drains. Nuradyne's
targeting didn't choose victims; the physics did, and the physics chooses *almost-you*. Donor One
has its own Quinmore, its own river, its own Nuradyne — and its own Kira Voight, who is standing
in the subway wearing a stolen rebreather. U-7134 spent nine years burning a world of its own
doubles to keep the lights on. The reveal is fully playable: the ledger (read a computer), Warden's
account (conversation), and the two Voights face to face (entering an area triggers the scene).

**Twist 2 — the FMA's hidden agenda: *Cinder, and the Draft.*** The FMA recognized the anomaly's
physics at detection because it is their own: the portal network runs on a licensed tithe drawn
from universes certified "fallow" by a process the liaison — who signed some of the paperwork —
knows to be theater. Ryker was never sent to protect naive universes; he was sent to find a leak
of the FMA's founding secret, recover it, and mark every witting person for sanitation — himself
included, via the prologue's "baseline scan." Playable: Wray's conversations, Vale's terminal
(read a computer → triggers the HQ infolink), the prologue's boring foreshadow emails.

**Twist 3 — the provocation: *Deepwell at one-forty.*** Nuradyne's response to losing a reactor
is to overdraw the rest — answering an act of war by escalating the atrocity that caused it,
on a timeline that intersects Warden's forty-day deadline. Delivered by a child who doesn't
understand the words he's repeating.

**Twist 4 — the ambassador, not the bomber.** The "terrorist attack" was an aperture breach from
the far side; the operative has killed only armed security and came carrying terms. The monster
of the wanted posters is the most disciplined moral actor in the story — and even she is carrying
a doomsday device, because her people could not afford to send hope without a threat behind it.

## 7. Player-choice structure & endings

Throughout, choices bank consequences rather than branching the spine: how Ryker enters the
cordon; whether Theo comes home; whether Voight sees the ledger and her twin; whether Okonjo gets
her leverage; whether Wray gets his promise of passage; how much of the truth Dana's warehouse
network hears; how Vale's thirty hours get spent. At Deepwell, four endings — each an ideology,
each achievable with the player's verbs, each with a distinct epilogue over the drain telemetry
Counterlight is reading like a telegraph.

### Ending 1 — CINDER (Order): "The toll booth stays."

Ryker executes the mission as truly written. He opens the service dock for the compliance team,
gives them Harmon, the inner circle, the ledger, the cores, Wray, and — if he can take her — the
operative's harness. The FMA does what institutions do: it works. The reactors are retuned off
Donor One within days (the drain stops; Counterlight reads seven days of silence; the Last Winter
is never fired). Quinmore's grid is quietly stabilized under FMA stewardship it will never know
about. Ryker sits for his "normalization" or negotiates his way out of it, depending on final
choices — either way, he is commended.

Epilogue: Donor One's dawn measurably brightens for the first time in nine years — Counterlight's
telemetry, over which someone weeps off-camera. Final shot: an FMA logistics screen. Quinmore's
retuned reactors have been folded into the network as licensed capacity. Source: *Fallow Sector
9. Certified non-progenitive.* Certifying signature on file. The lights stay on. Somewhere
"empty," a winter begins.

### Ending 2 — THE METER READING (Truth): "Let them see the bill."

Ryker burns it all open: Harmon's ledger, Okonjo's telemetry, Wray's testimony, Warden delivering
her own ultimatum to the CU board's emergency uplink — while Dana's warehouse network and the
Greyline push the raw files past any corporate kill-switch. The board, facing forty days and two
billion counts of manslaughter, orders the full stop; seven days of silence answer the telegraph.
Nuradyne is broken up on the board's own table. The cost is the truth's cost: the grid runs on
honest power now, which means rationing, brownouts, evacuated outer sectors, and a population
that knows its golden age was theft. The FMA, its secret loose in a universe that can now name
it, quarantines U-7134 rather than sanitize a whole world — and somewhere in the FMA's own
parliament, Vale's forwarded copy of the Draft briefing starts its slow fire.

Epilogue: the warehouse, months later, cold and lit by half-power lamps. Dana teaching kids in a
ration line. On the wall, someone has painted the loss curve with its 24-hour schedule and, under
it: WE KNOW WHAT TIME IT IS. Warden, stranded and unhidden, drinks bad coffee with Voight in a
sector cafeteria — two identical women, one world between them, learning to be strangers.

### Ending 3 — RECOIL (Their justice): "The debt comes due."

Diplomacy dies — or the player never tries it. Ryker helps Warden build the Recoil into
Deepwell's aperture chamber and holds the gallery — against Voight's security, Harmon's
lockdown, and the compliance team, in whatever order the player's choices have queued them —
through the six hours of the reversed draw. U-7134's grid pays nine years of debt in one night:
rolling dome failures, Atlas reserves buying evacuation time (more if Okonjo is an ally, less if
she's dead), the outer sectors going dark for good. The channels collapse permanently behind the
surge — every aperture, including the FMA's access to U-7134. No more siphon, ever. No more
portals, ever. The compliance team's ride home closes in front of them.

Warden can ride the surge home through the collapsing channel — or, if the player has walked her
there, choose not to: her world needs the energy more than it needs her, and this world, which is
almost hers, needs a witness. Ryker is stranded either way, and knew he would be. The FMA's file
on U-7134 ends mid-sentence.

Epilogue: Quinmore under half-light, alive, smaller, honest. Ryker in the warehouse district with
the other stranded man — Wray, who finally, horribly, belongs somewhere. Far away on the only
telemetry that matters, Donor One's sun, at ground level, is one measurable degree warmer.

### Ending 4 — ASHES (The dark road): "Nobody gets the fire."

Ryker concludes that the technology itself is the crime — Nuradyne's version, Counterlight's
version, the FMA's version — and that any hand holding it, including the honest ones, becomes
Harmon eventually. He destroys the cores, the ledger, the harness, and the Recoil; collapses
Deepwell's aperture with the plant's own overdraw; and closes the leak the only way Cinder never
imagined — by making there be nothing to recover. The player chooses how far "witting" goes:
walking Harmon into CU justice for the mundane crimes, or sanitizing the inner circle himself,
becoming the protocol he refused. The drain stops — Counterlight reads its seven days and stands
down, never learning why. The FMA finds a burned site, a jammed portal, and an agent who won't
come home. Quinmore faces its power crisis with no miracle and no thief's shortcut.

Epilogue: the wasteland outside the domes, toxic wind over dead ground. Ryker walking toward the
cloaked outpost — or away from it; the player's last input is a direction. Vale's final infolink,
already breaking up: *"For what it's worth — I'd have signed it."* On a dead channel underneath,
almost too faint to render: a rhythmic modulation in nothing at all. Somebody, somewhere,
tapping the pipe. Saying thank you. Or asking who's there.

## 8. Themes

**The metered universe.** The horror is not that the void is empty; it's that it has a billing
cycle. Every institution in the story — Nuradyne, the CU, the FMA — converts atrocity into
infrastructure by putting a gauge on it, and every gauge has a reader who chooses not to ask
what's on the far end of the pipe.

**The twin as victim.** The siphon drains what resembles you. Exploitation here is not of the
alien but of the almost-self — Voight hunting Voight, Quinmore burning Quinmore. The question the
operative embodies: what do you owe the version of you that got your bill?

**Stranded men.** Wray and Warden are the same figure at opposite moral poles — cut off from home,
surviving on what they carried in their heads. One sold it; one is spending it. Ryker ends the
story eligible to become either.

**Keeping the lights on.** Harmon's defense is real, and the endings price it four different
ways. The story never lets "four million people breathing" be a lie — only a bill, and the player
signs it.
