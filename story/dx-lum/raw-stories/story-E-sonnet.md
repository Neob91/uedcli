# DX_LUM — "The Null Suppressor"

Writer: sonnet | Emphasis: Ryker's past, his handlers, and a personal reveal

## Logline

A mercenary with no memory of grief is sent to investigate an energy leak between worlds, and
discovers that the agency that made him what he is has done this before — to his own home.

## How this version reads

Everything the bible establishes stays exactly as written: Ryker, the FMA, U-7134/Quinmore, the
Corporate Union, Nuradyne's siphon reactors, Atlas, the operative, the stranded liaison, Harmon,
Voight, the missing kid. What I add sits underneath it, not against it: Ryker's flatness isn't just
temperament, it's maintained — a standing-order neural suppressant the FMA gives to a specific class
of recruit, ones pulled from universes that no longer exist to go home to. His FMA partner isn't a
neutral voice on the infolink; she recruited him, and she is the one person in the story who knows
exactly what he is. The FMA's hidden agenda isn't a rogue faction — it's policy, one Ryker has
personally benefited from without ever being told its price. The reveal never breaks his affect: it
lands as documents he reads, a stranger who recognizes a scar, a directive he's given and has to
decide whether to follow. He doesn't get a breakdown. He gets a choice.

## What I take as fixed vs. what I invented

**Fixed (canon, unchanged):** the whole Overview.md spine — FMA structure and mission, the
transversal-anomaly detection, the outpost, the portal-and-partner prologue, Northgate/train arrival,
the slum checkpoint with Selene and the missing kid, the reactor site and the datacube naming Harmon,
Harmon's stonewalling, Voight as Chief of Security, Atlas vs. Nuradyne's public/private blame split,
the liaison's full backstory and status (alive, broken, in the slums, unknown to the FMA), the
operative's origin and rampage, the CU board structure, the player-verb constraints, androids as
non-revolting flavor, the Aire Gardens/elevator/subway idea from Ideas.md.

**Invented, all additive:**
- **Nadia Osei** — Ryker's FMA handler and the partner who travels with him to the outpost. She
  recruited him personally.
- **The Cradle Program** — an FMA recruitment track that pulls operatives from universes recently
  destroyed or annexed, because they have nothing to defect back to. Ryker is one.
- **The Null Suppressor** — a standing neural-conditioning regimen (implant + dosing) Cradle
  recruits are kept on, marketed internally as trauma stabilization, that also flattens affect.
  This is offered as one *possible* explanation for Ryker's low-affect presentation, layered under
  the existing characterization, not a replacement for it — the game never definitively resolves how
  much of "Ryker" was always like this.
- **The Aftermath Directorate** — a quiet FMA division that, rather than always intervening,
  sometimes lets a doomed universe finish collapsing and then moves in afterward to recruit
  survivors and requisition salvageable tech. This is the FMA's hidden agenda the brief asks for.
- **U-2231 / U-2233** — Ryker's homeworld and the operative's homeworld, drained by unrelated events
  a decade apart, and adjacent on the FMA's universe registry — evidence the Directorate has been
  working this cluster for a while.
- **Desmond Cray** — a name for the previously-unnamed stranded liaison, and the detail that,
  pre-war, his FMA logistics post processed Cradle intakes, which is how he recognizes what Ryker is
  on sight.
- **Selene and Toby Marsh** — names for the mother and missing kid.
- **Kira Voight's independent board case, an Atlas fixer named Ines Tovar, a slum information
  broker named Coyle, and CU board rep Rosalind Vane** — new supporting cast for the second and
  third acts, all built to serve existing factions rather than add new ones.
- **The operative given a name and voice**, Serel Kaan, reachable through an FMA translator matrix
  Ryker already carries as standard kit — no new player verb, just an existing one (initiate
  conversation) applied to a character the bible left silent.

Nothing above overwrites an established fact. Cray is still the same broken man who traded FMA
knowledge to Nuradyne; Harmon still suppressed the reports; the reactors still siphon; the operative
still causes the reactor's, then flees. I'm filling seams, not replacing planks.

## Factions & power structure

**The FMA.** Not a monolith. Field agents like Ryker and handlers like Nadia believe the mission
statement at face value: police multiversal crime, protect naive worlds. Above them, the Aftermath
Directorate runs on a colder calculus — the multiverse is too large to save every world, so triage
some, and where a world is already lost, extract what's useful (survivors with skills, salvageable
tech, strategic footholds) rather than waste resources trying to prevent an ending already written.
Ryker's recruitment was Directorate business dressed as a rescue. Nadia was the field asset who did
the dressing, and she has never fully made peace with that.

**Nuradyne.** Runs city power via multiversal siphon reactors it can't fully explain even to its own
CU board, because that explanation would end the company. Harmon's inner circle knows what the
reactors are; the board thinks it bought a patent. Kira Voight sits one level below the truth — she
knows something is being hidden from her and is building a case, not out of principle but because an
uninvestigated cover-up under her watch is a liability she can be blamed for later.

**Atlas Subsystems.** Depends on Nuradyne's power, resents the dependency, wants the Corporate Union
board seat that power gives Nuradyne. Publicly blames Nuradyne for the blast; privately suspects a
Nuradyne power play; will happily trade resources for leverage over Harmon, whether or not it's the
truth Ryker's after.

**The Corporate Union board.** Rosalind Vane represents Nuradyne's board seat and is functionally as
in the dark as the rest of the board — she approved "cutting-edge energy tech," not a multiverse
weapon. The board's only real interest is which corp comes out of this scandal owning the reactor
seat.

**The slums / the Undercroft.** Coyle runs an information-and-smuggling network that survived the
evacuation by knowing which checkpoints to avoid. Selene Marsh becomes one of Coyle's runners after
Toby is rescued — she has nothing left to lose and wants Nuradyne to answer for the district, not just
give it back.

**The drained universes.** Represented in-story by one: U-2233, Serel Kaan's home, a world with no
multiversal defense of its own, which had to gamble everything on one operative and one aperture
before it collapsed for good. Not a faction with an agenda beyond survival — the story's clearest
victim, and Ryker's mirror.

## Key characters

**Liam Ryker.** Unchanged in presentation: an outsider, no cover, blank affect, tactically excellent,
leans good but flexible. What's added lives entirely below the surface and never gets voiced as
melodrama — a scar behind his left ear that dialogue occasionally lets other characters notice before
he does anything about it; a standing weekly dose he takes without comment, shown once in an idle
armory animation before the player has any reason to care; a total absence of any personal
correspondence, ties, or backstory dialogue options where every other character has some.

**Nadia Osei — FMA handler.** Stays at the outpost, feeds Ryker objectives via infolink exactly as
canon has her doing. Recruited Ryker out of the wreckage of U-2231 nine years ago. Genuinely protective
of him in the way a handler can be protective of an asset she also, quietly, thinks of as something
closer. Under direct orders from the Directorate she has not been read into fully — she knows enough
to be complicit, not enough to be in control.

**Desmond Cray — the liaison.** As canon: stranded, traded FMA knowledge to Nuradyne, cut loose,
broken, terrified, believed dead by the FMA. New detail: his old FMA logistics post processed
Cradle-track intake files before the war, so he knows the tell — the scar, the affect, the exact model
of the Null Suppressor case Ryker still carries as spares. He is the first person in the story to say
what Ryker is, out loud, to Ryker's face.

**Serel Kaan — the operative.** Force-field-augmented soldier from U-2233. Sent through a
deliberately-widened aperture with no expectation of return, to find and disable whatever was draining
her world before it finished dying. She's not a monster; she's Ryker with worse luck and no FMA to
scoop her up afterward.

**Director Harmon.** As canon — stonewalls, suppresses, has weathered "abnormal readings" before and
was wrong about having it handled. New load-bearing detail: the failsafe he quietly built after the
last near-miss is a manual siphon shutdown, not a stabilizer — he's always known these reactors could
be stopped and chose not to, because stopping them stops the city's power.

**Kira Voight.** Competent, not naive, allies with Ryker only as far as it serves her own case against
Harmon; will burn him if it stops serving her.

**Selene and Toby Marsh.** As canon, plus an afterlife in Act 2 — Selene becomes a slum contact who
resents Nuradyne more with every excuse the CU board makes.

**Ines Tovar, Rosalind Vane, Coyle.** Supporting cast, one per faction, each an avenue for a different
ending.

## Full plot, mission by mission

### Act One — What Ryker Is Told

**M0. FMA HQ (established).** Recall from leave, briefing, medical, armory, optional orientation. New
beat, playable without dialogue-forcing: at medical, an attendant logs a routine dose under a line
item the player can read on a clipboard/terminal — "Cradle maintenance, subject 0231-R" — before
moving on. Nothing is explained. Nadia meets Ryker at the portal bay; her briefing matches canon's
"faint anomaly, moderate threat expected" almost verbatim, because that's genuinely all she's been
told.

**M1. Outpost → Northgate → downtown train (established).** Portal to the outpost, sensors
triangulate the slum reactor, cloaked flight to Northgate, train downtown, the thug encounter. New
color: one thug, recognizing something off in how Ryker doesn't flinch, calls him "corpse-eyes" before
the fight — first external hint, discardable as slum slang, that something reads wrong about him to
strangers.

**M2. Slum district checkpoint (established).** Selene arguing with security over Toby, the officer
redirecting to Ryker, Ryker's forged clearance, Selene's plea.

**M3. Reactor site investigation (established).** Erased logs, dead security, the vented datacube
naming Harmon and the abnormal readings.

**M4. Aire Gardens / down to the subway (Ideas.md, expanded).** Before Nuradyne HQ, Ryker's infolink
picks up a stronger residual trace pointing through Aire Gardens' train concourse. Luxury ads for
domed high-rises paper over cracked concourse tile — first real look at the CU's PR gap between image
and infrastructure. An elevator (per Ideas.md) drops to the subway level. Down there: signs of a fight
— scorched force-field burn patterns on concrete, a dead Nuradyne black-ops trooper nobody's come to
collect. First indirect confirmation the operative is still alive and armed, before anyone tells
Ryker so directly.

**M5. Finding Harmon (established).** Nuradyne HQ, stonewalled, rehearsed answers, the datacube
dismissed. Ryker also gets a first read of the building layout the brief calls for (basement gun
range/armory/labs/forensics; upper floor CEO/offices/security/intelligence) — sets up later infiltration
routes.

**M6. The holding cell — Toby (established).** Found mid-investigation of a Nuradyne facility. He
overheard a phrase he doesn't understand: two security officers arguing about whether "the shutdown
option" was ever briefed to the board. Toby repeats it verbatim to Ryker, meaninglessly to him,
loaded to the player. Returning Toby to Selene closes the loop the checkpoint opened and gives Ryker a
slum contact who owes him — Selene, later Coyle.

Act One ends on the subway burn marks and Toby's overheard line: two threads (who's the intruder, what
is Harmon sitting on) that the player now has to pull separately.

### Act Two — What Nadia Knows, What Cray Says

**M7. The Undercroft.** Selene, now running with Coyle's network, can point Ryker to whoever's been
selling gear to a "ghost" moving through the slum tunnels since the blast — the operative. Coyle wants
payment in intel Ryker's gathering; player can trade, steal, or strong-arm the lead. Establishes the
slums as a faction with its own economy, independent of the corp war above it.

**M8. Harmon's failsafe.** A break-in to Nuradyne R&D archives (multitool bypass or credentials, per
verb constraints — no tape review, no timestamp-hacking). Turns up engineering notes for a manual
siphon interrupt Harmon commissioned after an earlier "abnormal reading" incident and never told the
board about. Also turns up a cross-referenced old file, half-redacted, using the word "liaison" in a
context that predates Nuradyne's founding paperwork — Ryker recognizes the term from FMA training.
Reading it triggers an infolink call: Nadia, tense, telling him it's an old dead lead, not worth his
time. It's the first time in the story her voice doesn't match what's on the screen in front of him.

**M9. Kira Voight.** She's been quietly building her own case against Harmon (self-interest, not
principle) and will trade what she has — including confirmation the "shutdown option" Toby overheard
is real hardware, not a rumor — for whatever Ryker's found in the archive. Alliance of convenience,
openly transactional, breakable later.

**M10. Atlas's approach.** Ines Tovar, posing as an insurance investigator, offers resources
(equipment, safehouse access, a seat at the CU board hearing later) in exchange for anything that
proves Nuradyne's negligence. Player can take the deal, refuse it, or feed her false material to see
what Atlas does with it — first real fork toward the corporate-power ending.

**M11. Serel Kaan, first contact.** Tracking the operative through the tunnels below Aire Gardens
using the standard FMA translator matrix Ryker's carried since the outpost (existing kit, no new
verb), Ryker can talk her down instead of fighting. She's dying — the aperture burned most of her
suit's field generators on the way through — and has nothing left to complete except confirming the
reactor she came to stop is still running. Talking to her (an initiate-conversation option, per verb
constraints) surfaces her home designation: U-2233. Ryker doesn't react to it on-screen. The player,
if they've read the archive file from M8, might.

**M12. Desmond Cray.** Selene or Coyle can lead Ryker to a broken man squatting near the old transit
tunnels who flinches at anyone in FMA-adjacent gear. Cray, drunk and scared, recognizes the case
Ryker's Null Suppressor spares come in — he processed a thousand of them before the war. He says
what Nadia wouldn't: Cradle intake, U-2231, "they don't rescue you, son, they inventory you." This is
the scene where the personal twist and the FMA-agenda twist meet. It plays entirely as dialogue the
player chooses to pursue or cut short — Ryker's only required reaction is standard low-affect
delivery, the reveal lands on the player, not on a cutscene breakdown.

**M13. The registry.** Optional but signposted: a Nuradyne intelligence terminal (read-computer verb)
holds a scavenged fragment of FMA universe-registry data Cray sold them years ago as a demonstration
of what he knew. U-2231 and U-2233 sit two entries apart, both flagged in an old annotation column:
"Directorate review — post-collapse." Confirms, in Ryker's own hands, that his world and Kaan's were
both marked for exactly the same disposition.

Act Two ends with three live threads: Harmon's real, functioning shutdown switch; Kaan, dying and
done fighting; and the fact, now documented rather than implied, that the FMA has done this before —
to Ryker.

### Act Three — What Ryker Does

**M14. Confronting Nadia.** Ryker can raise the registry fragment over infolink or in person if the
mission routes him back through the outpost. She doesn't deny it. She explains, as much as she's
permitted: the Directorate doesn't run every recruitment through agents like her without reason —
some worlds are past saving, and pretending otherwise wastes people the FMA could actually help. She
believed it when she pulled Ryker out. She's less sure now that she's watching it happen to a world
with a name and a missing kid in it. She does not apologize on his behalf; that's not what she's for.

**M15. Harmon's endgame.** With Voight's help, Ryker can reach the shutdown interrupt. Using it stops
the drain on U-2233 (too late to save Kaan, if she's already died of her injuries, but it ends the
active drain) and every other universe currently being siphoned — and also kills a meaningful chunk of
Quinmore's power grid, with everything that implies for the domes. Harmon, cornered, will offer to
"handle it quietly" one more time — bribe, threat, or confession, player's read.

**M16. The board hearing.** With Vane and the CU board convened over the scandal, whatever Ryker's
gathered — Voight's case, Tovar's leverage, Cray's testimony if he can be convinced to give it,
Harmon's own failsafe as evidence — determines who takes the fall and who takes the seat.

### Endings

**A — Report in full (Order).** Ryker files everything with the FMA, including the registry fragment.
The Directorate quietly folds U-7134 into its post-collapse recruitment pipeline once Nuradyne's
grid finishes destabilizing — the same processing Ryker went through, run on Toby's generation next.
Nadia stays his handler. He stays on the Null Suppressor. Nothing about him changes, which is the
point.

**B — Expose it (Liberation).** Ryker routes the evidence outward — the CU board publicly, or an
independent settlement beyond CU control, or Cray's testimony broadcast where Nuradyne can't quietly
disappear it. Nuradyne's siphon program becomes impossible to hide; the FMA has to pull back from any
overt move on U-7134 while eyes are on it. Ryker becomes unreliable to the Directorate and useful to
no one but himself — closest the story gets to a clean win, at the cost of his standing with the only
organization that's ever given him a place.

**C — Take the seat (Business).** Ryker feeds everything to Atlas through Tovar. Atlas unseats
Nuradyne's board position using Ryker's evidence, absorbs the reactor program under new management,
and changes nothing about what the reactors do — just who profits. Ryker gets paid, promoted, and
kept on retainer. "Just business," same as the bible's stated tone for his morally flexible missions.

**D — Walk away (Renunciation).** Ryker kills the drain himself — Harmon if it comes to that, the
reactor regardless — without routing the fallout through the FMA, Atlas, or the CU board. He stops
taking the Null Suppressor on the way out, an optional epilogue beat (an armory animation mirroring
M0's, this time empty-handed) rather than a dialogue confession. He disappears into the slums, the
same ending Cray already lived, eyes open this time about what he's choosing.

Each ending can be flagged with whether Ryker's still dosing at the credits — a quiet variable, not a
fifth branch, that colors how each ending reads without adding new content to unlock it.

## The central twists, stated plainly

1. **The FMA's hidden agenda:** the Aftermath Directorate lets worlds it judges unsalvageable finish
   collapsing, then recruits from the wreckage — a policy, not a rogue cell, and Nadia is complicit by
   omission, not villainy.
2. **The multiverse-siphon horror, personalized:** Nuradyne's reactors are doing at industrial scale,
   to strangers, exactly what already happened to Ryker's own world once — and the FMA's response to
   that precedent was to harvest him, not prevent the next one.
3. **What the FMA made him:** the low affect the player has been reading as personality the whole game
   has a maintained, dosed component — never fully confirmed as the *whole* explanation, which is the
   point; Ryker himself may not know where the line is.

All three land through existing verbs: reading a datacube and a terminal, one dialogue with Cray, one
with Kaan, one infolink exchange with Nadia. No tape review, no timestamp-hacking, nothing outside the
constraints in `Prompts-For-LLM.md`.

## Themes

Whether rescue and inventory are the same act with different paperwork. Whether a person built by an
institution to not feel the cost of its decisions can still choose to feel it once he sees the
receipt. What's owed to a world already written off, and by whom. The gap between what a company says
its technology is and what it actually costs someone, somewhere, to run it — Nuradyne's reactors and
the FMA's Cradle Program are the same shape of lie at two different scales, and Ryker spends the whole
story standing inside both of them at once.
