# DX_LUM — "The Licensing" (Story B)

Writer: opus | Emphasis: the FMA's hidden agenda

---

## Logline

An FMA agent is sent to shut down a rogue power source that is quietly murdering entire universes — and discovers that his own agency isn't the multiverse's police, it's its cartel, and that "policing inter-universal crime" has always meant protecting a monopoly on the same atrocity. The only real question left is whose atrocity Ryker is willing to enforce.

## How this version reads

A slow tightening, then a floor dropping out. For the first two acts it plays like a corporate conspiracy thriller: Ryker peels a cover-up, races Nuradyne security to a fugitive, and works two megacorps against each other. The multiverse-siphon horror lands mid-game and reframes the villain from "greedy corp" to "civilization built on off-screen genocide." Then the last act turns the camera around: the thing Ryker has been treating as the fixed point — the FMA, home, the good guys, the mission — is revealed to be the largest and oldest version of the crime he came to stop. The twist isn't "everyone is evil." It's worse and quieter: everyone with the technology does this, and the FMA's whole moral architecture exists to make sure only the right people do. Ryker ends the game having to decide what "policing" was ever supposed to mean.

## What I take as fixed vs. what I invented

**Fixed (canon, kept intact):** Liam Ryker (blank-slate, ex-military, morally flexible); the FMA and its detected transversal anomaly from U-7134; Quinmore/Umbermont, the toxic atmosphere, dome sectors, the Corporate Union board; Nuradyne (power) and Atlas (shielding); the multiversal energy-siphon as the central secret and the source of the anomaly; the drained universe that sent a force-field operative who blew the slum reactor and fled; the framed-terrorist cordon and evacuee warehouse; the stranded pre-war FMA liaison who traded multiversal knowledge to Nuradyne and was cut loose; Director Harmon (suppressed the abnormal readings), Kira Voight (Nuradyne security chief), the FMA partner at the outpost feeding objectives, the slum mother and her detained son who overheard something; the prologue beat-sheet (HQ → outpost → Northgate → train → cordon → reactor → datacube → Harmon → the kid); the player-verb constraints.

**Invented (mine to fill the gaps):** the FMA as a *licensing cartel* whose member-worlds siphon "condemned" universes under sanction (the hidden agenda); the partner Corwin Halloran as FMA Acquisitions running the real op; the resonance-sampler as an acquisition-assessment tool; the identity of the drained universe (U-3300, a near-copy of U-7134's Earth); the named cast (Halloran, Controller Okonkwo, Chair Vivienne Sendra, the operative Sarai Vesh, liaison Silas Wray, mother Della Mott and son Kip, Atlas's Halden Cole, syndicate boss Vann Lorca, Dr. Marisol Ferro); "Quinmore" as the FMA's operational codename for the city the locals call Umbermont; the four endings.

## Factions & power structure

**The FMA (Federated Multiversal Authority).** Publicly (to its own agents): a federation of multiverse-capable Earths that polices inter-universal travel and punishes inter-universe crime. Actually: a licensing cartel. Every founding member world runs on *transversal extraction* — siphoning energy from universes the FMA's charts designate "condemned" (uninhabited, or close enough that the Directorate has ruled the loss acceptable). Extraction is legal if you're a member and you drain a licensed target; it's a capital crime if you're an outsider or you drain off-license. The FMA's entire moral vocabulary — "unauthorized," "rogue," "crime" — is monopoly language. Internally split: the field/enforcement arm (agents like Ryker, mostly true believers) and the Directorate/Acquisitions arm (which knows exactly what the org is). The two twists live in that gap.

**Nuradyne.** Runs Quinmore's power. Its inner circle knows the reactors siphon; the CU board and most of Nuradyne's own scientists don't. Ruthless, but internally rational: they believe they're keeping millions alive under glass and that the cost lands on people who don't exist to them. Led on the board by **Chair Vivienne Sendra**; the siphon program run by **Director Owen Harmon**; enforced by **Kira Voight**.

**Atlas Subsystems.** Dome shielding. Dependent on Nuradyne's power, resentful of the leverage, and privately convinced the reactor blast was a Nuradyne stunt. Atlas wants dirt, not justice. A useful, untrustworthy lever for the player.

**The Corporate Union board.** No civilian government; a board of corporate seats. Complicit by design: they approved the reactors as "cutting-edge energy tech" and made sure not to ask what "cutting-edge" meant. Their jurisdiction laws are what let Nuradyne cordon the slum district and screen every face leaving it.

**The slums / the Ashline syndicate.** The people the domes were sold to and then abandoned; the evacuees dumped in the relocation warehouse. **Vann Lorca** runs the Ashline crew — smuggling, multitools, movement through the sealed district that checkpoints can't stop. Not good people, but the only ones who owe Nuradyne nothing.

**The drained universe (U-3300) and its Envoy.** A version of Earth that *didn't* poison itself — and is dying anyway as Nuradyne bleeds its energy out. They traced the siphon and sent one operative through: **Sarai Vesh**. To the CU she's a terrorist. To the FMA's charts she's an unlicensed incursion (a criminal). She is, in fact, the only person in the story acting to stop a genocide.

## Key characters

- **Liam Ryker** — the player. Ex-military, low-affect, "ends justify the means / just business." His flexibility is the point: the game keeps offering him reasons to look away, and each ending is a different answer to *what he won't do for a paycheck and a chain of command.*
- **Corwin Halloran** — Ryker's FMA partner at the outpost; warm, funny, the one human Ryker trusts. Actually FMA Acquisitions, running the real operation. Not a cartoon: he believes the cartel keeps *his* universe's lights on, and he genuinely likes Ryker. His late orders include sanctioning Ryker if Ryker won't comply. The emotional spine of the FMA twist.
- **Controller Sela Okonkwo** — the by-the-book FMA officer who briefs Ryker in the prologue. A true believer in the enforcement arm; she doesn't know what Acquisitions knows. Useful late as a lever *inside* the FMA.
- **Director Owen Harmon** — head of Nuradyne's reactor project. Saw the abnormal readings before, understood they meant *someone on the other side*, and chose to suppress it and keep the domes lit. A utilitarian, not a sadist. His self-justification is the human-scale version of the FMA's.
- **Chair Vivienne Sendra** — Nuradyne's CU board seat; the architect of the secrecy. The clean, unbothered face of institutional atrocity.
- **Kira Voight** — Nuradyne Chief of Security. Ambiguous. She's hunting the operative and stonewalling Ryker, but she's the one who quietly kept the detained kid alive, and she has privately concluded her own leadership is monstrous. A possible ally, a possible final wall.
- **Silas Wray** — the stranded pre-war FMA liaison. Broke, terrified, brilliant, guilt-rotted. He gave Nuradyne the multiverse and got the slums for it. He knows more about the FMA's true nature than Ryker does, and he's the one who tells Ryker what "condemned universe" means.
- **Sarai Vesh** — the Envoy of U-3300. Force-field-shrouded, near-mute at first. A soldier and a mourner; her world's *light* is going out as it's drained. The story's moral mirror to both the slum mother and to Ryker himself.
- **Della Mott & Kip Mott** — the slum mother and her ~11-year-old son, detained in a Nuradyne cell because he was hiding in the sealed district and overheard a guard. Kip is the child who says the sentence that unlocks the whole horror.
- **Halden Cole** — Atlas regional director. Wants leverage on Nuradyne, offers Ryker access in exchange for dirt. Never an ally, always a lever.
- **Vann Lorca** — Ashline syndicate boss. Sells movement, multitools, and truth, in that order.
- **Dr. Marisol Ferro** — the dead Nuradyne scientist whose datacube starts the trail.

## Full plot, mission by mission

### Act I — The cover-up

**1. Prologue: FMA Headquarters.** Ryker is recalled from leave. A low-level clerk routes him to the briefing room, where **Controller Okonkwo** lays out the mission: a faint, unprecedented transversal anomaly from U-7134, operational codename **Quinmore**. Assess, contain, report. Covert insertion — no cover, no channels; the FMA lost its U-7134 contacts in that world's war and has been dark there for thirty years. Medical eval, armory, optional orientation (a returning team, flavor, a glimpse of FMA life). *Seeds, all playable via conversation and reading terminals:* the briefing language is "assess and **contain**," never "protect." The armory tech mutters that they don't usually field a *resonance sampler* "for a look-and-report" — it's a yield-assessment instrument, though Ryker (and the player) take it as a locator. Okonkwo, asked why the city already has an FMA **codename** if this is a fresh detection, doesn't have a clean answer. A wall plaque states the FMA's founding creed: *"Order across the crossing."* It will read very differently by the end.

**2. The outpost and the partner.** Ryker portals to the cloaked pre-war bunker in the poisoned wasteland and meets **Corwin Halloran**, his partner and controller for the op. Easy chemistry; Halloran will be the friendly voice in Ryker's ear the whole game. Sensors pick up residual multiversal energy; Halloran triangulates it to a reactor in Quinmore's slum district — Ryker's target. *Seed:* if Ryker asks, Halloran confirms the outpost has been *watching* U-7134 for thirty years — through the war, the chemical death of the sky, the domes, all of it — and never intervened. "Not our mandate. We watch, we log, we don't fix worlds." It's said kindly. It plants the question the whole game answers: *what is the FMA actually for?* Halloran stays behind for "independent research" and sends Ryker out via cloaked aircraft to **Northgate**.

**3. Northgate.** First taste of the world: sealed domes, corporate frontage, filtered air, a city that reads 1990s-industrial, not chrome-future. Thugs from the **Ashline** crew jump the obvious outsider — a test, not an execution (Lorca likes to know who's new). Player's first branch (stealth/talk/violence). Ryker learns the shape of things: the slum reactor blew, the sector's shielding is dead, the air got in, and Nuradyne has the district cordoned as a "terrorist crime scene," funneling evacuees through checkpoints. He takes the train downtown.

**4. Into the cordon.** At the slum-district entrance, **Della Mott** is arguing with a Nuradyne officer about her missing son; the officer waves her off, tells Ryker the district is sealed. Ryker infiltrates — forged Nuradyne credentials, a hacked checkpoint terminal, or social-engineering a harried guard (player's approach). Della catches him on the way in: *find my boy.* The evacuee warehouse looms in the background — overcrowded, corporate-run, a preview of what "we can keep you alive" actually buys.

**5. The reactor site.** Inside the sealed sector: dead and incapacitated Nuradyne security, scorched aperture housing, logs erased or corrupted. Nuradyne's on-site line is *terrorism, no idea who.* Searching thoroughly, in a vent by the body of **Dr. Marisol Ferro**, Ryker finds the datacube (canon): security doubled, comms cut, "reactor readings way outside normal operating parameters," reported to **Director Harmon**, told to forget it. Two leads: Harmon, and the abnormal readings. *Planted clue, playable:* Ryker's resonance sampler pings the aperture and returns a signature Halloran, over infolink, calls "occupied-source." Ryker asks what that means. Halloran goes briefly, uncharacteristically quiet — then says "later." (First crack in the partner.) Objective: find Harmon.

### Act II — The horror

**6. Finding Harmon.** Nuradyne HQ. Harmon is at his desk, business as usual — vanishing would look guilty. He stonewalls: rehearsed answers, dismisses the datacube, mourns "the tragedy," redirects to "the terrorist." What he avoids is louder than what he says. Reading an assistant's terminal (an email trips an infolink from Halloran) surfaces a suppressed internal reference to "prior resonance events — contained" and a routing tag for a *holding facility.* Security tightens; **Kira Voight** appears, sizes Ryker up, and makes clear she knows he isn't who his credentials say. She doesn't arrest him. (Seed: she's letting him run.)

**7. The missing kid.** The holding tag leads to a Nuradyne facility where **Kip Mott** is in an unlogged cell — grabbed for being found in the sealed district, forgotten. Rescue is a full branch (stealth extraction / talk past the desk / cut through security). Kip is scared but unhurt (Voight's quiet mercy — no one ordered him killed, which in Nuradyne terms is a decision). He tells Ryker what he overheard two guards say while Harmon's people argued down the hall — the sentence a child couldn't parse and Ryker can: *"Harmon says the ones on the other side finally stopped screaming."* Ryker banks it. Reuniting Kip with Della (or handing him to Lorca's people to move him out) pays off the canon thread and buys Ryker the slums' trust.

**8. The liaison.** Kip's line plus the "occupied-source" signature point the same way: *there are people on the other side of the reactor.* The slums remember an old man Nuradyne used to protect and then threw away, "the one who taught their science boys." Lorca sells the location. **Silas Wray** — broke, shaking, certain he's about to be killed — is the exposition floor dropping out. He tells Ryker everything the bible holds: the multiverse; that Nuradyne's reactors don't *generate* power, they *steal* it from other universes; that he, a stranded FMA liaison, traded that knowledge for a life under glass and got the slums instead; and that draining a universe *kills* it. And then the line that turns the game: Wray is more afraid of the **FMA** than of Nuradyne. Because Wray knows what the FMA actually is. He won't say it plainly yet — "you wouldn't believe me, and they're listening" — but he warns Ryker: *do not tell your handler you found me.* (Ryker, of course, can. Consequences follow.)

**9. The Envoy.** Voight's people are hunting the "bomber" — really the operative. Ryker races them and finds **Sarai Vesh**: force-field-shrouded, wounded, hunted, not a terrorist. Through broken conversation (and, if the player earns it, a lowered field), she reveals U-3300: an Earth that never poisoned its sky, whose *light itself* is failing as Nuradyne bleeds its energy across the crossing — crops dying under a dimming sun, oceans cooling, a slow freeze that reads, from inside, like the end of the world with no visible cause. Her people traced it, enlarged the aperture with one charge, and sent her through to end the source before it collapsed. She blew the slum reactor. She is trying to stop a genocide and every authority in the city has branded her the genocide's cause. *The gut-punch (Twist C, playable via her testimony + Wray + the sampler):* U-3300 is a **near-copy of U-7134's own Earth** — the version that made the other choice in the war and lived. Quinmore is, in the most literal sense, murdering the world it could have been. Branch: help her, capture her for the FMA (Halloran is very interested), or kill her.

**10. The corporate war.** **Halden Cole** of **Atlas** makes contact: he thinks the blast was a Nuradyne power play and wants proof to take to the CU board. He offers Ryker access — Atlas maintenance conduits into Nuradyne's core, a board-level credential — in exchange for dirt. Ryker can play the corps against each other, using Atlas's access to reach Nuradyne's inner core and Harmon's real files, and using Nuradyne's paranoia to keep Voight off his back. The sequence exposes the CU board's willful blindness and Chair **Sendra**'s machinery of suppression: the reactors were sold to the board as "energy tech," the abnormal-reading reports were killed at Sendra's level, and Atlas's shielding failure was, by the letter of the contracts, Nuradyne's fault — which is the only crime Cole actually cares about.

**11. Harmon, for real.** With Atlas's access or a hard infiltration, Ryker corners the real Harmon in the reactor control core beneath Nuradyne HQ. No script this time. Harmon knew. He'd seen the resonance spikes years ago, understood they meant something *inhabited* on the other side, and chose the domes — *"I have four million people breathing because those reactors run. You want me to let them suffocate for a universe I can't see, can't verify, can't save? I made the arithmetic no one else would."* He's not lying and he's not a monster in the cartoon sense, and that's the point: he's Ryker's own "ends justify the means" said out loud, scaled to a city. He points up the chain to Sendra and the board — and, if pressed on where Nuradyne *learned* all this, lets slip that it wasn't invented here. It was **given**. By a man who came from "an authority that polices this sort of thing." The FMA. First time Ryker hears his own agency named by the enemy.

**12. The FMA turn.** Ryker reports up: the leak (Wray), the tech, the operative, the horror. And Halloran's infolink objectives *change shape.* No longer "assess and report." Now: **secure** the reactor apparatus intact; **identify everyone** in U-7134 who knows the FMA exists; **prepare assets for extraction.** The resonance sampler's readings, Halloran now admits, were never about locating the anomaly — they were grading the *yield*. Ryker, suspicious, does the thing the game has trained him to do: he reads a computer. Returning to the outpost (or breaching Halloran's relay remotely), an FMA Directorate directive — triggered as a readable file, kicking an infolink — spells it out. **Twist A lands here:** the FMA does not intend to shut the siphon down. The FMA's member-worlds *run on the same technology.* Extraction is the foundation of the federation; "policing the multiverse" is *licensing* it. Nuradyne's crime is not the genocide — it's doing it **unlicensed**, crudely, on a target the FMA's own charts hadn't cleared, and *leaking the secret to outsiders.* The directive's disposition list is the tell: the reactor tech — **acquire.** Wray — **acquire or terminate.** Sarai Vesh — she isn't a victim to the FMA, she's an *unauthorized incursion*, a criminal to be neutralized. Della, Kip, the slum witnesses — **loose ends.** And U-3300, the dying near-copy of Earth, is stamped on the FMA's map with a single word: **condemned.** The FMA's charts lie by definition — "condemned" doesn't mean empty, it means *cleared for draining.*

**13. Halloran.** The confrontation that carries the whole game's weight — playable as one long conversation, with the option to walk in shooting. Halloran doesn't deny it. He's tired, not triumphant. *"You think I don't know what we are? My daughter's dome runs on it, Liam. Some universe I'll never see is going dark so she can read at night. That's the deal. That's every deal. Nuradyne just did it without paying the license and without the good manners to hide it."* He offers Ryker the clean path: bring in the tech and the liaison, let the operative be "neutralized," let the FMA absorb Nuradyne's method under sanction, and go home promoted with the city's lights still on. Just business. Then the last card, the one that makes it a real choice and not a lecture: the Directorate's directive lists a contingency for the field agent's non-compliance. If Ryker won't close the loose ends, Halloran is the one ordered to close **Ryker.** He hasn't decided whether he will. **That's Twist B** — the partner was never support; he was the operation, and Ryker was always a disposable instrument of it — but it's played as a man asking his friend not to make him choose either.

### Act III — Convergence

Everyone wants the reactor core beneath Nuradyne HQ (twin to the slum aperture, the master apparatus): **Vesh** to destroy it; **Sendra/Voight** to protect it; **Cole/Atlas** to seize it as leverage; **Halloran/the FMA** to acquire it intact; **Wray** holds the knowledge that makes it usable or unmakeable; **Della and Kip** are the human stakes (and, per the FMA directive, marked for cleanup). Ryker's Act II choices set the board — who trusts him, who's alive, whether Voight is a wall or an ally, whether Vesh is at his side or in an FMA cell. The finale resolves at the core, and the choice is ideological, not tactical.

## The central twists (explicit)

**Twist A — the FMA's hidden agenda (primary).** "Policing inter-universal crime" is monopoly enforcement. The FMA's founding worlds are themselves powered by siphoning "condemned" universes under license; the whole legal-moral apparatus exists to keep the technology — and the atrocity — in approved hands. Ryker wasn't sent to stop the horror. He was sent to *assess an unlicensed competitor, acquire its method, and contain the leak.* The operative he was told was a terrorist is, by the FMA's own law, merely *unlicensed*; by any other standard she's the only just actor in the story. Planted from the prologue: "contain," the pre-existing codename, the yield-sampler, thirty years of watching-not-helping, Halloran's "not our mandate," and Wray's terror of the FMA over Nuradyne. Delivered via readable Directorate directive (triggering infolink) + the Halloran confrontation — all inside the player verbs.

**Twist B — the partner was the operation.** Halloran isn't Ryker's support; he's FMA Acquisitions running the real op, and Ryker is the disposable field instrument. His final orders include sanctioning Ryker for non-compliance. But he's a conflicted man, not a reveal-villain — which is what makes the betrayal land and makes turning him a genuine, earnable possibility rather than a plot gift.

**Twist C — the drained world is the road not taken.** U-3300, the universe Nuradyne is killing and the operative is dying to save, is a near-copy of U-7134's own Earth: the version that didn't poison its sky. Quinmore survives under glass by murdering the world it could have been — and the FMA's map calls that world "condemned" to license the draining. The multiverse-siphon horror made personal and inescapable.

(Supporting reveal: **Voight's mercy** — she spared the kid and has privately turned on her own leadership — recontextualizes the "enforcer" into an ambiguous figure the player can recruit, seeded by the unlogged, un-executed cell.)

## Player-choice structure & endings

Choices accrete through Act II — who lives (Vesh, Wray, Voight, Della/Kip), whom Ryker trusts (Halloran, Cole, Lorca), and what he reads and reports. At the core, four ideological endings, each requiring different alliances and each honestly costed. All reachable within the verbs: conversations, infolink, reading the directive, multitool access, killing/incapacitating.

**1. The License (Compliance — "just business").** Ryker takes Halloran's deal. He secures the apparatus, delivers Wray, lets Vesh be neutralized, and the loose ends are closed. The FMA absorbs Nuradyne's method under sanction; Quinmore's domes stay lit; U-3300 and the other drained worlds keep dying, now *regulated.* Ryker goes home promoted, Halloran relieved and a little smaller. Order across the crossing, maintained. The coldest ending: Ryker becomes the cartel's hand and calls it professionalism. *The game does not flinch — the lights stay on and that is the horror.*

**2. The Broadcast (Exposure — reformist).** Ryker refuses containment and does the opposite: using the outpost's transmitter, Wray's knowledge, and the reactor data, he broadcasts the truth *across the crossing* — the siphon, the drained worlds, and the FMA's complicity — to member-world populations and the CU board at once. The monopoly's moral cover shatters. Both Nuradyne and the FMA's legitimacy crater; a reformist faction inside the FMA (Okonkwo's arm, the true believers who never knew) has something to seize. But exposure isn't rescue: Quinmore's siphon power is now a scandal to be shut down, and the domes may go dark before any clean replacement exists. Ryker trades stability for a chance at truth, and can't promise the truth wins. Requires Wray alive and Halloran either turned or dead.

**3. The Collapse (Justice — the Envoy's ending).** Ryker sides with the victims and helps **Vesh** finish what she came to do: destroy the master apparatus and the aperture permanently, ending the siphon everywhere it reaches from U-7134. Countless unseen universes stop dying. But Quinmore runs on that theft — the domes lose power, the seals fail, the poisoned sky comes in. A genuine trolley problem with no trick exit: the seen city for the unseen worlds. The game lets Ryker try to soften it — evacuate through Lorca's routes, force Atlas to stand up emergency shielding, buy the domes hours instead of minutes — but the arithmetic Harmon named is now Ryker's to own. Vesh, if she lives, goes home to a world that might, slowly, get its light back. The most idealistic and the most brutal ending in the same act.

**4. The Broker (Power — the third way).** Ryker cuts out both his employer and the corp. With Wray's knowledge, Voight's guns, and the apparatus intact, he seizes the siphon himself — not to use it, but to *hold* it: leverage over the FMA and Nuradyne both, a stranded man's road home for Wray, a seat no one elected him to. He forces terms — a moratorium, a real accounting, maybe a slow wind-down on his timeline — at the price of becoming exactly the kind of unaccountable power the FMA pretends to be. The pragmatic-tyrant ending: Ryker keeps the lights on *and* stops the worst of the draining, and the only guarantor of that mercy is one morally-flexible man with his finger on the aperture. The game leaves open whether that holds — or whether Halloran was right that this is just how every deal starts.

## Themes

- **Legality is not morality; it's whoever writes the license.** The FMA turns "crime" into a synonym for "competition." The story asks what's left of justice when the biggest offender is also the judge.
- **The arithmetic of the unseen.** Harmon, the CU board, Halloran, and the FMA all run the same equation: my visible people are worth more than your invisible ones. Ryker is handed that equation at every scale and made to sign it or tear it up.
- **Complicity is comfort.** No one in power is a sadist. The sky stays clean and the child reads at night *because* a world you'll never see goes dark. The horror is that it's livable.
- **The road not taken.** U-3300 is the mirror: the same Earth, the better choice, murdered by the survivors of the worse one. Survival bought by killing your own better self.
- **What "policing" is for.** The FMA's creed — *order across the crossing* — is true. The game's whole argument is that order and justice were never the same word, and Ryker's endings are four answers to which one he'll enforce.
