# DX_LUM — The Umber Accord

Writer: sonnet | Emphasis: the Nuradyne/Atlas corporate war & the slums

## Logline

When a Nuradyne reactor blows and floods a slum sector with poison air, undercover investigator Liam
Ryker digs past the official "terrorist attack" story and finds two corporations who both bet the
lives of the poor to win a boardroom fight — and an employer of his own willing to burn the whole
city to bury the evidence.

## What I take as fixed vs. what I invented

**Fixed, unchanged:** Ryker as blank-slate operative; the FMA and its cross-universe policing
mandate; U-7134/Quinmore; the Corporate Union board-of-corps government; Nuradyne's reactors as
secret multiversal energy-siphons; Atlas as the dome-shielding corp, mutually dependent on and
distrustful of Nuradyne; the operative sent through from a drained universe; the stranded FMA
liaison who sold Nuradyne the multiversal knowledge that built the siphons; Director Harmon
(suppressed prior "abnormal reading" reports); Kira Voight as Nuradyne's Chief of Security; the
slum mother and her detained son; the relocation warehouse; the Northgate arrival, the train
downtown, the checkpoint, the vent datacube, Aire Gardens and its elevator to the subway; the
player-verb constraints (no tape review, no timestamp-hacking — reveals come from conversation,
infolink, terminals, and what Ryker does with his hands).

**Invented (mine to develop):** the missing kid gets a name (Toby Delgado) and his mother a face
(Rosa Delgado); a named Atlas field investigator (Dana Reyes) who becomes Ryker's in-again-off-again
corporate ally; a syndicate boss who runs the slum's black market (Dessa Kolvig, "the Broker"); a
corrupt relocation-warehouse administrator (Colm Pruitt); a CU Board compliance officer trying to
referee the Nuradyne/Atlas fight for her own reasons (Priya Anand); the specific mechanism of the
corporate cover-up (Atlas quietly under-funded slum-sector backup shielding as a boardroom gambit);
the liaison's name (Mercer Voss) and his fate; the operative's fragmentary voice and what it wants;
and the FMA's actual doctrine, "Cauterize" — the hidden agenda twist.

## Factions & power structure

**Nuradyne.** Controls Quinmore's power grid — leverage over every other corp on the CU board.
Publicly a terrorism victim; privately racing to contain a reactor design flaw its own inner circle
doesn't fully understand. Chief of Security Kira Voight runs the cordon and the checkpoint hunt for
"the bomber." Director Harmon runs the reactor program and has buried two smaller anomalies before
this one. Below them, a corporate PR cell (fixer Sable Kade) is already writing the story the public
will believe.

**Atlas Subsystems.** Builds and maintains dome shielding. Needs Nuradyne's power; resents needing
it. Publicly blames Nuradyne's reactor security. Privately, Atlas leadership — specifically VP
Corwin Ashe — has been playing a longer game: quietly starving slum-sector backup shielding of
maintenance budget for two years, betting that if Nuradyne's reactor ever had a bad day, the
failure would land on Nuradyne's books, not Atlas's, and Atlas would walk away with a bigger cut of
infrastructure contracts board-wide. Field investigator Dana Reyes was sent to gather evidence of
Nuradyne's negligence and has no idea her own company sabotaged the outcome.

**The Corporate Union board.** No civilian government, just corp reps trading favors and market
share. A blast this size threatens to shift seats. Compliance officer Priya Anand is nominally
neutral — her real employer, third-place transit corp Meridian Transit, benefits any time the two
biggest players bleed each other, and she's quietly feeding Ryker's findings to whichever side
currently helps Meridian most.

**The slums and the Hollow Market.** Quinmore's poorest sector, now half-flooded with toxic air and
sealed. Dessa Kolvig's syndicate — the Hollow Market — runs smuggled filters, medicine, forged
IDs, and a slow, expensive underground route out of cordoned districts. She despises both
corporations and profits off their war regardless of who wins. Some of her people are opportunists;
some are the only functioning emergency service the slum has ever had.

**The relocation warehouse.** A converted transit depot, thousands packed in, run by Nuradyne
appointee Colm Pruitt, who skims relief supplies and sells "priority processing" through a deal with
Kolvig's people. Rosa Delgado is here, still fighting to be heard about her missing son.

**The drained universe.** Sent one operative through a collapsing aperture — a force-shielded
soldier acting under orders to find and stop the source of the drain, sympathetic to a player who
gets close enough to hear it out, lethal to anyone who doesn't.

**The FMA.** Ryker's employer, publicly a peacekeeping body. Its actual standing doctrine for a
civilization that stumbles onto siphon technology is not "arrest and extradite" — it's
containment by any means, up to and including erasing the technology's home entirely, because a
working siphon reactor is a weapon any FMA-hostile universe would kill to copy.

## Key characters

- **Liam Ryker** — as established: low-affect ex-military operative, morally flexible, learns
  Quinmore as the player does.
- **Selin Marsh** — Ryker's FMA partner, holds the outpost, feeds him objectives via infolink.
  Loyal to Ryker personally; increasingly uneasy about what HQ is asking her to relay.
- **Director Aldous Harmon** — Nuradyne's reactor chief. Not a mustache-twirler: a company man who
  told himself the first two anomalies were manageable and has spent two years being right, until
  now. Genuinely doesn't know the siphon's full destructive scale — Nuradyne's real inner circle
  kept even him partly in the dark.
- **Kira Voight** — Nuradyne Chief of Security. Competent, controlled, running the cordon on orders
  she believes are about a bomber. She is not read into the siphon secret. When she learns the
  truth mid-game, how she reacts is one of the game's turning points.
- **Toby Delgado** — the missing kid, ten years old, found in a Nuradyne holding cell. Overheard two
  guards talking about "the old man" and "wiping the vent logs on sub-level three."
- **Rosa Delgado** — his mother, first met arguing at the checkpoint, then found again in the
  relocation warehouse if Ryker doesn't get her out. Carries the human stakes of every corporate
  decision Ryker uncovers.
- **Mercer Voss** — the stranded FMA liaison. Old, ruined, living under a false name in the Hollow
  Market on filtered-air black-market credit. Traded the FMA's knowledge to Nuradyne decades ago in
  exchange for protection; discarded once Nuradyne's engineers no longer needed him.
- **Dana Reyes** — Atlas field investigator. Believes she's uncovering Nuradyne's negligence in
  good faith. Becomes Ryker's most useful ally in the corporate half of the plot — until she finds
  out what her own company did.
- **Dessa Kolvig ("the Broker")** — runs the Hollow Market. Neither friend nor enemy; sells access,
  intel, and exit routes to whoever pays, including Ryker.
- **Colm Pruitt** — relocation-warehouse administrator, quietly rich off relief-supply skimming and
  a "priority processing" racket run with Kolvig's people.
- **Priya Anand** — CU Board compliance officer, ostensibly neutral, actually working an angle for
  Meridian Transit.
- **The operative ("the Glassman," slum nickname)** — force-shielded soldier from a drained
  universe. Not a monster: a soldier sent to stop a war crime it can't fully explain to anyone who'd
  believe it.
- **Sable Kade** — Nuradyne's PR handler, shaping the "terrorist attack" narrative for the public
  and the board.
- **VP Corwin Ashe** — the Atlas executive who ran the under-funding gambit. Never physically
  confronts Ryker; his fingerprints are all over the paper trail Reyes and Ryker dig up.

## Full plot, mission by mission

### Act 1 — What actually happened

**M1 — FMA HQ.** As established: recall, briefing, medical, armory, optional orientation. Ryker's
handler frames this as a low-confidence, moderate-risk signal — "erratic, unprecedented, could be
nothing." Marsh is assigned as his outpost partner. Portal to the cloaked U-7134 outpost.

**M2 — Triangulation, Northgate, the train.** Marsh triangulates the residual signal to a reactor in
Quinmore's slum district. Cloaked aircraft to Northgate; Ryker's jumped by local thugs working
Northgate's transit corridor — an early, deniable taste of how little the CU's writ means once you're
off the corporate campuses. Train downtown.

**M3 — The checkpoint.** Slum district entrance, cordoned. Rosa Delgado is arguing with a Nuradyne
officer about Toby. The officer redirects to Ryker, refuses him — Ryker forges/talks/hacks his way
through (player choice of approach). Rosa stops him on the way in, asks him to find her son. This is
the emotional anchor the player carries for the rest of the game.

**M4 — The reactor site.** Erased logs, dead or incapacitated security, Nuradyne staff publicly
"stumped." Ryker finds the vent datacube from the dead scientist (established text, unchanged) —
names Harmon and "abnormal reactor readings." Two leads: Harmon, and the readings themselves.

**M5 — Nuradyne HQ, finding Harmon.** Ryker infiltrates Nuradyne HQ (basement gun range/armory/
labs/forensics, upper-floor executive suites — per `Locations.md`). Harmon stonewalls, feeds
rehearsed lines, dismisses the datacube as "instrument drift." What he won't say is louder than what
he does: he refuses to authorize an independent readout of the reactor's core logs, citing "national
security," a phrase that means nothing under a corporate board and everyone in the room knows it.

**M6 — The holding cell.** Deeper in the same Nuradyne facility (forensics wing, or a security
sub-level), Ryker finds Toby Delgado, unlogged, in a holding cell. Freeing him is a straightforward
incapacitate-the-guards-or-badge-through beat. Toby doesn't understand what he heard, but repeats it
verbatim: two guards, before the blast made the news public, talking about "wiping the vent logs on
sub-level three" and about someone called "the old man" who "still doesn't know how much he's worth
to us." Ryker now has a second unexplained figure to chase, independent of the terrorism story.

Returning Toby is a branch point: Ryker can personally walk him back to the checkpoint and reunite
him with Rosa now (safe, but she and Toby end up in the relocation warehouse, exposed to Pruitt's
racket for the rest of the game), or tell Toby to stay hidden with a Hollow Market contact Ryker
hasn't met yet (riskier up front, safer later — this thread pays off in Act 3).

### Act 2 — Two corporations, one war

**M7 — Sub-level three.** Following Toby's lead, Ryker breaches Nuradyne's sub-level three — heavier
security than the public floors, consistent with hiding something more than a PR problem. A
terminal read triggers an infolink message from Marsh (the established "email triggers ally
infolink" mechanic): she's cross-referenced the vent-log wipe with two earlier, smaller "reactor
incident" entries Nuradyne's own insurance filings tried to bury, both during Harmon's tenure. Marsh's
new objective: find out what "contained" actually looked like the first two times.

**M8 — Atlas makes contact.** Dana Reyes intercepts Ryker (posing as an insurance auditor, or
approaching him directly if he's been visibly asking Nuradyne people about the reactor). She's
building a negligence case against Nuradyne on Atlas's behalf and offers a trade: access to Atlas's
own post-blast diagnostics (which Nuradyne has refused Atlas permission to run on-site) in exchange
for whatever Ryker digs out of Nuradyne's side. This opens the corporate-war track in earnest —
Ryker can play double agent for both sides at once, and the player can choose how much to actually
hand over to either.

**M9 — The Hollow Market.** Ryker goes looking for anyone in the slums who saw something the night
of the blast. Enters Dessa Kolvig's territory — first contact is transactional: she'll sell rumors,
not give them away. Two threads open here: (a) sightings of a figure that moves wrong, doesn't
breathe, and killed two of Kolvig's own runners who got too close — the operative, unnamed by anyone
who's seen it, called "the Glassman"; (b) a name, "Voss," attached to a reclusive old man who pays
for filtered air with information nobody else seems to want, and who's been unusually terrified since
the blast.

**M10 — The dome-shielding backup.** Reyes gets Ryker into the blast-site shielding relay Atlas has
been denied access to. On an Atlas terminal (legitimately hers to use, or bypassed with a multitool
if she's stalled by her own chain of command), Ryker finds the diagnostic Reyes wanted — and one more
thing she didn't: a maintenance-budget memo trail showing the slum sector's backup shielding was
flagged for replacement two years ago and repeatedly deprioritized, signed off by VP Corwin Ashe, with
a margin note tying the deferred spend to "sector risk profile — acceptable." Reading it triggers an
infolink from Marsh confirming the timeline lines up: if the backup had been at spec, the sector
might have held air long enough for a controlled evacuation instead of a flood.

This is the corporate-war reveal: Nuradyne's reactor caused the trigger; Atlas's own cost-cutting
made the trigger catastrophic instead of survivable. Reyes, shown the memo, is shaken — her
employer gambled with slum lives to win a fight with Nuradyne, and she's spent weeks building the
case that would have let Atlas do it again with a clean conscience. How Ryker handles this — hand it
to Reyes straight, hand it to Nuradyne's Voight to burn Atlas at the board, sell it to Kolvig, or sit
on it — is a major branch that shapes which corp treats Ryker as an asset later.

**M11 — Voss, found.** Following the "Voss" thread into the Hollow Market's deep blocks, Ryker finds
Mercer Voss squatting behind a false wall, addicted to filtered-air credit, terrified of "them" —
he means Nuradyne, but he's also increasingly convinced "the old world" (his word for the FMA) has
finally noticed him. Conversation, not tape review or hacking, is how this lands: Voss talks in
fragments unless pushed. Pushed hard enough (or shown FMA-issue gear, which visibly wrecks him), he
confirms it outright: decades ago he traded the FMA's multiversal knowledge to Nuradyne for
protection; that knowledge built the siphon reactors; Nuradyne cut him loose once their own engineers
didn't need him anymore. He begs Ryker not to report finding him — not out of loyalty to the FMA, but
because he's certain that whichever side finds him first, corporate or FMA, kills him this time.

**M12 — Voight.** With the sub-level-three lead, the memo, and Voss's confirmation stacking up, Ryker
can now approach Kira Voight directly instead of Harmon. Voight is not read into the siphon secret —
she genuinely believes she's hunting a saboteur. Shown enough evidence (the buried prior incidents,
the vent-log wipe under her own command structure, Harmon's stonewalling), she has to decide whether
to keep running Harmon's cover story or turn on her own director. This is playable multiple ways:
recruit her as an ally against Harmon, let her stay loyal and become a harder obstacle later, or
provoke a confrontation that removes her from the board entirely.

**M13 — The relocation warehouse.** Whether or not Toby went back to Rosa in M6, this mission
converges on the warehouse: overcrowded, filthy, Pruitt running his priority-processing racket
through Kolvig's people, relief medicine diverted and resold. If Rosa and Toby are here, Nuradyne
security shows up looking for "the boy from the holding cell" — Toby is now a loose end who can place
guards at a scene Nuradyne wants erased, and Ryker has to get the family out before Pruitt hands them
over for a fee. This is the mission built to make the corporate war's cost concrete rather than
abstract: everything Ryker has been reading on terminals, a ten-year-old is now living through.

**M14 — Aire Gardens and the subway.** The luxury sector — copied train-station geometry, elevator
down to the subway level (per `Ideas.md`) — becomes the site where all three hunts collide: Nuradyne
security, an Atlas covert team sent by Ashe to grab hard evidence (or the operative itself) before
Reyes's honest investigation can surface it, and Ryker, all converging on the same subway tunnels
where Voss says the Glassman has been sighted. This is the mission built for playing the corps
against each other: a badge, a forged comm burst, or a well-timed tip can point one security team at
the other and let Ryker slip past both, or Ryker can pick a side and fight alongside it.

**M15 — The Glassman speaks.** Cornered rather than killed (a stealth/non-lethal path the game should
reward, consistent with the established verbs — incapacitate, not necessarily execute), the operative
can be reasoned with in a short, strange exchange: it doesn't hate Ryker or Quinmore specifically. It
was sent because its home is dying, drained by a reactor grid it can't reach any other way, and it
followed the only aperture wide enough to fit through before the blast that opened it collapsed. It
has no way home either. This is the emotional gut-punch that reframes the "terrorist" from Act 1 as
another casualty of Nuradyne's secret, same as the slum sector, just from the other side of the drain.

### Act 3 — Whose war is it

**M16 — Marsh's warning.** With the siphon confirmed, Voss found, and the operative's story known,
Ryker reports in. Marsh relays FMA Command's response — and it's wrong in a way she can't quite
justify to herself: not "arrest Nuradyne's leadership," not "shut the reactor down," but a directive
to recover Voss and any Nuradyne documentation referencing the FMA or the siphon method, "population
impact not a mission parameter." Marsh, reading further into the tasking than she's supposed to,
finds a standing contingency doctrine buried in the outpost's archive: codename **Cauterize** — if a
civilization independently reconstructs siphon technology and containment can't be guaranteed clean,
the FMA is authorized to trigger a total grid collapse across every reactor built on the stolen
knowledge, city-wide, accepting mass civilian death as a bounded cost against the technology
proliferating further across the multiverse. She sends Ryker the file via infolink rather than
sitting on it — the game's central FMA-hidden-agenda twist, delivered exactly the way the brief's verb
constraints intend: a one-way message from an ally, not a hacked log or a review of tape.

**M17 — The board convenes.** Priya Anand calls Ryker in — she's read the fallout of whatever he's
leaked to Reyes, Voight, or Kolvig by this point and wants to know what he actually has, because a CU
board inquest is now scheduled and whoever controls the narrative going in controls which corp
survives it with its seats intact. This is the mission where every earlier choice (who Ryker gave the
Atlas memo to, whether Voight turned on Harmon, whether Reyes trusts him) determines what evidence is
actually in the room, and it's the last point where Ryker can still shape the corporate outcome before
the FMA's own plan comes due.

**M18 — Cauterize, or not.** Endgame mission set: Ryker has to act before Command executes Cauterize
on its own timeline (Marsh can stall, but not indefinitely — another verb-appropriate infolink
countdown, not a hacked terminal). Where this lands depends on the ending chosen below.

## The central twists

1. **The FMA's hidden agenda ("Cauterize").** The FMA was never sent to stop a bomber or even
   principally to punish Nuradyne — its actual doctrine treats any civilization that independently
   builds working siphon tech as a proliferation risk to be contained "by any means," up to a
   full grid-collapse of every reactor built on Voss's stolen knowledge, accepting Quinmore's dome
   population as acceptable collateral. Delivered via Marsh's infolink message, not a hack or a tape
   — she reads the doctrine and chooses to warn Ryker rather than let him walk into it blind.
2. **The corporate cover-up.** Nuradyne's reactor caused the blast; Atlas's own leadership (VP Corwin
   Ashe) made it a mass-casualty event on purpose, by quietly under-funding slum-sector backup
   shielding for two years as a bet against Nuradyne in the boardroom. Both corporations gambled the
   slum's air on their rivalry, and both are still lying about it in public. Delivered by reading an
   Atlas terminal Ryker's ally Reyes gets him into — a document reveal the player achieves by reading
   a computer, exactly within the established verb set.
3. **A minor third turn, playable and optional:** the operative isn't the villain of the terrorism
   story Nuradyne sold — it's a soldier from a universe Nuradyne's own reactor is killing, given voice
   only if the player chooses the non-lethal path to reach it.

## Player-choice structure & endings

Throughout, Ryker's leverage — what he's told Reyes, Voight, Kolvig, Anand, and Marsh, and whether
Toby and Rosa made it out of the warehouse safely — determines which of these are actually reachable
and how much they cost:

- **Cauterize the truth (FMA loyalist).** Ryker helps Marsh feed Command exactly what it needs to
  execute a *narrow* strike — sabotaging only the slum-district reactor cluster and Nuradyne's core
  archive, not the whole grid — trading his standing with the FMA to talk Command down from full
  Cauterize. Quinmore's power grid takes a serious hit and thousands face a hard winter of rolling
  dome failures, but the technology dies, Voss is extracted (or quietly killed by Ryker himself, "just
  business," if the player chooses not to risk him being found alive), and Ryker goes home believing
  he did the necessary ugly thing. Most classically "ends justify the means."
- **Burn the corps, save the doctrine question for later (whistleblower).** Ryker routes the Atlas
  memo, the buried-incident record, and Voight's testimony to Priya Anand and the board inquest
  simultaneously, forcing a public reckoning that strips both Nuradyne and Atlas of board seats and
  puts the reactors under enforced civilian — well, CU-appointed — oversight. It doesn't fix the
  Corporate Union, but it ends the specific lie. Ryker has to actively defy or outrun Cauterize to
  make this stick, possibly burning his relationship with the FMA entirely; Marsh's help becomes the
  price of the ending, since she has to falsify the outpost's report to keep Command from moving in
  before the board acts.
- **Torch it all (radical/street ending).** Ryker sides with Dessa Kolvig: leaks everything —
  Nuradyne's siphon, Atlas's sabotage, and the FMA's existence itself — into the Hollow Market's
  distribution network, betting that public chaos is safer for ordinary Quinmore residents than any
  version of corporate or FMA "containment." This is the ending most likely to get Rosa and Toby
  killed if they weren't already safe, and it ends with Ryker stranded in Quinmore himself — the FMA
  cuts him loose the way Nuradyne cut Voss loose, for exactly the same reason: too much paperwork, and
  nobody would believe him either.
- **Walk the tightrope (pragmatist/save-what-you-can).** If Ryker built enough trust with Voight
  specifically, he can hand her — not Anand, not the FMA — enough evidence to let her force Harmon out
  and take over the reactor program herself, betting a career security officer who now knows exactly
  what she's protecting people from will run it safer than the men who lied to her. Cauterize is
  averted by convincing Command that Nuradyne's siphon capability is genuinely, verifiably being
  dismantled under new management rather than destroyed outright — the closest thing to a stable
  ending, and the one that leaves the most structural rot (the CU board, the checkpoint regime, the
  Hollow Market's leverage over the poor) completely untouched.

## Themes

Two corporations can each be individually rational and still produce a catastrophe neither intended
and both will lie about forever — Nuradyne didn't mean to blow the reactor, Atlas didn't mean to kill
that many people, and both would make the same bets again given the chance, because the people who
pay for the bet were never in the room where it was made. The FMA, framed to the player as the
adults in the room policing a dangerous multiverse, turns out to run on the same arithmetic as
every corporation in Quinmore: an acceptable number of strangers is not a number zero. And the people
actually living in the blast radius — Rosa, Toby, Voss, even the operative — never get a seat at any
of these tables; the entire back half of the game is Ryker deciding whether he's an instrument of the
tables or the first person in the story willing to hand the people underneath them a chair.
